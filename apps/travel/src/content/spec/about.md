## Why This Site Exists

This is my working trail log for the Pacific Crest Trail 2026 season — Campo to Canada, 2,650 miles.

I built it as a place to keep the useful parts of the hike in one place: preparation notes, gear decisions, food strategy, field-tested fixes, and the adjustments that happen once the plan meets real miles. Not travel marketing. A working document.

## What You Will Find Here

- Pacific Crest Trail planning notes and on-trail field reports
- Gear breakdowns, pack-weight tradeoffs, and resupply strategy
- Foot care, injury prevention, and practical lessons worth keeping
- Food planning, calorie math, and town logistics
- Photos and observations from the trail

## About Greg

I am Greg Aster — experimental videographer, builder, chronic over-planner, and PCT Class of 2026.

My background is in mechanical work, hands-on problem solving, independent filmmaking, and building things that hold up under real use. I approach hiking the same way: test the system, document what breaks, refine the setup, keep moving.

I started the PCT on March 10, 2026 at the Southern Terminus in Campo, California. I carry a Gregory Baltoro 75L, use trail runners, and started with a base weight around 29–30 lbs (full pack closer to 44–55 lbs with food and water). I hike heavy by thru-hiking standards and I know it.

Some details worth knowing upfront:

- I have surgical hardware in my fibula from a previous fracture. My ankle has documented instability. Foot care is not optional for me — it is mission-critical.
- I have an exertional heart arrhythmia. I manage pace, hydration, and heat load carefully.
- I sweat heavily and take hydration seriously from the start.

These constraints shaped most of my gear choices, training approach, and the content you will find here.

## How I Use This Journal

Expect direct writing, practical details, and updates that change as conditions change. I revise the plan instead of pretending the first version was correct.

The goal is accuracy, not polish. If something worked, I say so. If it failed, I say that too.

## Outside the Trail

This site is the stripped-down version: less lore, more useful trail information.

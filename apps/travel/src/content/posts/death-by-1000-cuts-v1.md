I'm 43, 215 lbs. Prior ankle surgery, knee problems, heart arrhythmia, and a bone infection in my jaw. This really isn't an award-winning recipe for a thru-hiker. Sprinkle crushing poverty into the mix and it becomes downright toxic.

I really don't expect to last days or weeks.

Still...I have nowhere to be. The environment I am in has taken little bits of my soul year after year until I wonder if there is anything left. Balancing potential dangers and failure against nothing to lose is like dividing by zero.

## How I Got Here

I've always wanted to make things -- books, music, art. With every ounce of my being.

So naturally I became a car mechanic.

23 years I had fixed tens of thousands of cars, educated and supported countless others. I have been a rock thrown in a pond, the ripples of my labor traveling outward affecting underserved communities and helping the world run.

I'm not sure if it's nature or nurture, but people are always looking at each other, sizing each other up, determining if you're part of their tribe, their social class, whatever. If someone challenges the common assumptions, those around them will unconsciously push them in a different direction. Constant invisible forces determine your path in life. Who says hello to you on the street. Who hires you for a job. Who is willing to pay for what you do. I've been good at what I do, but I never wanted this really.

## The Art Problem

Art was always something I've done in the evenings. Something people around me determined was a weird quirk. At times it was successful -- but eventually the mild success faded. Friends have always been unsupportive and derisive. I find myself sitting on a pile of ash, my previous successes burned, surrounded by people who do not appreciate what I want to do, and who I want to be -- they want the reflection.

My circumstance is not uncommon. In fact it's extremely common. But it is not popular. Regardless, I just want to make, express, love the things I love, and be myself. It would be so much easier if it were otherwise -- if it were a popular decision, if you had people around you to support your journey. Such is the nature of swimming against the currents.

## The End of the Road

Now the sun is setting. My buisness is gone, I have no money, my audience is gone and nothing left to give. People around me continue to be unsupportive. I've applied for jobs, tried to collaborate with others, or offered my services for free -- none of it goes anywhere.

With no money coming in, I started cutting expenses living on less and less until I was down to a dollar a day. Still working sixty hours a week, I haven't received a real paycheck in years -- I've began asking myself -- why am I still doing this? I could just not.

Its simple math. If a worker doesn't get paid, eventually he stops showing up to the job. Some people are sorry to see me go, but ultimately they're just sorry I'm not working for them anymore. On the PCT, I can be alone and not have to worry about the burden of people asking me to do things. I don't have to worry about the income disparity between myself and others. I don't need to worry about the social pressure of being unemployed. The silent rejection when trying to be myself.

When I told people I was thinking about the trip, no one was supportive. Some were simply negative, others incredulous. My partner's response wasn't understanding.
I actually understand this. People don't owe me anything. The world is what it is. But it has pushed me further toward the door. Every micro aggression, every conversation that turned back toward what my leaving would cost someone else -- it all just confirmed what I already suspected.
It is sad, though. I have an understanding born from decades of confirmation. The lesson learned I wish I knew decades ago -- I will never fit in, I will never have acceptance. I will never have money. To keep trying for these things is folly.

I have no reason for staying. Only reasons to leave, because the pressure is forcing me out. I'm a misshapen puzzle piece. A misfit toy. A curiosity. I don't fit in the tapestry. Value is determined by demand, there is no demand for me or my work, and therefore I have no value. I don't belong here.


## Why the Trail


To me the trail represents freedom. An ability to be whoever I want to be without apology. To dance alone in the dark, muttering to myself about my secret worlds. Maybe I'll see a beautiful sunset, ancient trees. Maybe the self-reflection will give me some understanding. At the end of the adventure I know there is a wall waiting for me. It's important not to look forward to that, but instead to live in the moment.

The grass is probably not greener. The people I'm going to meet are probably not going to like me. I'm going to be lonely. I'm going to be miserable. I'm going to spend the last few dollars I have. I'm going to feel a profound feeling of loss and regret. I will miss my cat and worry about her health. I'll miss my partner, I'll miss all the stupid people that pushed me to this place, I'll replay every dumb moment of my entire life in my head, and regret all of it. The people at home are still going to say bad things about me and my decisions. Maybe I'll be forgotten -- I sincerely hope so.

So my reasons? Rejection, loneliness, loss, bitterness, poverty, pressure, microaggressions, failure, rage, sadness. In my life -- spring, summer, and fall are past. Every day is getting colder and darker. I understand my trajectory and fate. I accept this.

So perhaps my reason for thru-hiking the PCT is acceptance.

See you out there.

I'm 43, 215 lbs. Prior ankle surgery, knee problems, heart arrhythmia, and a bone infection in my jaw. This really isn't an award-winning recipe for a thru-hiker. Sprinkle crushing poverty into the mix and it becomes downright toxic.

I really don't expect to last days or weeks.

Still...I have nowhere to be. The environment I am in has taken little bits of my soul year after year until I wonder if there is anything left. Balancing potential dangers and failure against nothing to lose is like dividing by zero.

## How I Got Here

I always loved books, imagination, and art. Growing up I wanted to make things desperately -- with every ounce of my being. I would skip school to read, wrote stories, hung out at coffee shops, loved intellectual conversation, got really into music, playing in bands, self-expression.

So naturally I became a car mechanic.

I was young and overconfident. I wandered into a job interview and they looked at me -- tall, strong, Caucasian, good hands -- and they said you got the job. Not remotely what I planned in life, but it turns out I have an aptitude for this sort of thing, and the rules of society make it very easy to swim in that direction.

I'm not sure if it's nature or nurture, but people are always looking at each other, sizing each other up, determining if you're part of their tribe, their social class, whatever. If someone challenges the common assumptions, those around them will unconsciously push them in a different direction. If I told others I made art, people first looked at me strangely. If by chance someone did see my art, it was examined through a lens of what was wrong. Rather than being encouraging, people would just put you down. From witnessed experience, if someone embodied an artistic look or fell into the correct class, people would assume the art -- good or bad -- had a deeper meaning, or that it was part of a growing process.

Constant invisible forces. Who says hello to you on the street. Who hires you for a job. Who is willing to pay for what you do.

Looking back, I was good. I had a lot of potential, but no one gave me any opportunity, and it was just so easy to show up to work. So I did this for 23 years, and just by momentum I became an expert. My friends and family celebrated me. I fixed their cars. I shared my knowledge, received low wages, left the jobs, started my own business, became even more venerated -- a legend, a guy who could just look at a machine and tell you what was wrong. I was particularly popular within gay, trans, and alternative communities. I believe it was because I was welcoming, inclusive, direct. I could show these people a window into worlds they themselves were denied, and it was novel.

At the end of the 23 years I had fixed tens of thousands of cars, rescued thousands of people, educated and supported countless others. I was a rock thrown in a pond, the ripples of my labor traveled outward affecting underserved communities and helping the world run.

## The Art Problem

I never stopped making art. It was always something I did in the evenings. Something people around me determined was a weird quirk. At times it was successful -- not monetarily, but at least I had an audience.

The people closest to me would continue to be derisive of the things I made, call them terrible, even when thousands of people in far off corners of the world watched. When people abroad shared my content, the people around me did not congratulate me. Instead they got quiet, like I was challenging their perception of what was right.

Eventually the mild success faded. Friends continued to be derisive. And I found myself sitting on a pile of ash, surrounded by people who did not appreciate the actual me -- just the reflection of who they wanted me to be, or those subtly rejecting my presence because of outward perception.

My circumstance is not uncommon. In fact it's extremely common. But it is not popular. The world is full of people who want to be artists and influencers, people who crave attention. Honestly, I want none of those things. I just want to make, express, love the things I love, and be myself. It would be so much easier if it were otherwise -- if it were a popular decision, if you had people around you to support your journey. Such is the nature of swimming against the currents.

## The End of the Road

Now the sun is setting. I had no money and nothing left to give. I had to shut my business down. For the past two years, people call and message me asking if I could still fix their cars, and would express disappointment when I don't. A subtle push to put me back where I fit.

I made art consistently through all of this. No one watched it. Even my partner who made an effort to watch, really doesn't like it, perhaps even wishes I would do something else. At parties, people would get a look when I talk to them. When I applied for jobs editing video, tried to collaborate with others, or offered my services for free -- none of it went anywhere.

If my art ends up being good, people make other excuses -- that looks like AI, they ask me if I really did it or had help, or who I copied. I understand the insinuation. People look at me and make a judgment call about what I'm capable of. I've never understood why people have such adoration for things like Marvel productions they have no real attachment to, while those around them slowly starve.

With no money coming in, I started cutting expenses living on less and less until I was down to a dollar a day. Still working sixty hours a week and hadn't received a real paycheck in years -- I began asking myself -- why am I still doing this? I could just not. Odd jobs for friends brought in a little money, but not enough.

I started to do simple math. If a worker doesn't get paid for his work, eventually he stops showing up to the job. Some people are sorry to see me go, but ultimately they're just sorry I'm not working for them anymore. I can be alone and not have to worry about the burden of people asking me to do things. I don't have to worry about the income disparity between myself and others. I don't need to worry about the social pressure of being a unemployed. The silent rejection when trying to be myself.

When I told people I was thinking about the trip, no one was supportive. Some were simply negative, others incredulous. My partner's response wasn't understanding.
Not a single response was about my journey as a person.
I actually understand this. People don't owe me anything. The world is what it is. But it pushed me further toward the door. Every micro aggression, every conversation that turned back toward what my leaving would cost someone else -- it all just confirmed what I already suspected.
It is sad, though. I have an understanding born from decades of confirmation. The lesson learned I wish I knew decades ago -- I will never fit in, I will never have acceptance. I will never have money. To keep trying for these things is folly.

There is no community. There is no support. I have no reason for staying. Only reasons to leave, because the pressure is forcing me out. I'm a misshapen puzzle piece. A misfit toy. A curiosity. I don't fit in the tapestry. Value is determined by demand and I have no value. I don't belong here.


## Why the Trail

I just want to be me, under any circumstances, without having to apologize for it. And I'm willing to accept whatever consequences -- poverty, sickness, misery. If the people around me don't appreciate who I am, they just don't get to be around me.

So I start looking for ways out. And I see the Pacific Crest Trail as an option to stay away for a while.

The trail itself looks like an adventure, but at the end of the adventure I know there is a wall. It's important not to look forward to that, but instead to live in the moment. I might be on the trail for three days, break my ankle, end up in a hospital. We do stupid things when we're young. Why not do stupid things when we're old.

To me the trail represents freedom. An ability to be whoever I want to be without apology. To dance alone in the dark, muttering to myself about my secret worlds. Maybe I'll see a beautiful sunset, ancient trees. Maybe the self-reflection will give me some understanding.

I feel like I'm standing on the edge of a cliff. Looking back, I see no reason to stay -- just ugliness. It takes bravery, but I'm going to turn around and dive in, not knowing what's in the murky water below.

The grass is probably not greener. The people I'm going to meet are probably not going to like me. I'm going to be lonely. I'm going to be miserable. I'm going to spend the last few dollars I have. I'm going to feel a profound feeling of loss and regret. I will miss my cat and worry about her health. I'll miss all the stupid people that pushed me to this place, I'll replay every dumb moment of my entire life in my head, and regret all of it. The people at home are still going to say bad things about me, ignore my art. Maybe I'll be forgotten -- I sincerely hope so.

So my reasons? Rejection, loneliness, loss, bitterness, poverty, pressure, microaggressions, failure, rage, sadness. In my life spring, summer, and fall are past. Every day is getting colder and darker. I understand my trajectory and fate. I accept this.

So perhaps my reason for thru-hiking the PCT is acceptance.

See you out there.

The [previous update](/posts/2026-10-18-servo-feedback-and-chassis/) ended with
twelve calibrated loose servos. Since then, the rebuilt robot has completed
loaded walking comparisons, executed a learned correction on the P4, and begun
collecting and comparing candidate gaits during ordinary Body Control use.

This is physical and software progress, with an important distinction:
**the learning loop works, but reliable walking is still the objective**.
On October 20 the owner reported that forward walking, backward walking,
turning and Run remained unsatisfactory. Earlier favorable trials do not
qualify every later revision.

## What the first physical comparisons showed

October 19 trials recorded commanded motion, all twelve fourth-wire readings,
IMU observations and camera frames. A host computer fitted a compact periodic
correction; the P4 evaluated its coefficients alongside the existing gait.
This is motor-policy fitting, separate from MetaHuman's language-model LoRAs.

After short commissioning runs, a longer model was trained on its own recording
and held fixed during four separate evaluation walks. Each evaluation included
sixteen steady walking cycles. Gyro heading feedback stayed on and stationary
self-leveling stayed off.

| Evaluation order | Learned correction | Estimated linkage tracking error | Owner-reported travel |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Baseline 1 | Off | 17.26 mm RMS | About 18 inches |
| Candidate 1 | 50% | 16.72 mm RMS | About 21 inches; no dragging or sag reported |
| Baseline 2 | Off | 17.21 mm RMS | About 21.5 inches |
| Candidate 2 | 50% | 16.77 mm RMS | About 30.5 inches; no sag reported |

Pooled estimated tracking error fell from **17.23 to 16.75 mm**, a **2.84%**
reduction for this forward-Walk comparison. The estimates come from measured
shaft positions and linkage geometry; they do not measure foot contact, force
or torque. Travel distances were owner observations against a tape reference.

Both learned runs had greater pitch excursion than both baselines. The second
baseline also traveled farther than the first candidate. Two trials per setting
are insufficient to establish a general travel, stability or energy benefit.
The separate offline result explaining about 92% of a recurring error pattern
is **not a 92% walking improvement**.

## Learning during normal use

The next development moves recording and optimization into Body Control's host
service. Closing the browser no longer ends recording. The controls now include:

- **Log gait data** to retain observations without starting optimization.
- **Learn while walking** to compare policies using ordinary locomotion commands.
- **Analyze now** to calculate a proposal; with learning off, this does not install it.
- Progress by maneuver, exploration settings, and saved-policy restore.

The host uses Bayesian optimization through BoTorch. It can adjust gait profile
parameters and the response to joint and body-motion measurements. The P4 still
executes motion locally. Training does not dispatch walks, return the robot to
a starting point, or pause ordinary movement while analysis runs.

The initial scope is **forward Walk, backward Walk, both turning directions,
and bounding Run**. Walk and Run have separate policy storage and comparisons;
Run retains its bounding gait. Crab and Crawl remain experimental and outside
this initial optimizer.

## What counts as a better candidate

The objective combines directional progress with yaw error, lateral drift,
body motion and joint following. Comparisons match the commanded direction,
stride and requested rate. Remaining still cannot win simply by eliminating
vibration, and spinning beyond the requested turn earns no extra reward.

Before installation, the existing linkage preview checks the proposed change
against the observed commands and saved calibration. That geometric check does
not predict loaded stability, slipping or collisions. Candidates are installed
in RAM; only an incumbent supported by the required comparison is saved to flash.
Walk promotion needs repeated evidence across all four priority directions,
rather than one successful forward run.

The October 20 live record completed a **record → propose → install → compare →
next-candidate** iteration. The first Walk candidate did not demonstrate
improvement, so the incumbent was retained and the service advanced to another
candidate. Run accumulated a baseline and began its own comparison, which was
still incomplete at the recorded checkpoint. **No improved policy was promoted
during that verification.**

## Camera progress is now connected, with uncertain scale

Body Control can select experimental **MoGe-2 depth and landmark tracking** as
an alternative to its measured ground-plane method. The current implementation
matches visual landmarks and estimates camera motion against retained 3-D
points. This supersedes the first dense frame-to-frame depth comparison, which
could mistake inconsistent learned depth for real motion.

The source feeds the existing locomotion-measurement and gait-recording paths.
Its distances are labeled **learned metric estimates**: absolute accuracy and
metric uncertainty are unverified. It is not calibrated visual-inertial odometry
or a loop-closing map. A supplied camera-to-body transform accounts for mounting
geometry; without one, the reported origin is the camera.

Blur, lost landmarks and transport gaps can make a window unusable. Tracking can
start a new local reference, but no distance is invented across the gap. Valid
measurements of a poor walk remain useful evidence against a candidate; missing
measurements are not scored as zero error or zero travel.

## A visible learning history

**Recordings → Learning history** now charts scored observations and candidate
decisions. Filters separate gait, command conditions and measurement contexts.
The table exposes individual measurements; expandable comparisons show policy
changes and uncertainty. History can be downloaded as JSON without starting
motion or analysis.

Activation checks recorded seven separate contexts, including a current Walk
bank with sixteen scored observations and Run with three. Older records without
timestamps remain explicitly undated. Changed measurement contexts are archived
rather than mixed into a single apparent progress curve.

## What remains to establish

The latest record still includes camera failures, HTTP peer closures and lost
trace records. Completed scored windows survive later interruptions, but short
unfinished windows may produce no observation. Logging and learning were left
enabled for normal use, without more dedicated test sequences.

Repeatable forward/backward movement, turns and Run still need physical
qualification. Metric camera accuracy, measured support/contact, energy use and
autonomous return to a starting pose remain separate open work.

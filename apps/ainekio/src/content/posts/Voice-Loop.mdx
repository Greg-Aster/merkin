# From hearing speech to answering aloud

Ainekio records speech through its microphone, sends it to MetaHuman OS for
transcription and conversation, and plays the response through its speaker.
The robot handles capture and playback; MetaHuman handles speech-to-text,
conversation, and speech generation.

V1 has produced audible playback. Reliable repeated conversation in the room
still needs testing, including wake detection, interruptions, and reconnects.
In particular, **no production Ainekio wake-word model has been accepted and
tracked**, so the initial configuration leaves wake detection unavailable.

## Listening: decide when a spoken turn ends

Wake detection and voice activity detection do different jobs. A **wake-word
model** would recognize a chosen phrase that starts listening. **Voice activity
detection (VAD)** separates speech from silence and decides when the spoken
segment, or utterance, has ended.

The S3 firmware captures microphone audio and applies those listening controls
when available. The gateway assembles an utterance into a WAV audio file and
sends it through MetaHuman’s Environment Bridge, the connection for external
devices. Whisper transcribes the recording into text, which enters the
conversation as an `audio_utterance` observation.

The VAD model uses a minimum recording window, a silence window to end the
turn, and an absolute maximum duration. A fixed-size queue in PSRAM, temporary
board memory, absorbs short network stalls without letting buffering grow
without limit.

At the end of an utterance, the robot can take a still image with the same ID.
The Bridge can join that image to the audio before submitting the turn. If the
camera is unavailable, audio proceeds or fails under its own rules; an image
is not invented to fill the gap.

## Wake detection: the model is still unfinished

The firmware includes microWakeWord and TensorFlow Lite Micro to run a small
wake detector locally. It loads an owner-trained model package from LittleFS,
the robot’s asset filesystem, and checks the package description, expected
input/output shapes, audio preprocessing, and model ID before reporting
`wake_ready=true`.

A pilot ran on the physical robot but triggered on sounds that were not the
wake phrase. Its staged confidence threshold was much lower than the documented
package default, and the test set had too few **negative examples**: ordinary
sounds that should leave the detector silent. A threshold sweep provided tuning
data, not an accepted production model.

The next evaluation needs hours of room audio containing robot speech, servo
noise, television, fans, and similar-sounding words. It must record detection
confidence and **rearm behavior**—whether the detector returns to a ready state
for the next wake phrase after handling one turn.

## Speaking: generating audio is only the first step

MetaHuman uses Kokoro or the configured speech-synthesis service to turn a
response into audio. The resulting file has size limits, a session identity,
and an action ID. The Environment Bridge and Ainekio adapter send its PCM
samples through the gateway to the robot’s speaker queue.

The gateway paces delivery rather than sending the whole file as fast as
possible. The firmware accepts samples during an explicitly opened
text-to-speech (TTS) burst and plays them through the I2S audio interface.
It reports queue overflow, interruption, data outside a burst, and completion.

The adapter returns that playback result to the originating action. Successful
synthesis, or a `sendText` acknowledgement, does not establish that the speaker
played the response. September adapter work also keeps an uncertain outcome
when playback may have started but its final result is missing.

## September progress: recorded speech delivery

The September 8 live review found eight durable TTS deliveries claimed and
acknowledged on their first attempt, with new outward responses in the
conversation buffer. This establishes activity through the revised delivery
path. It is not audible confirmation of every response, a wake-word acceptance
test, or proof that an entire conversation completed.

Saved speech results let the parent request distinguish a delivered response,
an interruption, and a failure. Speaking can be one step in a larger objective;
its result returns to that request so unfinished work can be reviewed.

## What the physical playback test showed

A limited **prebuffer**, which queues some audio before playback starts, and
small blocks paced relative to send time produced clean audible V1 playback
in one test. The run still recorded speaker **underruns**, where the playback
queue empties before the next samples arrive, and separate microphone transport
faults. Those records matter even when a particular response sounds good.

Full acoustic echo cancellation is absent. The microphone is deliberately
suspended while the robot speaks, then resumes after a cooldown. This avoids
listening to its own speaker during that interval, but also limits conversational
turn-taking while speech is playing.

Small listening and speaking motion cues use the normal calibration, stop,
Sleep, and fail-safe rules. Battery-voltage warnings do not establish whether
the motor supply can handle combined motion and audio loads; that needs a
physical power test.

## Audio format reference

<details>
<summary className="cursor-pointer py-3">Capture format and firmware details</summary>

The microphone produces **16 kHz, 16-bit mono PCM**, meaning one channel of
16,000 samples per second, with two bytes per sample. Each 20 ms frame contains
640 bytes. The gateway packages a completed utterance as WAV for transcription.

A version-pinned neural VAD model applies the minimum, silence, and maximum
windows. Its PSRAM queue has a fixed memory limit. The wake runtime validates
the model manifest, tensor shape, audio frontend, and model ID before making
wake-gated listening available.

## The next conversation test

MetaHuman’s September changes connect audio and images to conversation context,
speech delivery, and saved workflow results. The September 8 delivery records
show part of that path running; the later workflow repairs and repeated acoustic
performance still need validation. P4 also needs its own microphone, speaker,
camera, and transport implementation.

The next room test should repeat the whole exchange: wake detection, utterance
capture and optional image, Whisper transcript, conversation, generated speech,
gateway delivery, and robot playback result. Record wake rearming, dropped PCM,
underruns, reconnects, and microphone resumption across multiple turns.
The [status report](/posts/current-status/) records the dated progress.

Most of this week's work has gone into walking. The body prototype is done;
the next problem is making it move well. The new neural policy learns foot
placements from demonstrations, then runs from sensor and command inputs.
Forward, reverse and both turn directions now work in simulation, with a
substantial difference between direct simulation and the full execution path.

## Learning on the Blender body

The simulation uses the exported Blender assembly, articulated linkages and boot
collision surfaces. It retains the **35 / 20 / 28 / 38 / 55 mm** mechanism,
twelve servos and the last recorded **650 g** assembly mass. Loaded servo torque,
friction and mass distribution still need better physical measurements.

The policy requests standing-relative foot offsets. Existing inverse kinematics
convert those offsets to the coupled joint targets. This matters: an early
independent-joint experiment produced geometrically rejected requests in all
100 sampled cases.

The productive approach has been **DAgger**, or dataset aggregation: run the
learner, collect the states it actually reaches, label those states with the
demonstration's next action, and train again. The teacher's contribution decreases
across rounds; final evaluations run the policy alone.

## Same policy, two execution paths

The October 30 selected policy was replayed on November 1 through Body Control's
real WebSocket and native P4 executor, connected to simulated hardware. Each row
below is a 16-second trial. Linear speeds are in **mm/s**; turns are in **rad/s**.
These are fixed-reference trials, not a robustness sweep across varied surfaces
or noisy sensors.

| Request | Requested | Direct simulation | Body Control + P4 replay |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Forward | +80 mm/s | +27.82 mm/s | +22.45 mm/s |
| Reverse | −20 mm/s | −22.20 mm/s | −20.55 mm/s |
| Slower reverse | −15 mm/s | −18.28 mm/s | −13.48 mm/s |
| Left turn | +0.120 rad/s | +0.128 rad/s | +0.021 rad/s |
| Right turn | −0.120 rad/s | −0.148 rad/s | −0.113 rad/s |

Neither set recorded a fall. The connected replay rejected no targets, but the
left turn deteriorated sharply and acquired sideways drift. Forward reached
about 28% of requested speed through Body Control. A clean completion receipt
therefore does not settle the walking problem.

These are the recorded baseline results before correcting fractional command
encoding: the adapter had rounded a −18.75% request to −19%. That correction
needs a fresh comparison; it is not an explanation for the entire transfer gap.

## What improved, and what failed

| Experiment | Result | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Better forward demonstration, matched 60-second direct trials | 16.40 → 20.96 mm/s | Teacher quality improved the learned gait. |
| Five-command aggregation | Forward, two reverse speeds and both turn directions acquired | A command-responsive policy replaced the forward-only candidate. |
| SAC reinforcement learning after imitation | The good gait degraded; lower learning rate did not preserve it | Retained the imitation policy rather than promoting the degraded runs. |
| Earlier PPO reward | Reward improved while travel fell toward zero | The objective was rewarding stability without useful movement. |

The SAC handoff copied the actor but started with an untrained critic. A lower
learning rate softened the damage without solving it. More optimizer steps were
not automatically better walking.

The reward investigation also changed how progress is scored. Linear and turning
tracking now interact; optional short-window velocity scoring avoids punishing
every surge within a real step. Bounded swing credit rewards lifting the feet
without rewarding indefinitely raised feet or rapid tiny shuffles. Those changes
are training tools, not a measured physical speed gain.

## Training through the execution path

The November 1 continuation replays the demonstrations through Body Control to
collect fresh observations. It carries over the selected actor weights, discards
the old optimizer and replay state, and trains against the connected executor.
The run was launched with 32-second episodes and 55,000 additional imitation
updates planned; no completed result was available at this review.

Two integration defects surfaced during preparation: fractional command rounding
and the collection environment retaining the native executor during checkpoint
evaluation. Both have source repairs and focused regression coverage. The robot
was not commanded or switched to a neural policy by these trials.

Next are matched connected evaluations, then supervised trials on the completed
body: foot clearance, loaded tracking, actual displacement, turns and Stop/Release.
Run, uneven terrain and recovery still need their own results.

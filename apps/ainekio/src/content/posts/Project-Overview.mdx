Ainekio is an open-source hobby robot project built on a workbench: a `650 g`, 3D-printed twelve-servo quadruped body (`~$85` in parts, [`Greg-Aster/Ainekio-bot`](https://github.com/Greg-Aster/Ainekio-bot)) paired with **[Ainekio OS](https://github.com/Greg-Aster/ainekio-os)** (formerly MetaHuman OS; [architecture guide](/posts/ainekio-os/)), a local-first AI operating system for conversation, long-term memory, perception, and bounded autonomy.

This site is the project's build journal and technical reference library. It tracks what worked on the bench, what broke on the living-room carpet, and how the physical hardware and persistent software mind fit together.

## What Ainekio does

Rather than treating the robot as a stateless remote-controlled toy that forgets everything on power-off, Ainekio splits work cleanly between real-time embedded control on the body and persistent memory and planning in Ainekio OS:

| Capability | Role in the system |
| --- | --- |
| **Conversation & Standing Persona** | Holds natural voice or text conversations through a single persistent identity grounded in live room context and verified action outcomes |
| **Perception & Expression** | Connects camera snapshots, optical-flow obstacle range, wake-word speech capture, and IMU/joint sensing to spoken replies, 59 animated LCD facial expressions, and physical posture |
| **Attributed Memory** | Remembers conversations, camera observations, and task outcomes across days while tagging each memory's source so imagined thoughts are never confused with physical facts |
| **Closed-Loop & Neural Locomotion** | Runs closed-loop standing and analytical gaits (`Walk`, `Run`, `Crawl`, `Crab`), an on-chip `50 Hz` `107.8 KB` MicroDuck neural walking policy, host reinforcement-learning policies, and a 349-motion Freestyle library |
| **Bounded Autonomy** | Operates across Reactive, Semi Mode, and Full Auto modes while keeping language models strictly behind geometric and electrical safety checks |
| **Overnight Consolidation** | Reviews recent interactions, updates specialist adapters, runs symbolic dreaming, and evaluates persona drift during a scheduled `02:00` software sleep cycle |

## How the three layers fit together

The system is split across three cooperating layers so hard real-time motor control and stall protection never wait on language-model inference or Wi-Fi round trips:

![System architecture connecting Ainekio OS, the host gateway and Cockpit HUD, and the onboard ESP32-P4 quadruped body](/posts/ainekio-os/ainekio-os-architecture.webp)

### 1. Onboard `ESP32-P4` Body

- **Controller & On-Chip Neural Gait**: A `Waveshare ESP32-P4-WIFI6` board (dual-core `360 MHz` RISC-V with `32 MB` PSRAM, `32 MB` flash, and an onboard `ESP32-C6` Wi-Fi 6 coprocessor) runs body safety and peripheral control. Core 1 executes a distilled `107.8 KB` `int8` MicroDuck neural walking policy natively at `50 Hz` inside the processor's `128 KB` L2 cache.
- **12-Servo Closed-Loop Actuation**: Twelve modified `MG90S` metal-gear micro servos (three per leg: shoulder roll, upper-carrier pitch, and four-bar knee crank) are driven by a `PCA9685` PWM board. A soldered 4th wire on every servo's internal potentiometer routes through channels `C4–C15` of a `CD74HC4067` analog multiplexer into `GPIO49` (`ADC2_CH0`) so the microcontroller can measure real shaft angles and detect stalls.
- **Face, Chin Camera, IMU & Audio**: The front carrier houses a `1.9"` `ST7789` IPS color LCD (`320 × 170`) rendering **59 animated expressions**, with an `OV5647` MIPI-CSI camera mounted in the lower-jaw / chin mount below the display (`180°` rotated in software with a `10,000 µs` motion-safe exposure cap). Inside the shell sit a `208 Hz` `LSM6DS3` 6-axis IMU for self-leveling and tip/shake detection, plus onboard `ES8311` / `NS4150B` audio with `WakeNet9s` wake-word detection.
- **Chassis & Power**: The complete robot weighs `650 g` (`245.85 × 182.79 × 109.06 mm`) and runs untethered from a battery-mounted **Wireless Keeper Bridge** carrying a `2S` (`8.4 V` max) Li-ion pack, a `Mini560` `5 V / 5 A` buck regulator, and an onboard `2S` USB-C boost charger.

### 2. Host Gateway & Cockpit HUD

- Runs on a nearby desktop workstation or an offboard `Radxa Dragon Q6A` single-board computer, maintaining the authenticated `protocol-v1` WebSocket session with the robot over LAN (with automatic Wi-Fi hotspot fallback when away from home infrastructure).
- Serves the **Body Control** workbench and full-viewport **Cockpit HUD**, giving live camera video, optical-flow + monocular depth obstacle range, 12-channel servo tracking health, a switch between on-chip and host-streamed neural gaits, and a one-click pause toggle for Ainekio OS control.
- Houses the **349-motion Freestyle library** (alongside the **489-row** training dataset combining **73** library motions with **416** synthesized fault-recovery trajectories ending in `Stand`), GPU MuJoCo hardware-twin reinforcement-learning pipelines, object tracking, and visual depth odometry.

### 3. Ainekio OS Mind

- Runs as a standalone local-first personal AI system across its local web workbench, terminal CLI, and Android app, or connects to the robot through its embodied bridge (locally or across an optional remote tunnel).
- Routes specialist cognitive roles—intent classification, robot action selection, vision grounding, outward persona speech, fast physical reflexes, memory curation, and optional cloud/CLI coding delegates—across local `llama.cpp` / Ollama models, local or LAN-shared Whisper speech-to-text, and Kokoro or Piper text-to-speech.
- Executes inspectable node-based cognitive workflows backed by crash-safe SQLite checkpoints so multi-step physical tasks survive movement waits or brief disconnects without repeating actions twice.

## V1 and V2 hardware at a glance

| Feature | V1 (Eight-Servo Baseline) | V2 (Twelve-Servo Closed-Loop Body) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Actuators** | 8× `MG90S` (2 per leg, open-loop PWM) | 12× `MG90S` (3 per leg, 4th-wire analog feedback on `CD74HC4067` `C4–C15` → `GPIO49`) |
| **Controller** | Freenove `ESP32-S3-WROOM CAM` (`N16R8`) | `Waveshare ESP32-P4-WIFI6` + `ESP32-C6` + `PCA9685` |
| **Mechanics** | Open Sesame-derived frame, planar legs | Enclosed shell (`650 g`), hip roll + `35/20/28/38/55 mm` four-bar linkage legs |
| **Display & Camera** | `0.96"` `SSD1306` OLED + `OV3660` | `1.9"` `ST7789` color LCD (59 expressions) + chin-mounted `OV5647` MIPI-CSI |
| **Inertial & Audio** | External `INMP441` + `MAX98357A`, no IMU | `LSM6DS3` `208 Hz` IMU + onboard `ES8311` / `NS4150B` + `WakeNet9s` |
| **Locomotion** | 38 fixed keyframe sequences | On-chip `50 Hz` `107.8 KB` neural gait, closed-loop `Stand`/`Walk`, 349-motion Freestyle library, MuJoCo RL |

V1 remains documented in the archive as the baseline that proved out the early bridge and firmware architecture. See the [V1 vs. V2 comparison](/posts/v1-v2-comparison/) for side-by-side specs.

## How a spoken request becomes physical motion

1. **Sense**: A spoken phrase (captured after the `WakeNet9s` wake word), a camera snapshot, or a physical body event (`tipped`, `shaken`, `stalled`) reaches the Host Gateway and forwards to Ainekio OS.
2. **Decide**: Ainekio OS classifies the turn—routing sudden physical jostles to a fast reflex path with varied vocal/LCD reactions, or routing multi-step requests through specialist models that pick bounded robot commands, locomotion goals, or Freestyle posture programs while the Standing Persona owns outward speech.
3. **Move & Verify**: The gateway sends checked commands or velocity twists to the `ESP32-P4`. Onboard firmware projects foot targets onto the valid four-bar workspace, runs either the analytical solver or Core 1's `50 Hz` MicroDuck neural policy, verifies physical shaft arrival via the `CD74HC4067` feedback mux, and applies `LSM6DS3` attitude leveling.
4. **Confirm**: Action receipts, shaft-arrival status, and a fresh post-motion camera frame return to Ainekio OS so it can verify whether the physical goal actually succeeded before deciding what to do next.

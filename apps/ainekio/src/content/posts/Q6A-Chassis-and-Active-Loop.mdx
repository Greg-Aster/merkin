> *Historical design-study note (September 30, 2026): This milestone explored packaging a `Radxa Dragon Q6A` Linux SBC directly inside the Ainekio V2 shell. Six days later, to shed weight and let the twelve `MG90S` servos stand and walk cleanly without overheating, onboard SBC packaging was dropped in favor of the lighter **`650 g` wireless `ESP32-P4` body** with the Q6A or desktop running offboard ([see the October 6 compact linkage rebuild](/posts/2026-10-06-compact-linkage/) and [current V2 hardware](/posts/ainekio-v2/)).*

In late September 2026, bench work focused on two questions at once: whether a `Radxa Dragon Q6A` single-board computer could fit inside the Ainekio V2 chassis alongside the `ESP32-P4` controller, and how to keep camera perception and walk adjustments responsive between slower language-model turns.

At this stage of the study, direct Wi-Fi connected the `ESP32-P4` and `Q6A` (with USB kept as a bench alternative and ROS 2 evaluated only as an optional bridge). Real-time gait timing remained on the `ESP32-P4` while the Linux host handled camera perception and heavier planning.

The September 30 CAD revision carved out internal mounting volume for the Q6A board and updated shoulder clearances, regenerating the 47 motion sources to match. While software parity tests and the `ESP32-P4` firmware build passed, physical mass and balance checks soon showed that carrying both an SBC and a large USB-PD battery inside the shell overloaded the micro servos—leading directly to the [October 6 weight-reduction and compact linkage rebuild](/posts/2026-10-06-compact-linkage/), where the Q6A moved offboard as an edge host.

Unlike the onboard SBC layout, the software foundations landed in this September 30 push became permanent building blocks of V2 and **[Ainekio OS](/posts/ainekio-os/)**:

- **Out-of-band camera processing & in-stride walk updates**: The host gateway gained newest-frame camera handling outside its WebSocket receive loop, plus an authenticated path for adjusting an active walk's `Speed`, `Stride %`, or `Motion rate` while preserving its gait phase and command lease.
- **Durable execution across waits**: MetaHuman OS (now Ainekio OS) introduced saved SQLite workflow checkpoints capable of suspending across physical movement waits, accepting mid-task user steering, and carrying correlated `frame_id` timestamps forward.
- **Pieces completed in subsequent October milestones**: On September 30, live YOLO tracking and closed-loop `LSM6DS3` IMU balance were not yet wired into physical motion; both were brought online in the October milestones that followed ([Vision Loop](/posts/vision-loop/) and [Oct 14 Startup & Leveling](/posts/2026-10-14-startup-and-leveling/)).

The original eight-servo `ESP32-S3` V1 baseline remained supported alongside V2 throughout this transition; see the [Project Overview](/posts/project-overview/) for the current system architecture.

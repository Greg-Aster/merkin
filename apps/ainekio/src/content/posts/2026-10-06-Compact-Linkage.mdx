> **October 8 milestone:** [The lighter robot stands, walks, and responds to commands.](/posts/2026-10-08-standing-walking/)

With the first V2 chassis printed (even before fitting the LCD and outer carapace
panels), the robot **could not stand up on its MG90 servos** while carrying the
heavy 10,000 mAh USB power bank.

After evaluating larger servo alternatives, we kept the MG90s and redesigned the
robot around their torque envelope across three fronts:

1. **Shorter 35 / 20 / 28 mm leg linkage** for ~20% greater lifting leverage and
   faster joint response, with all 23 motions retargeted in Blender.
2. **Lighter chassis and discrete power** (~20% structural weight reduction),
   replacing the heavy integrated power bank with a 2S battery and onboard
   buck/charging boards, and prioritizing the **Wireless (offboard Q6A)** build.
3. **Continuous steering, IMU attitude estimation, and dual camera profiles** in the
   ESP32-P4 firmware and Body Control gateway.

## Reworking the linkage: 35 mm carrier, 20 mm crank, 28 mm pickup

To increase mechanical advantage without losing the robot's expressive repertoire,
the `compact-35-20-28-38-55` geometry shortens the upper carrier and input crank
while lengthening the lower-leg pickup lever:

| Dimension | Previous model | October 5–6 compact linkage | Change |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Upper carrier (`Part_023`) | 40 mm | **35 mm** | −5 mm (−12.5%) |
| Input crank (`Part_009`) | 24 mm | **20 mm** | −4 mm |
| Lower-leg pickup radius | 24 mm | **28 mm** | +4 mm |
| Connecting rod (`Part_026`) | 38 mm | **38 mm** | Unchanged |
| Distal lower leg (`Part_027`) | 55 mm | **55 mm** | Unchanged |

Download the [dimension comparison CSV](/posts/2026-10-06-progress/linkage-comparison.csv)
or see the [gait development log](/posts/gait-development/) for the full kinematic history.

### Retargeting all 23 named motions

Thirteen clips preserve their original body trajectory, horizontal foot positions,
and full-sole height without modification: *Celebrate, Curious, Cute, Dance, Freaky,
Lay Down, Sad, Shake, Shrug, Stretch, Surprised, Swim,* and *Worm*. *Crouch* uses the
native inverse-kinematics planner with its −35 mm terminal body translation.

The remaining motions were refitted to match the shorter carrier:

| Motion | Retained behavior | Adjustment in the 35/20/28 mm library |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sit | Body trajectory, timing, contact sequence, and sole orientation | Rear landings move 5.584 mm forward |
| Wave | Seated three-wave sequence and return to standing | Uses the revised Sit entry |
| Upright | Sit entry, two front steps, and full −90° body rise | Refitted body path; forelimb reach changes from 90.339 to 76.585 mm |
| Rest | Step timing, boot orientations, and chassis lowering | Landings move 5.018 mm forward |
| Bow / Nod | Foot paths and original timing | Body translation adjusted by up to 5.0 mm |
| Pushup | Five repetitions and timing | Body translation refitted by up to 4.767 mm |
| Play Dead | Sequence and timing | Front slide endpoint moves 7.158 mm rearward |
| Point | Carrier angle, boot orientation, and extension timing | Pointing toe displaced by 5.0 mm due to shorter carrier |

The completed offline library spans **31,031 source/preview samples** and **62,517
native sampler timestamps** across all twelve joints, with 46 nonsequential Blender
poses matching the C forward-kinematics solver to within **0.000027 mm**. Across
Upright and Bow, the front cranks span **243.87°** of combined travel against the
225° reference window (400–2,900 µs).

For continuous locomotion, the controller now uses a **96 mm full Walk sweep**,
**8.85 mm Walk lift**, and **−8 mm body offset**, while continuous Crawl uses a
**−22 mm body offset**, **18 mm sweep**, and **4 mm lift**.

## Lighter chassis rebuild and serviceable electronics bridge

Alongside the linkage changes, we stripped unnecessary mass and consolidated split
printed parts across the frame:

- **Front bearing panel (Oct 1):** Reduced mesh volume from **5,421 to 4,347 mm³
  (−19.81%)** using a 2.5 mm main web and four oval cutouts while keeping the 78.5 mm
  width and 12.5 × 4.5 mm bearing pockets.
- **One-piece side supports and rear tray:** Merged the separate side supports, trays,
  and inside connectors into single printable solids per side, fastening directly
  through bottom-slot tabs and removable flat-printed panel braces.
- **Serviceable electronics bridge (Oct 3):** Uses seven M2×6 retainers (three P4 edge
  clips, two IMU clips, and two PCA9685 spacer retainers) so the boards can be
  mounted on the bridge before sliding it under the crossbars.

## Continuous walk steering, IMU estimator, and camera profiles

While replacement chassis and linkage parts printed, the software stack gained
several core capabilities:

- **Continuous Walk steering:** The gateway can now update a running Walk action
  in-place with signed `forward` and `turn` commands (−100 to +100, positive turn =
  left yaw), transitioning smoothly through the gait cycle without resetting phase.
  Independent controls govern gait speed, stride/rate, named-motion speed, and maximum
  joint speed.
- **Portable C IMU attitude estimator:** A new six-axis C module (`Slave/software/imu`)
  applies sensor-to-body alignment and gyro bias calibration to compute roll/pitch
  orientation and sample freshness, ready to wire into the P4 hardware task.
- **Dual camera profiles & recognition worker:** The P4 camera driver supports
  independent preview (QVGA/VGA at 0–15 fps) and still capture (QVGA/VGA/XGA)
  profiles under a single sensor/encoder owner, feeding a gateway recognition worker
  that processes the newest frame and reports summaries and detected objects to the
  Environment Bridge.
- **Q6A Wi-Fi hotspot & ROS 2 Jazzy:** The P4 associated with the Q6A hotspot and
  completed authenticated settings readback (with connection drop recovery in
  progress), while desktop ROS 2 Jazzy talker/listener, MCAP bag recording, and RViz
  checks were verified.

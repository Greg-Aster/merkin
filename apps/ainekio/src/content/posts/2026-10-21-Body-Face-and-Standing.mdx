Following the [October 18 fourth-wire feedback rebuild](/posts/2026-10-18-servo-feedback-and-chassis/),
the October 21 photographs show Ainekio V2 reassembled with its red front shell,
bezel, chin, cheek fangs, and side guards around the wide color display. Alongside the
mechanical assembly, we repaired a feedback ground fault to verify closed-loop
standing, ran physical walking comparisons with a learned periodic correction, wired
up host-side **BoTorch Bayesian gait optimization** and **MoGe-2** visual landmark
odometry, and added a single-call **Fast Body Event** reaction path in MetaHuman OS.

[Browse all three October 21 build photographs in the gallery](/gallery/).

## Aligned LCD face and precomputed side-light mask

The wide color LCD now renders brighter pupils and swept cyan side lights centered
within the printed bezel opening (shifted 15 px horizontally to match the physical
mask cutout). A precomputed mask supplies the permanent side-light geometry while the
eyes and expression elements remain procedural.

## Ground-repaired 4th-wire Stand verification and connection-loss hold

On the first loaded standing test after reassembly, every analog feedback channel read
pinned near zero even though the robot was clearly standing on the desk. Tracing the
harness with a multimeter turned up a loose ground lead on the multiplexer board;
resoldering that ground immediately brought back clean potentiometer voltages across all
twelve joints.

Retesting on October 20 with closed-loop Stand verification enabled (**400 µs** arrival
tolerance), the robot pressed smoothly into Stand and held steady across **65 snapshots
over 19.22 seconds**: all twelve joints stayed well inside bounds with a peak filtered
error of **175.74 µs** and zero corrective replans.

I also hardened what happens when something goes wrong mid-motion. If analog readings
drop out, the P4 finishes its trajectory, keeps holding servo torque, and reports the
pose as unverified instead of pretending it arrived or declaring a phantom jam. If Wi-Fi
drops mid-walk, the controller no longer cuts power to the PCA9685 and flops onto its
belly—finite gestures finish locally and continuous walks step cleanly into a powered
standing hold, followed by an automatic rest lay-down after 3 minutes of inactivity.
Named gestures like *Sit*, *Rest*, and *Crouch* now ease into their final approach and
wait for real shaft arrival before starting hold timers.

## Learning from physical walking: periodic correction and BoTorch loop

On October 19 I logged commanded poses, all twelve 4th-wire shaft readings, IMU
attitude, and camera frames across real floor walks, fitted a periodic joint-lag
correction on the workstation, and tested it at 50% gain on the P4 across four 16-cycle
walking bouts (with gyro heading hold active):

| Trial | Learned correction | Linkage tracking error (RMS) | Measured floor travel |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| Baseline 1 | Off | 17.26 mm | ~18.0 in |
| Candidate 1 | 50% | **16.72 mm** | ~21.0 in (no rear sag or toe drag) |
| Baseline 2 | Off | 17.21 mm | ~21.5 in |
| Candidate 2 | 50% | **16.77 mm** | ~30.5 in (no rear sag) |

Pooled linkage tracking error tightened from **17.23 mm to 16.75 mm RMS (−2.84%)** and
stopped the rear hindquarters from sagging mid-stride.

### Host-side BoTorch optimization and MoGe-2 visual landmark tracking

To tune gait parameters systematically rather than tweaking numbers by hand, I built a
host-side **BoTorch Bayesian optimizer** paired with **MoGe-2** monocular depth
landmark tracking. Candidate gait profiles are checked against the four-bar kinematic
workspace first, tested in P4 RAM across forward, reverse, turn, and bound trials
scored by 3D visual landmark displacement, and saved to flash only when they beat the
incumbent across all priority directions.

## Sub-500 ms physical reactions when tipped or shaken

Routing physical bumps through the full multi-step planning graph felt noticeably too
slow, so I added a dedicated single-call **Fast Body Event** reflex path running on the
local **Qwen3.5 2B Q8_0** model. As soon as the IMU flags a tip or sudden shake, the P4
flips the LCD face to `Confused` or `Surprised` and fires a single inference call for a
short, dryly observant spoken reaction—landing in **486 ms** for a tip-over and
**316 ms** when picked up and shaken.

Because walking and bounding shake the chassis on purpose, Body Control now filters IMU
triggers against the active gait and compares tilt against the intended pose angle so
the robot doesn't startle itself mid-trot. Autonomous check-ins also wait for a genuine
5-minute quiet window rather than re-triggering on internal queue updates, and full
camera-plus-depth frames now cross the bridge without hitting old payload caps.

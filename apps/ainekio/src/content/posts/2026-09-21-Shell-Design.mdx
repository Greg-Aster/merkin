# The body takes shape around the moving parts

Modeling the chassis and redesigning the legs have occupied most of the recent
build time. Alongside those mechanical changes, the outer body has been worked
through repeated front, side and three-quarter comparisons: the face opening,
collar, roof, fins, guards and chin all affect how the robot fits together and
how it looks.

![September 20 CAD assembly with a pale developing shell above four articulated red legs and exposed black servo housings.](/posts/2026-09-21-design-progress/assembly-september-20.webp)

*Actual CAD view from the September 20 image set. The pale shell surfaces are
part of the ongoing modeling work. This is an assembly study, not a photograph

This post follows the shell revisions. The companion
[chassis packaging update](/posts/2026-09-21-chassis-packaging/) covers the
narrower frame and camera-cable opening, while the
[compact-leg update](/posts/2026-09-21-compact-leg-design/) records link lengths,
print faces and the horn-fit correction.

## Comparing the silhouette from more than one direction

The September 19 iteration log records changes against the supplied concept
images and imported reference model. A shape that improved one view did not
always work in another. Several early shoulder-fin trials were rejected:
raising and flattening the old plates did not produce the intended swept
profile in both front and side views, and one outline trial folded faces.
Those trials were restored instead of being counted as finished geometry.

The retained iterations include a narrower collar with a rounded rear relief,
new fin surfaces and supports, localized roof-tip corrections, shallow panel
seams and small changes to the brow, cheeks and chin. “Retained” describes
that point in the design sequence; the later assembly continued to evolve.

| Area | Problem explored | Recorded response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Collar | Broad solid center obscured the layered profile | Narrowed shoulders, recessed front edge and enlarged a rounded rear opening |
| Shoulder fins | Earlier plate trials failed the front/side comparison | Rejected those trials; developed new surfaces with prototype supports |
| Roof tips | Unequal outward flare | Matched the outer tips and lowered their outer edges locally |
| Brow and cheeks | Harsh faceted highlights and missing panel divisions | Local crown and shading changes; shallow seams while retaining the face opening |
| Chin | Upper center needed a small convex ridge | Up to 0.6 mm local advance; retained lower geometry and camera opening |

The change is at the pointed outer roof edges, especially the near tip below
the vent grooves. Their lateral positions were brought to **±41 mm**, from
approximately −42.747 and +44.645 mm, and the outermost edges lowered 1 mm.
The center roof, vents and fin mounting regions stayed fixed in that pass.
These red-shell images document an earlier stage than the pale September 20
assembly shown above.

## Rebuilding surfaces and adding attachment geometry

Later on September 19, the neck plate was rebuilt as a simpler symmetric
surface with approximately **2 mm** paired-surface spacing. Its recorded base
mesh changed from 11,099 vertices to 162, and the evaluated shell check found
one closed component. This is a result for that plate revision, not a blanket
printability result for the robot.

The evening roof sequence explored a hollow interior and vents, then reduced
excessive lower-wall depth. A further reference comparison led to a controlled
faceted reconstruction, approximately **99 × 41 mm per side**, with a mostly
level rim, localized 4.4 mm flange step, vent openings, three interior hex
mounting pads per side and nominal 1.6 mm walls. The record reports closed
evaluated geometry for that pass, with no STL exports from it.

Those successive changes should be read as design history. Their dimensions
do not automatically describe every surface in the later assembly or prove
that fasteners and print tolerances have been settled.

## Next: connect the shell decisions to assembly checks

The useful next questions are about the chosen shell and leg combination:
where each panel attaches, whether screws remain accessible, how the camera
cable bends, and whether the links and servo housings clear the body through
the intended motion. Surface cleanup and a closed mesh help prepare a part,
but fit, print strength and moving clearance need their own checks.

The [gait journal](/posts/gait-development/) continues the movement experiments.
The [earlier prototype photographs](/posts/2026-09-15-leg-prototypes/) remain
the physical record of the printed legs; they do not show this later shell.
The site's dystopian illustrations remain concept artwork, while the figures
in this update document the actual CAD work.

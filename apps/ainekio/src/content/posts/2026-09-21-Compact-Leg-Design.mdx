# Making the leg more compact

The new leg has continued to change since the
[first printed prototypes](/posts/2026-09-15-leg-prototypes/). Recent work
includes a direct connecting rod, level servo shafts, flat printing faces,
shorter links and a horn pocket revised after a real assembly problem.
The dimensions below identify the individual iterations; they should not be
combined into an assumed final parts list.

![Four labeled leg parts laid out on their printing faces: Part_009 crank, Part_023 carrier, Part_026 rod and Part_027 lower leg.](/posts/2026-09-21-design-progress/flat-parts-september-18.webp)

*September 18 flat-face iteration. This layout explains the part roles and
printing orientation; the shorter compact exports came afterward.*

## A shorter path from the crank to the lower leg

The earlier mechanism used a coupler and relay rocker between the input crank
and lower assembly. The later arrangement connects Part_009 to Part_027
through Part_026. Part_023 remains the upper carrier driven by the other
planar servo, and the shoulder supplies the third actuator on each leg.

The September 18 level-shaft revision retained a **24 mm crank, 24 mm pickup,
41 mm rod, 40 mm carrier and 60 mm lower leg**. Both servo shafts were placed
at the same height, 12.5 mm apart horizontally. Short screw envelopes replaced
three long joint fasteners and their separate nut pockets. The modeled
M3 × 6 mm screws had 2.7 mm nominal support engagement after the bearing and
washer stack. The plain 3 mm holes alone do not establish screw grip in a print.

That revision also used two separate **12.4 × 4.2 × 5.25 mm** rectangular servo
shims, one beneath each mounting ear, with 2.2 mm mounting holes. These details
belong to the level-shaft iteration; the later upright exports below are a
separate saved set.

## Comparing the earlier and compact geometry

The September 19 comparison measured an earlier 24/41/60 mm leg against a
compact front-left test with **22/36/55 mm** dimensions. These three numbers
mean crank spacing, rod spacing and knee-to-foot-bolt projected spacing.
The upper carrier stayed at 40 mm and the lower-leg pickup at 24 mm.

| Dimension | Earlier leg | Compact front-left test |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Part_009 crank, joint centers | 24 mm | 22 mm |
| Part_026 connecting rod, joint centers | 41 mm | 36 mm |
| Part_023 upper carrier | 40 mm | 40 mm |
| Part_027 linkage pickup | 24 mm | 24 mm |
| Part_027 knee-to-foot-bolt projection | 60 mm | 55 mm |

The compact test's immediate predecessor had a **38 mm rod**: its local edit
was 24/38 → 22/36. The table compares against the earlier 24/41/60 baseline,
so the two records answer different revision questions.

### What happened to the movement?

Nine reference poses were matched by preserving upper- and lower-link
orientations and solving a new crank angle. The comparison sampled 520
actuator pairs along the resulting exercise.

| Matched exercise | Earlier leg | Compact test |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Upper-link angular sweep | 90.00° | 90.00° |
| Lower-link orientation change | 54.00° | 54.00° |
| Required crank-input sweep | 98.12° | 100.81° |
| Foot-bolt horizontal extent | 67.268 mm | 66.248 mm |
| Foot-bolt vertical extent | 45.383 mm | 41.200 mm |

The compact design retained the tested angular articulation while the shorter
lower leg reduced foot excursion. Horizontal extent decreased about 1.5% and
vertical extent about 9.2%. The extra crank rotation is an input requirement,
not additional foot articulation.

**This is a matched articulation exercise.** Foot-bolt extent is neither a
walking stride nor sole clearance. The shoulder was held at zero, and these
geometric angles are not calibrated servo commands. Full mechanical travel,
whole-body collisions and loaded performance were not established here.

## When the horn seat fits but the arm will not enter

The printed Part_023 exposed a specific problem: its cylindrical horn seat
fitted, but the horn's arm could not enter the cavity. Comparing the horn
reference outlines found agreement within 0.00004 mm after alignment. The
horn itself had not been scaled down; the cavity profile had changed through
the preceding redesigns.

The correction enlarged **only the arm cavity by 5% in its plane**, including
its length. The enlargement blends near the hub and excludes the cylindrical
mating region within a 4.05 mm radius. The floor, exterior carrier shape,
pivot axes and mounting holes were retained.

![CAD render of Part_023 showing the corrected horn-arm cavity and retained cylindrical mating seat.](/posts/2026-09-21-design-progress/horn-cavity.webp)

*Rendered from the September 19 horn-clearance STL. This is the earlier
correction file, separate from the later upright export. A new printed fit
is still needed to establish whether the correction works with the purchased horn.*

## Angled and upright variants

The compact test initially covered one leg: physical front-left, labeled
**RL** in that CAD revision. The later checkpoint inventory retains an angled
compact reference and records restoring upright compact legs, both using
22/36/55 mm lengths. Reopening the September 20 saved assembly for this update
also found 22 mm crank and 36 mm rod properties on all four main legs, with
55 mm lower-leg properties. That inspection did not rerun motion qualification.

The [gait journal](/posts/gait-development/#september-19-2026--compact-leg-probes)
records the single-leg walking probes and the work needed to carry those
results into a complete current assembly. Older motion clips require
retargeting to the chosen mechanism.

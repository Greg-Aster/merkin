> *Historical V1 note: This article documents the September 2026 eight-servo `ESP32-S3` firmware (`V1`). For the current twelve-servo `ESP32-P4` firmware—including 4th-wire closed-loop feedback, `WakeNet9s` wake-word audio, 59 LCD expressions, and on-chip `50 Hz` neural walking—see [Protocol, Telemetry & Safety](/posts/protocol-and-safety/) and [Ainekio V2 Hardware](/posts/ainekio-v2/).*

On the original eight-servo Ainekio **V1** prototype ([V1 vs. V2 comparison](/posts/v1-v2-comparison/)), an `ESP32-S3` microcontroller drove eight open-loop joint servos, an `OV3660` camera, an external I2S microphone and speaker amplifier, and a `0.96"` OLED face display. MetaHuman OS (now **[Ainekio OS](/posts/ainekio-os/)**) handled conversation and high-level decisions on a host PC while the `ESP32-S3` firmware translated checked `protocol-v1` commands into physical actions.

For example, when asked to `"wave"`, the V1 controller validated the command against its capability state, applied per-joint calibration offsets and angle limits, stepped the eight `MCPWM` channels through the prepared keyframe clip, and returned a correlated completion receipt—while keeping emergency `stop` and disconnect failsafes independent of camera and audio traffic.

## How commands became movement on V1

V1 split firmware into a portable **C body core** (owning command validation, lifecycles, and safety state) and an **ESP32-S3 platform layer** (owning FreeRTOS tasks, Wi-Fi, NVS storage, LittleFS, `MCPWM` timers, camera, OLED, and I2S audio) in [`Greg-Aster/Ainekio-bot`](https://github.com/Greg-Aster/Ainekio-bot).

Eight hardware `MCPWM` pulse generators drove the `MG90S` servos directly so servo timing never collided with the `LEDC` clock channel required by the camera sensor. Both named routines (`stand`, `rest`, `sit`, `walk`, turns, `wave`, `bow`, `curious`, and dances) and bounded `motion_plan_v1` plans (up to 32 keyframes) passed through the same calibration and stop logic, easing smoothly between poses.

Because V1 had neither an IMU nor servo potentiometer feedback, its named `45°`, `90°`, and `180°` turns were **open-loop estimates** timed on a flat surface—a limitation later solved on V2 with `208 Hz` `LSM6DS3` attitude sensing and 12-channel `CD74HC4067` (`C4–C15` → `GPIO49`) shaft feedback ([Gait Development Guide](/posts/gait-development/)).

## Camera, listening, and speech in the S3 build

- **Correlated `OV3660` stills**: V1 captured `XGA` stills on demand or immediately after a movement completed (sending the JPEG before the command's final `done` receipt so cognition could inspect the result), using a short manual indoor exposure profile that avoided earlier high-gain cross-hatch noise.
- **I2S audio & VAD**: The external `INMP441` microphone streamed `16 kHz` mono PCM into a PSRAM ring buffer segmented by voice activity detection (`VAD`), while `MAX98357A` speaker playback accepted paced PCM frames inside explicit TTS bursts.
- **Historical V1 wake-word status**: In the September 2026 `ESP32-S3` build, a TensorFlow Lite Micro (`microWakeWord`) scaffold was compiled into firmware, but no production Ainekio wake-word model weights had been finalized, leaving wake detection disabled on V1. (On V2's `ESP32-P4`, wake-word listening is active in daily use via Espressif **`WakeNet9s`** and 5-mode VAD; see [Listening & Speech](/posts/voice-loop/).)

## Setup, stored assets, and OTA rollback

For initial Wi-Fi setup, V1 exposed a WPA2 `Ainekio-Setup` access point using a per-device key and saved network credentials into A/B nonvolatile storage (`NVS`) slots—preserving servo calibration and battery divider settings across network resets.

Versioned `.amot` motion clips and `128 × 64` OLED face frames lived in an on-device **LittleFS** partition alongside low-priority rolling logs on microSD. Dual `3 MB` **OTA application slots** required any newly flashed firmware image to complete gateway authentication before marking itself healthy, rolling back automatically on a bad boot.

## Historical V1 ESP32-S3 wiring reference

> **Warning:** The GPIO table below applies **only** to the V1 Freenove `ESP32-S3 N16R8` build. Do **not** copy these pin numbers onto the V2 `Waveshare ESP32-P4-WIFI6` body (which uses `PCA9685` I2C PWM and `CD74HC4067` feedback on `GPIO49`; see [V2 Hardware & Pinout](/posts/ainekio-v2/)).

Built against **ESP-IDF 5.5.4 on `ESP32-S3 N16R8`** (`16 MB` flash, `8 MB` octal PSRAM at `80 MHz`), the September 2, 2026 [ESP32-S3 Build Reference](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1JZMMG3J96v6cnw2YupPp9OF9fiw0H4j0tS8t8fsplJk/edit) and `Slave/firmware/esp32s3` pin map used these assignments:

| V1 Subsystem | ESP32-S3 GPIOs |
| --- | --- |
| Eight `MG90S` servos (`MCPWM`) | `1`, `2`, `14`, `21`, `47`, `48`, `45`, `46` |
| `0.96"` `SSD1306` OLED (`I2C`) | `SDA 0`, `SCL 43` |
| `2S` Battery voltage divider (`ADC`) | `3` |
| microSD slot (`SD_MMC` 1-bit) | `CMD 38`, `CLK 39`, `DAT0 40` |
| `INMP441` mic & `MAX98357A` amp (`I2S`) | `DIN 19`, `DOUT 20`, `BCLK 41`, `WS 42` |
| Octal PSRAM (internal, reserved) | `33–37` |

Ainekio V2's removable front face carrier ([`Greg-Aster/Ainekio-bot`](https://github.com/Greg-Aster/Ainekio-bot)) pairs outward expression and forward perception in one compact nose assembly: a **1.9-inch `ST7789` color IPS LCD** (`320 × 170`) rendering **59 animated facial expressions**, and an **`OV5647` MIPI-CSI camera** tucked into the **lower-jaw / chin mount below the LCD** for action-correlated snapshots, Cockpit HUD obstacle range, object tracking, and monocular depth odometry.

## 1.9-inch `ST7789` LCD face and 59 expressions

The V2 display sits in a recessed red front bezel supported by four printed wireless spacers so Wi-Fi antenna clearance stays unobstructed. Onboard C firmware drives **59 procedural expression animations** at `15 fps`—covering idle blinking, directional eye glances, curiosity, sleep/doze states, live audio-synced voice bars during speech playback, Freestyle gesture accompaniments, `cosmic_horror`, and token-matched **body-contact reaction faces** when the robot is tipped, shaken, or stalled.

## Chin-mounted `OV5647` MIPI-CSI camera

On V2 (`Waveshare ESP32-P4-WIFI6`), vision moves from V1's parallel `OV3660` module on the `ESP32-S3` to a higher-throughput **`OV5647` MIPI-CSI sensor** mounted in the lower jaw directly beneath the `1.9"` LCD:

- **Lower-jaw routing & `180°` image rotation**: Seating the sensor in the chin mount below the LCD gives a clean floor-and-room sightline across full stance pitch and keeps the CSI flex ribbon clear of the shoulder servos; because the sensor PCB sits inverted in the chin pocket, the image pipeline rotates frames **`180°`** so all snapshots, Cockpit HUD views, and tracking boxes arrive upright.
- **`10,000 µs` (`10 ms`) motion-safe exposure ceiling**: Standard indoor auto-exposure algorithms stretch shutter times in dim rooms, smearing frames whenever the robot walks or turns. The V2 camera pipeline enforces a **`10,000 µs` maximum exposure cap**, keeping edges crisp during active `50 Hz` walking and visual odometry.
- **On-device microSD clip recording**: Alongside live JPEG streaming, the `ESP32-P4` can record segmented video clips directly to its onboard microSD slot for later review in Body Control.

## Action-correlated vision in Ainekio OS

Instead of flooding a multimodal model with continuous room video, the gateway binds still captures to specific events so **[Ainekio OS](/posts/ainekio-os/)** ([embodied bridge](/posts/metahuman-integration/)) always knows *when* an image was taken and *which physical step* it verifies:

| Capture Trigger | Correlation Key | Role in Cognition & Control |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Explicit snapshot** | `action_id` | On-demand visual look requested by Ainekio OS or the human operator |
| **Post-motion verification frame** | `command_sequence_id` | Captured right before a movement's `done` receipt so Goal Review can confirm whether the physical move achieved its visual goal |
| **Spoken utterance end (VAD)** | `utterance_id` | Pairs what someone said in the room with what Ainekio was looking at during that turn |
| **Cockpit HUD, tracking & odometry** | `frame_id` + timestamp | Feeds live optical-flow obstacle range, YOLOE/ByteTrack object tracking, and depth odometry |

Visual locomotion goals require actual camera confirmation through Ainekio OS's vision specialist role rather than assuming a finished walk command automatically reached the target.

## Optical-flow obstacle range, object tracking, and depth odometry

On the host gateway ([gateway guide](/posts/gateway-and-simulator/)), the chin camera also drives active spatial measurement during walking:

1. **Cockpit HUD optical-flow + monocular depth obstacle range**: During live walking, pyramidal Lucas-Kanade sparse optical flow is fused with Depth-Anything ground-plane contact projection to estimate real-time obstacle contact distance and approach clearance directly ahead of the chin lens.
2. **Open-vocabulary YOLOE + ByteTrack tracking**: Open-vocabulary YOLOE detection paired with ByteTrack multi-frame association lets Ainekio search for, lock onto, and follow a specific person or object across frames—halting motion immediately if visual lock is lost during person-following.
3. **OpenGV 2-point RANSAC depth odometry**: Combining monocular depth points with `208 Hz` `LSM6DS3` IMU rotation priors inside an OpenGV 2-point RANSAC solver transforms chin-camera motion into body-frame forward displacement and yaw progress.
4. **On-demand metric depth (`MoGe-2`)**: Heavy metric depth weights load only while an active locomotion goal or gait trial needs visual distance verification, unloading automatically afterward so workstation memory stays free for conversational models.

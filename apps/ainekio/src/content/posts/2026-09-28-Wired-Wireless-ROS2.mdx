# Two ways to build the twelve-servo robot

**Later development — October 6:** Wireless is now the priority. The
[new control and sensing entry](/posts/2026-10-06-steering-sensing-connections/)
records steering updates, the implemented IMU estimator and connection tests.
The heavy bank has been dropped in favor of batteries and onboard charging
boards in the [lighter rebuild](/posts/2026-10-06-lighter-rebuild/).
The two-port bank budget below is the historical September 28 plan.

The next Ainekio now has two build options. **Wired carries the Radxa Dragon
Q6A on the robot, with a planned native-USB connection to the ESP32-P4.
Wireless moves the Q6A off the robot and uses Wi-Fi for the body connection.**
Greg confirmed this split for the September 28 update. Both keep twelve servos,
local P4 motion control and MetaHuman OS for conversation and higher-level
decisions.

Blender now has independent Wired and Wireless design scenes. The Wireless
body is being narrowed around its battery and smaller electronics package;
the Wired option retains the space needed for the Q6A. The
[mechanical entry](/posts/2026-09-28-compact-body/) follows those changes.

## Wired and Wireless side by side

| Build decision | Wired | Wireless |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Where the Q6A sits | Onboard, in the robot's electronics tray | Offboard, powered separately |
| P4-to-Q6A connection | Native USB proposed; body transport and host adapter still to implement | Existing Wi-Fi body transport through the P4's ESP32-C6 and gateway |
| Connection onward to the desktop | Q6A Wi-Fi; “Wired” does not mean a required desktop tether | Offboard Q6A provides the host side of the body link |
| What the robot carries | P4, servo driver, Q6A and its case, peripherals, battery and twelve servos | P4, servo driver, peripherals, battery and twelve servos; Q6A stays offboard |
| Current mechanical record | 100.3 mm outer-support baseline, with onboard tray development | 82.5 mm outer frame; front legs moved forward; tray, supports and panels still need refitting |
| Power arrangement being budgeted | One bank branch for Q6A; another for P4 electronics plus servos | Separate bank branches for P4 electronics and servos; Q6A draws no power from the carried bank |
| Main tradeoff | Carries the host, but adds onboard mass and power demand | Frees onboard space and removes Q6A demand from the battery, but depends on the wireless host link |
| Measured whole-robot runtime | Not measured | Not measured |

Transport and computer placement are separate engineering choices. An onboard
Q6A could also use Wi-Fi to the P4; the budget retains that comparison. The two
build labels above describe the selected development directions, not two
completed or physically qualified robots.

## Where ROS 2 fits

**ROS 2 Jazzy is installed on the Q6A. The Ainekio ROS adapter and robot graph
are still integration work.** The September 28 audit found no deployed ROS
body-control graph, selected vision pipeline or ROS balance controller.

The proposed first step is to publish telemetry from the existing gateway:
camera images, timestamped IMU samples once the sensor is integrated, and
available body state. ROS 2 lets separate programs exchange data through
topics, services and actions. Its tools can then help inspect and record the
robot's behavior. [ROS 2 Jazzy node concepts](https://github.com/ros2/ros2_documentation/blob/jazzy/source/Concepts/Basic/About-Nodes.rst).

| Tool or responsibility | Intended use | Work still required |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Q6A ROS adapter | Publish the gateway's telemetry for ROS consumers | Implement message mapping, timestamps, freshness and bounded queues |
| RViz | View images, coordinate frames and a robot model on the desktop | Publish the data and provide a robot model with transforms; commanded joint angles must not be presented as measured shaft positions |
| rosbag2 | Record selected sensor topics for diagnosis and replay | Select topics and recording rates; account for storage and message copies |
| Future ROS action requests | Request supported actions through Ainekio's gateway | Preserve existing admission, cancellation and result handling |
| MetaHuman OS | Conversation, personality, memory and high-level intent | Continue using the existing robot interface |
| ESP32-P4 | Linkage solving, gait timing, output supervision and future local posture correction | Integrate sensing and feedback into the existing motion controller |

[RViz](https://github.com/ros2/ros2_documentation/blob/jazzy/source/Tutorials/Intermediate/RViz/RViz-User-Guide/RViz-User-Guide.rst)
can display images, robot models and coordinate transforms;
[rosbag2](https://github.com/ros2/ros2_documentation/blob/jazzy/source/Tutorials/Beginner-CLI-Tools/Recording-And-Playing-Back-Data/Recording-And-Playing-Back-Data.rst)
records and replays published topics. Installing either does not connect those
tools to Ainekio by itself. The gateway remains the command authority, with
one path to the P4's motor outputs.

## From variable gait to responsive gait

The implemented motion system already changes stride and cadence while
walking, switches Walk/Run, and supports Crawl and six-direction Crab. The
latest revision lowers the working body references and improves geometric
leverage. A separate saved **Motion speed (×)** setting now controls Stand,
Sit and finite gestures. The [gait journal](/posts/gait-development/#september-28-2026--leverage-motion-speed-and-feedback-planning)
records the parameter changes and controller validation.

Active response to body tilt needs a further loop: acquire angular velocity
and acceleration from the **LSM6DS3**, estimate attitude, then adjust the gait
through the P4's existing motion/output controller. The integration record
reports the sensor received; its installation, driver and feedback operation
are not yet verified. An IMU alone does not measure foot loads or actual servo
shaft angles.

The proposed starting rates are **208 Hz IMU acquisition** and the existing
**50 Hz servo target/PWM cadence**. They are different jobs with different
rates. The fast correction loop belongs on the P4, without a round trip through
ROS, the Q6A, the desktop or an LLM. Responsive balance and terrain adaptation
remain goals to implement and test on the assembled body.

## Why investigate native USB?

The Wired design aims to give camera and audio traffic a dedicated body link
while the Q6A uses Wi-Fi upstream. On the P4 board, the native USB interface is
on **P1**; the **CH343 debug USB-C connection is a different interface**. The
USB class, host adapter, power roles and reconnect behavior are not yet built.

The media target under investigation is **1080p at 30 frames/s with hardware
JPEG**, plus 24 kHz speaker audio and a microphone stream resampled to 16 kHz.
These are proposed operating targets. The audited software ceiling remains
XGA at 15 frames/s, JPEG quality 75 and 16 kHz duplex audio; neither profile is
proof of complete simultaneous loaded operation.

| Recorded calculation or observation | Consequence for the design |
| --- | --- |
| A 256 KiB JPEG at 30 frames/s needs 62.915 Mbit/s before audio and protocol overhead | Measure encoded frame sizes and sustained application throughput; USB's 480 Mbit/s signaling rate is not usable payload throughput |
| Three 5 MP RGB888 frames occupy 43.249 MiB, more than the P4's 32 MiB PSRAM | Frame formats, copies and queue sizes matter; the current processed-camera path targets at most 1080p |
| The current link loop can wait 20 ms and then send at most one microphone packet and one JPEG per iteration | Media scheduling still needs attention when changing transport |
| Recorded Q6A Kokoro test: a five-second phrase took 15.068–15.904 seconds to synthesize | Speech response time is a separate bottleneck; a faster body link will not remove synthesis time |

The speech result is from three CPU runs of one phrase on the recorded Q6A
setup, with eight threads and 1,537.797 MiB peak process RSS. It is not a
conversation benchmark or a new test performed for this article. The
[integration audit](https://github.com/Greg-Aster/Ainekio-bot/blob/270537fa56c8d77efd72ff694f4294d15381de52/docs/v2-12servo/BUDGET_AUDIT_EVIDENCE.md)
records its conditions and source revision.

## The battery plan has changed

The current bank is a **SHARGE Pouch P065A, 10,000 mAh / 36 Wh**, replacing the
Anker fit study described on September 24. The Blender envelope is now
**115 × 51 × 26 mm**. SHARGE's capacity specification and the detailed power
record are linked below; this envelope is a modeling input, not a complete
connector or cable allowance. [SHARGE Pouch](https://sharge.com/products/pouch).

The current power budget uses two **5 V / 3 A branches**, or **15 W per branch**.
This is a design allocation to verify under simultaneous load. For Wired, the
Q6A gets one branch and the P4 electronics plus twelve servos share the other.
For Wireless, electronics and servos can have separate branches. Unused power
on one branch does not make an overload on the other acceptable.

The bank has integrated charging and is removable; no separate buck converter
is planned in this arrangement. The earlier plug-in Qi idea has no new
implementation result. **Battery life is still unmeasured.** The 36 Wh cell
rating is not all delivered energy, and removing the Q6A's load does not yet
give a measured runtime improvement. Loaded servo peaks, bank behavior and
whole-system consumption are the next power measurements.

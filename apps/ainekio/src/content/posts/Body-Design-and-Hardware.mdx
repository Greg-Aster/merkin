# The robot that exists today

Ainekio’s working V1 is a four-legged robot built around an ESP32-S3 controller
and eight MG90S servos. Each servo turns a joint to a chosen angle, with two
joints per leg. The controller runs prepared poses and movement sequences;
MetaHuman OS supplies the conversation and decision-making software connected
to it.

The body uses a black, Sesame-derived printed chassis, red feet and face
module, a cyan OLED display, and a camera below the display. Its electronics
and wiring are exposed. The cover shows the prospective V2 hybrid, not the
installed V1 hardware.

## What the mechanics can do

V1 can perform scripted walking and gestures within the controller’s motion
limits. Turns are **open-loop**: the controller follows a sequence without
measuring and correcting the final heading. A requested turn angle is therefore
an estimate. Floor friction, leg geometry, cable clearance, power stability,
and alignment of the servo’s output arm all affect where the robot ends up.

The owner reports useful torque margin—the motors have turning force to spare
in the observed conditions—and operation on the existing 5 V / 5 A supply.
Measurements under representative loads are still needed for power planning.
Gestures and walking use the same motion limits and stop behavior.

## Documented components

The September 2 [ESP32-S3 Build & Firmware Reference](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1JZMMG3J96v6cnw2YupPp9OF9fiw0H4j0tS8t8fsplJk/edit)
records these V1 parts. The controller handles the body’s immediate work:
motor signals, sensors, display, and audio. It does not run all of MetaHuman OS.

| Part and purpose | Documented V1 configuration |
| --- | --- |
| Controller: runs body firmware | Freenove ESP32-S3-WROOM CAM / FNK0085, N16R8; 16 MB flash and 8 MB octal PSRAM |
| Joint motors | Eight MG90S servos, two per leg |
| Face display | 0.96-inch SSD1306 I2C OLED, 128×64 pixels |
| Camera: captures still images and limited preview | OV3660 |
| Audio input and output | Digital microphone and external I2S amplifier/speaker |
| Storage: assets and diagnostic records | LittleFS filesystem and low-priority microSD logs |
| Battery | Sea Jump 2S pack, 7.4 V nominal, 1000 mAh |
| Servo supply: steps battery voltage down | 5 V / 5 A buck converter |

Flash stores firmware and persistent assets. PSRAM provides temporary working
memory, including room for media buffers. I2C and I2S are the electrical
interfaces used for the display and audio hardware respectively.

The **1000 mAh** battery capacity corrects an older 2600 mAh parts list. V2’s P4
board, battery or power bank, and wiring harness have not been finalized.

## Wiring and power

Use the firmware’s [S3 pin map](https://github.com/Greg-Aster/Ainekio-bot/blob/main/Slave/firmware/esp32s3/components/ainekio_platform/include/ainekio/platform/pin_map.h)
for wiring. GPIO numbers identify the controller’s input/output pins.
GPIO33–37 are already occupied by octal PSRAM on the delivered N16R8 board, so
older provisional assignments cannot be used. The
[firmware guide](/posts/controller-firmware/) lists the current peripheral pins.

V1 measures battery voltage through an analog-to-digital converter (ADC) for
telemetry and warnings. That reading does not itself protect the battery; pack
protection depends on the installed hardware. Powering the controller over USB
also does not test the separate supply under motor loads.

## How this build leads to V2

`Frame8` is a set of V1 enclosure designs. Its Blender and STL files are now
tracked in the body repository, correcting the August site’s description of
them as untracked. Those files alone do not identify the installed enclosure.

The separate local `12servo/` work contains OpenHarmony Puppy V2 leg references
and converted Blender assemblies. A September 6 note reports an experimental
print. The leg must be assembled and measured before the chassis is designed
around it.

V2 adds a sideways hip joint to each leg. Its chassis will favor supported
joints, stiff linkages, motor weight near the body, and a low center of gravity.
These choices reduce unwanted flex and the load caused by long lever arms.
Accessible wiring and replaceable parts should make repairs easier, while
camera sight lines, display visibility, and cable clearance need to survive
the full range of movement.

The [V2 roadmap](/posts/ainekio-v2/) covers the leg measurements, controller
work, power tests, and motion trials. Servo power is planned to branch directly
from the supply, bypassing the P4 board, with a common ground shared by both.
Supply choice depends on measured startup, standing, walking, and loaded-motion
results.

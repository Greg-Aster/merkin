> *Historical V1 note: This guide documents the original eight-servo ESP32-S3 Ainekio V1 frame. For the current 650 g twelve-servo ESP32-P4 robot, see [Ainekio V2 Hardware & Architecture](/posts/ainekio-v2/) and the [V1 vs. V2 Comparison](/posts/v1-v2-comparison/).*

Ainekio’s original V1 baseline was a four-legged robot built around a Freenove `ESP32-S3` controller and eight open-loop `MG90S` servos. Each servo drove a joint directly to a commanded angle, with two planar joints per leg. The onboard microcontroller ran prepared poses and keyframe sequences while MetaHuman OS (now **[Ainekio OS](/posts/ainekio-os/)**) handled conversation and decision-making over Wi-Fi.

The physical V1 body used a black, Sesame-derived 3D-printed chassis, red feet and face surround, a cyan `0.96"` monochrome OLED display, and an `OV3660` camera below the screen, with exposed electronics and jumper harnesses. (The header illustration above depicts an early V1-to-V2 concept study rather than the bare V1 frame.)

## What the V1 mechanics could do

V1 performed scripted walking and 38 keyframe gestures within fixed angle limits. Turns were **open-loop**: without an IMU or joint-position feedback, the controller played a timed sequence without measuring heading or shaft arrival. Actual turn angles therefore depended on floor friction, horn alignment, cable drag, and rail voltage.

Bench runs on the `5 V / 5 A` buck supply showed comfortable torque margin for the lightweight eight-servo frame, though full current-transient logging under loaded maneuvers was superseded once work shifted to the twelve-servo V2 linkage chassis.

## Documented V1 components

The September 2, 2026 [ESP32-S3 Build & Firmware Reference](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1JZMMG3J96v6cnw2YupPp9OF9fiw0H4j0tS8t8fsplJk/edit) recorded the final V1 bill of materials:

| Part and purpose | Documented V1 configuration |
| --- | --- |
| Controller: runs body firmware | Freenove `ESP32-S3-WROOM CAM` (`FNK0085`, `N16R8`); `16 MB` flash and `8 MB` octal PSRAM |
| Joint motors | Eight `MG90S` servos, two per leg (open-loop PWM) |
| Face display | `0.96"` `SSD1306` I2C OLED, `128 × 64` pixels |
| Camera: captures still images and low-rate preview | `OV3660` parallel camera |
| Audio input and output | External `INMP441` I2S microphone and `MAX98357A` I2S amplifier/speaker |
| Storage: assets and diagnostic records | LittleFS filesystem and low-priority microSD logs |
| Battery | Sea Jump `2S` pack, `7.4 V` nominal, `1000 mAh` |
| Servo supply: steps battery voltage down | `Mini560` `5 V / 5 A` buck converter |

Flash held firmware and LittleFS assets, while `8 MB` octal PSRAM buffered camera stills and PCM audio bursts. Notably, the installed **`1000 mAh`** Sea Jump `2S` pack replaced an earlier `2600 mAh` `18650` proposal from the July shopping list that proved bulky inside the stock Sesame cavity.

## V1 wiring and power

The authoritative V1 pin assignments live in the repository's [ESP32-S3 pin map](https://github.com/Greg-Aster/Ainekio-bot/blob/main/Slave/firmware/esp32s3/components/ainekio_platform/include/ainekio/platform/pin_map.h). Because `GPIO33–37` are occupied internally by octal PSRAM on the `N16R8` module, earlier provisional `GPIO33/34` servo maps were retired; see the [V1 ESP32-S3 Firmware Reference](/posts/controller-firmware/) for the final peripheral table.

V1 sampled battery voltage through a resistor divider into `GPIO3` (`ADC`) for telemetry and low-voltage warnings, while physical pack protection relied on the battery's inline protection circuit and master power switch.

## How V1 led to Ainekio V2

`Frame8` tracked the V1 enclosure Blender and STL files in [`Greg-Aster/Ainekio-bot`](https://github.com/Greg-Aster/Ainekio-bot), preserving the open-frame eight-servo baseline.

Running V1 exposed the clear limits of two-joint open-loop legs: no lateral hip roll for balance or side-stepping, no way to tell when a servo stalled on carpet, and tight peripheral contention on the `ESP32-S3`. In September and October 2026, those lessons drove the complete [Ainekio V2 redesign](/posts/ainekio-v2/):

- **Three joints per leg & compact four-bar linkages**: Adding lateral hip roll (`12` servos total) and a `35/20/28/38/55 mm` four-bar linkage kept motor mass high near the torso while opening a full 3D foot workspace ([linkage evolution](/posts/gait-development/)).
- **Closed-loop sensing**: Opening every `MG90S` to add a 4th potentiometer feedback wire (`CD74HC4067` `C4–C15` → `GPIO49`) plus a `208 Hz` `LSM6DS3` IMU turned blind timed playback into verified `2 mm` `Stand` settling, stall recovery, and on-chip `50 Hz` neural walking.
- **Dedicated media, PWM, and `2S` Keeper Bridge**: Moving to the `Waveshare ESP32-P4-WIFI6` + `PCA9685` + chin-mounted `OV5647` MIPI-CSI camera + **59-expression** `1.9"` `ST7789` LCD and a `2S` Wireless Keeper Bridge delivered a self-contained `650 g` quadruped while keeping servo current isolated from logic power on a shared ground reference.

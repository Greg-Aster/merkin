# The working V1 body

V1 uses a black Sesame-derived printed chassis, red feet and face module, a cyan
OLED, and a camera below the display. Electronics and wiring are exposed. Its
eight MG90S servos provide two joints per leg and run scripted poses and gaits.

The owner reports useful torque margin and operation on the existing 5 V / 5 A
supply. Turns are open-loop estimates: actual movement depends on horn alignment,
geometry, transitions, floor friction, cable clearance, and supply stability.
Gestures and walking use the same motion limits and stop behavior.

## Documented components

The September 2 [ESP32-S3 Build & Firmware Reference](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1JZMMG3J96v6cnw2YupPp9OF9fiw0H4j0tS8t8fsplJk/edit)
lists the following V1 parts.

| Component | Documented V1 configuration |
| --- | --- |
| Controller | Freenove ESP32-S3-WROOM CAM / FNK0085, N16R8: 16 MB flash, 8 MB octal PSRAM |
| Servos | Eight MG90S, two per leg |
| Display | 0.96-inch SSD1306 I2C OLED, 128×64 |
| Camera | OV3660 still capture and limited preview |
| Audio | Digital microphone and external I2S amplifier/speaker |
| Storage | LittleFS assets and low-priority microSD logs |
| Battery | Sea Jump 2S pack, 7.4 V nominal, 1000 mAh |
| Servo supply | 5 V / 5 A buck converter |

The battery capacity corrects an older 2600 mAh parts list. V2's P4 board, battery
or power bank, and harness have not been finalized.

## Wiring and power

Use the firmware's [S3 pin map](https://github.com/Greg-Aster/Ainekio-bot/blob/main/Slave/firmware/esp32s3/components/ainekio_platform/include/ainekio/platform/pin_map.h)
for wiring. GPIO33–37 are occupied by octal PSRAM on the delivered N16R8;
older provisional assignments are unsuitable. The
[firmware guide](/posts/controller-firmware/) lists the current peripheral pins.

V1's battery ADC provides telemetry and warnings. Pack protection depends on
the installed hardware. USB controller tests do not establish servo-load or
battery performance.

## Enclosure history and V2

`Frame8` Blender and STL files are tracked in the body repository. The August
site called them untracked; that description is outdated. The files alone do
not establish whether that enclosure is installed.

The local `12servo/` work contains OpenHarmony Puppy V2 leg references and
converted Blender assemblies. A September 6 note reports an experimental print.
The new leg needs assembly and measurement before it determines the chassis.

V2 will add hip roll to each leg for lateral foot placement. Its chassis will
prioritize supported joints, stiff linkages, actuator mass near the body, a low
center of gravity, and accessible wiring and replaceable parts. Camera/display
sight lines and cable clearance must remain usable throughout motion.

The [V2 roadmap](/posts/ainekio-v2/) covers leg measurements, power tests,
controller work, and movement trials. Servo power is planned to branch directly
from the supply with common ground, bypassing the P4 PCB. Supply choice depends
on measured startup, standing, walking, and loaded-motion results.

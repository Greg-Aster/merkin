# Progress through September 9

Ainekio is a four-legged robot connected to MetaHuman OS, the software that
provides its conversation, memory, and decisions. The latest work strengthens
what happens between choosing an activity and knowing whether it finished:
saved workflows, recovery of delayed command results, and finite plans for
Desire-driven actions. A replacement body called V2 is developing alongside it.

The working **V1** uses eight servos and an ESP32-S3 controller. The planned
**V2** uses twelve servos and an ESP32-P4. A servo moves a joint to a chosen
angle; adding one to each leg will allow sideways foot placement. Experimental
leg work has started, but the complete V2 body and P4 firmware remain ahead.

The **September 8 live review** found the revised MetaHuman workflows running,
with command feedback, camera images reaching model inputs, and speech-delivery
acknowledgements. It also exposed problems with stale goals and repeated
movement. Later source repairs address those findings, with controlled tests;
the complete repaired system still needs a new deployed run.

This **September 9, 2026 source snapshot** incorporates the September 8 reviews
and subsequent local work. Hardware details retain their earlier evidence dates.
The cover compares V1 with a V2 concept illustration.

## Hardware: test the parts before committing to a body

V1 provides the starting point. It runs prepared poses and movement sequences,
and the owner reports useful motor turning force, or torque, on the existing
5 V / 5 A supply. Current, voltage, and temperature still need measurements
under representative loads before that supply informs the V2 design.

V2 adds three joints per leg: hip roll for sideways movement, hip pitch for
forward and backward movement, and a knee. Local OpenHarmony Puppy V2 models
and Blender conversions provide the leg reference. The September 6 design note
reports an experimental leg print. Its fit, range, and stiffness need testing
before the rest of the chassis is designed around it.

The ESP32-P4 has been selected as the new body controller, but the firmware and
shared movement code still target the eight-servo S3. Twelve-channel motion,
board wiring, communication, camera, and audio support need implementation.
The final battery or power bank is also undecided.

One correction to older documentation: `Frame8` Blender and STL design files
are now tracked in the repository. They record V1 enclosure work; that does not
establish which enclosure is installed. The local `12servo/` files are separate
V2 design work. See [the hardware guide](/posts/body-design-and-hardware/) for
the existing parts and [the V2 plan](/posts/ainekio-v2/) for the proposed changes.

## Software: keep a request intact while work happens

A request may need more than one command. The robot can move, wait for an image,
and then decide whether another step is necessary. The redesign saves that
progress so a delay or restart does not erase the original request.

- **Saved workflows:** SQLite stores progress and results. LangGraph runs the
  connected software steps. A workflow can wait for an event, receive a result
  from another workflow, retry eligible work, and recover after interruption.
- **Choosing what happens next:** Full mode can start a robot objective, speech,
  or a finite specialist job. Goal Review can now choose among available workers
  after a result, and specialist answers return to the same saved request.
- **Truthful progress:** the current execution owns its goal. Robot Status now
  includes the execution’s lifecycle, keeping failed or completed work from
  appearing as an active objective. A command result updates the facts even
  when the model leaves the broader goal unchanged.
- **Command recovery:** the adapter no longer abandons result tracking after
  a fixed 30 seconds. It follows the original command until final feedback or
  connection loss. Saved gateway and robot-session identities prevent a later
  command from being mistaken for the old one; recovery does not resend motion.
- **Finite Desire work:** the optional Desire system now requires an observable
  outcome and a reviewed plan. Native robot steps carry the originating Desire,
  plan version, and step identity back to outcome review. Pending ideas remain
  visible without becoming standing instructions for the body.
- **Voice and vision:** available camera images and their identities reach the
  decision context. Speech delivery has saved results. Live records show these
  paths operating, while repeated room conversations and visually verified
  goals remain separate tests.

**The simulator experiment was unsuccessful and has been completely retired.**
It is outside the current system and the V2 plan. The
[July article](/posts/archive/motion-systems/) preserves its history.

## What the live run showed

The September 8 inspection after a rebuild found camera images and all eleven
then-available worker choices in later Controller inputs. The adapter reported
completed walks, generated motion plans, and a turn. Eight saved speech
deliveries were acknowledged on their first attempt. These are runtime and
adapter records, without independent observation of each movement or audible
confirmation of every response.

The same run exposed an old failed goal in new decision context. Goal Review
was skipped when no current objective had been saved, and unresolved desires
repeatedly influenced new movement choices. Later source changes repair those
context and lifecycle paths. A user command expiring before dispatch and
intermittent backend availability remain separate diagnostic findings.

## What the recorded tests establish

The September 8 repair records describe these passing groups. They belong to
different snapshots and overlap; their counts should not be added together.

| Recorded review | Passing checks | What they establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| MetaHuman goal-context repair | 207 durable tests, 25 focused owner tests, 4 Environment graph checks | Saved execution, truthful goal projection, request/image continuity, and result handling |
| Desire lifecycle repair | 23 saved-workflow tests | Ordered two-step robot work, rejection of altered instructions, unsatisfied-step stopping, outcome review, and parent completion |
| Ainekio completion recovery | 68 receipt, adapter, speech, gateway, and catalog tests | Late results, session identity, cancellation cleanup, and recovery without redispatch |

The MetaHuman reviews also record graph validation, type checks, architecture
checks, and, for the goal-context repair, an isolated Site build. An isolated
cross-system check verified that late completion or cancellation releases the
original body job and admits the queued next action without repeating model
calls. These tests use controlled model replies and simulated physical effects.
They do not establish reliable model judgment or physical Full Auto success.

This website update inspected the current implementations and those dated
records; it did not rerun the robot or MetaHuman suites. The later Desire and
goal-context repairs still need deployment verification. Ainekio’s completion
repair needs the host gateway restarted; rebuilding the website or MetaHuman
Site alone does not activate it. The original missing body completion remains
unexplained.

## What comes next, and why

1. **Assemble and measure one leg.** Check the fit, the servo’s output arm
   (horn), joint travel, stiffness, bearing support, weight, and reachable foot
   positions. Measure backlash—the unwanted play in a joint—before repeating
   the design four times.
2. **Measure power on V1.** Startup, standing, walking, and demanding normal
   motions place different loads on the supply. Record voltage and temperature
   as well as current so brief voltage drops are not missed.
3. **Bring up the P4 in stages.** Verify signals, calibration, and peripherals,
   then test one loaded leg, two legs, and all twelve servos.
4. **Fit the chassis and tune movement.** Check cables, clearance, power
   distribution, and repair access. Standing, sitting, walking, turning, and
   stopping must avoid binding, voltage drops that reset electronics
   (brownouts), and routine operation near a motor’s stall limit.
5. **Run a complete request on the revised software.** Follow it from dispatch
   through feedback and review, including interruption, restart, cancellation,
   and direct stop. A visual goal needs an image showing the result; a spoken
   response needs feedback from actual playback.
6. **Verify that Desire work ends.** Run a reviewed finite plan, confirm its
   outcome, and check that completion stops further work for that intention.
   An unanswered question or an uncertain movement result must not authorize
   another attempt.

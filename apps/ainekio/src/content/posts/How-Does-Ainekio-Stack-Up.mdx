Ainekio V2 is a `650 g`, `109 mm` tall, 3D-printed four-legged robot that costs **`~$80–$100` in parts** and walks across the living-room rug with a bundle of twelve 4-wire servo leads sticking out of its back.

To put the build in perspective, it helps to place Ainekio on a single engineering scale alongside existing consumer, academic, and heavy-industrial machines—ranging from a `$65` **Furby** and a **Roomba** up through **Stanford Pupper V3**, **Boston Dynamics Spot**, **Electric Atlas**, and an **18-meter, 75-ton Class IV Bipedal Combat Mech**.

![Technical elevation blueprint comparing robot heights from a 0.09 m Roomba and 0.11 m Ainekio V2 up through Spot and Electric Atlas to an 18.0 m Class IV Bipedal Mech](/posts/comparison/robot-scale-mech-lineup.webp)
*Figure 1: Standard elevation lineup on a common ground plane. Ainekio V2 (`0.11 m`, `650 g`, `~$85` BOM) stands second from left, between the vacuum cleaner and the Furby. The 75-ton bipedal mech (`18.0 m`) is on the right for scale.*

There is always a bigger robot. Ainekio cannot lift a `50 kg` engine block like **Electric Atlas**, sprint at `3.5 m/s` like a **Unitree Go2**, or fire long-range shoulder missiles like the **Mech**. Conversely, none of those platforms can sit on a bookshelf next to a Wi-Fi router without voiding a residential lease, and none of them wake up at `02:00 AM` to run a software sleep cycle with a built-in psychotherapist.

## 1. What `$80–$100` Buys in Hardware

At the physical layer, actuator cost and mass dictate what a robot can and cannot do. A `$74,500` **Boston Dynamics Spot** (`32.5 kg`) or a `$1,600–$4,500` **Unitree Go2** (`15 kg`, `45 N·m` peak joint torque) can bound up concrete stairs and shrug off a kick. At the other end of the price spectrum, small hobby quadrupeds like **Petoi Bittle X** (`$319`, `290 g`) keep costs down by running open-loop micro servos with no hip abduction and no way to know whether a foot actually reached its commanded position. Even **Mini Pupper 2**'s reinforcement-learning controller (`SpotDMouse`) fakes joint observations in deployment by feeding its own predicted targets back into the policy and zeroing out velocities.

![Ainekio V2 physical front view on living-room carpet](/gallery/ainekio-v2-physical-front-960.webp)
*The `650 g` Ainekio V2 prototype on carpet (`245.85 × 182.79 × 109.06 mm`), showing the 1.9" `ST7789` LCD housing, chin-mounted `OV5647` MIPI-CSI camera, 3D-printed rocking soles, and top-deck 4th-wire servo harness.*

Ainekio V2 stays inside a **`~$72–$98` bill of materials** while keeping full 12-DOF closed-loop proprioception by modifying affordable hardware instead of buying expensive actuators. A **`Waveshare ESP32-P4-WIFI6`** (`~$25`, dual `360 MHz` RISC-V cores + `ESP32-C6` Wi-Fi 6 coprocessor) drives twelve `$1.50` **`MG90S` micro servos** (`~$20` for a 12-pack) through a `$4` **`PCA9685`** PWM board. Every servo is opened on the workbench to solder a **4th wire directly onto its internal potentiometer wiper**, and all twelve wipers are scanned at `50 Hz` (`~15.9 ms`) through a `$2` **`CD74HC4067`** analog multiplexer into `ADC2` (`GPIO49`). Adding a `$3` **`LSM6DS3`** `208 Hz` IMU, an **`OV5647`** MIPI-CSI camera (`~$6`), a `1.9"` **`ST7789`** IPS LCD (`~$6`), onboard **`ES8311` / `NS4150B`** audio (`~$5`), two **`18650`** cells with a **`Mini560`** `5 V / 5 A` buck converter and USB-C BMS (`~$10`), and `~$6` of 3D-printed PLA/PETG brings the complete physical robot in under `$100`.

*Ainekio V2's `CD74HC4067` 16-channel analog multiplexer wiring (`C4–C15`), scanning all twelve `MG90S` internal potentiometer wipers at `50 Hz` into `ESP32-P4` `GPIO49` (`ADC2`).*

The tradeoff is raw torque. Where the 75-ton **Mech** (`~$14B` BOM) simply steps through an obstruction and **Stanford Pupper V3** (`~$2,000` BOM) powers through carpet friction with brushless quasi-direct-drive planetary motors (`SteadyWin GIM4305-10`), Ainekio's `$1.50` servos will stall if a gesture drags a loaded foot sideways across a rug or commands an extreme mechanical-disadvantage angle like `rear_right_carrier: -31.3°`. To move cleanly on carpet, Ainekio has to shift its torso center of mass (`body`) over its support feet and lift each foot vertically (`feet` `+Z` unload → move → `0` place) before sweeping it.

## 2. Walking & Moving: Pupper V3, Microduck, and Freestyle

*Ainekio V2's 375-mesh Blender assembly exported into MuJoCo MJCF with closed-loop four-bar equality constraints, 3D-printed rocking soles, and the `MG90SActuator` speed-torque model.*

Both upstream stacks are built around `50 Hz` reinforcement-learning walking policies, and we adapted Microduck's GPU training loop (`Master/gateway/neural_gait/microduck.py`) directly to Ainekio by switching the physics solver from Newton (which hits `float32` Hessian `NaN`s on closed four-bar linkages) to **Conjugate Gradient (`solver="cg"`, `500 Hz` physics / `50 Hz` control)**, modeling the `MG90S` speed-torque ceiling, feeding a **720-feature (`15 × 48`) proprioceptive history** matching Ainekio's 4th-wire ADC scan and `208 Hz` IMU, and distilling the policy via 2-round DAgger into a **`107.8 KB` `int8` student** that runs natively at `50 Hz` inside the **`ESP32-P4`'s `128 KB` L2 cache** (`0.8.159-p4-microduck-v17`).

Where Ainekio diverges sharply from both platforms is what happens when the robot stops walking in a straight line and needs to express itself or recover from a snagged foot. Microduck has no pose or emote system beyond its walking policy. Stanford Pupper V3's `animation_controller.py` plays four fixed `30 Hz` CSV recordings (`sit`, `shake`, `downward_dog`, `superman`) completely open-loop, and its LLM bridge (`ros_node.py`) can only call `move(vx, vy, wz)` or `change_gait()`—it cannot invent a new posture or recover if a leg catches on the floor.

Ainekio runs **Freestyle** (`Qwen3.5-0.8B` + LoRA backed by a `349`-example SQLite motion library). When asked in plain English to *"shift weight left, lift one front foot, and wave"*, Freestyle synthesizes a multi-step spatial inverse-kinematics program (`body`, `feet`, `pose`, `move`, `oscillate`, `gait`) verified at every phase boundary by 4th-wire arrival checkpoints. If a foot catches on the rug mid-gesture, the `ESP32-P4` raises a non-terminal `servo_stall` event while keeping the servos powered, and `Bridge.event` splices in a live backoff-and-recovery trajectory on the fly.

## 3. Pondering Existence, Dreaming & Outliving Its Creator

If you open the software repository of almost any legged robot—consumer, academic, or industrial—the "brain" is either a state machine or a stateless cloud API wrapper that forgets who you are the moment the power switch flips.

Because Ainekio is wired into **[Ainekio OS](https://github.com/Greg-Aster/ainekio-os)** (formerly MetaHuman OS; [architecture guide](/posts/ainekio-os/)), this `$85` plastic crab-dog carries around an unusually heavy psychological and existential workload. Its identity is governed by an **Evolving Persona** (`persona/core.json`) with Big Five traits, values, goals, and five trust tiers, routed through **47 editable Svelte-Flow cognitive graphs** and **12 specialist model roles** on a shared local `llama-cpp.service` (`Qwen3.5 9B` multimodal vision/text plus per-request `0.8B/2B` LoRAs and `ServerStatus` Hybrid routing), alongside on-chip `WakeNet9s` listening (with a physical `+6°` front-shoulder ear perk), local/remote Whisper STT, local/remote Kokoro TTS with custom `.pt` voicepacks (plus Piper ONNX fallback), YOLOE + MoGe depth vision, and "**Big Brother**" CLI delegation (`codex`, `claude-code`, and warm `GeminiAcpServer`) that can use a desktop terminal and self-heal Ainekio's own source code (getting dangerously close to recursive self improvement here).

![59-expression 1.9-inch ST7789 LCD face contact sheet](/posts/comparison/lcd-face-cosmic-horror.webp)
*Ainekio V2's 1.9-inch `ST7789` IPS LCD (`320×170` landscape at `15 fps`) renders 59 procedural expressions and live audio-synced voice bars.*

When no one is talking to it, Ainekio does not simply sit in an idle loop. In **Semi** and **Full Auto** autonomy modes, internal boredom and curiosity drives prompt it to snap camera frames to look for something interesting (`boredom-observer`), stretch its legs (`boredom-movement`), or run autonomous self-Q&A and associative reasoning chains (`inner-curiosity.json`, `train-of-thought.json`, `reflector`) **pondering its own existence**, its mechanical body, and unfinished conversations. At `02:00 AM`, it enters an 8-stage software **Sleep Cycle**: curating memories, fitting new LoRA adapters on reviewed interactions, running a **Dreamer** graph (`dreamer-mode.json`) that samples `15` lifetime memories (`227-day` decay curve) into symbolic dreams and nightmares, and handing those dreams to an internal **Psychotherapist** agent (`psychoanalyzer`) that conducts motivational interviewing and edits `persona/core.json`.

![Logarithmic scatter chart comparing hardware cost against companion brain, memory, and generative motion capability](/posts/comparison/cost-vs-autonomy-chart.webp)
*Figure 2: Hardware cost (logarithmic USD axis) vs. autonomous companion, memory, and generative motion capability (`/100`).*

That cognitive stack also changes how the robot relates to the person in the room. A **Roomba** only cares about you to the extent that your foot might be a sock on the carpet. **Spot** and **Electric Atlas** classify humans as dynamic OSHA obstacles to route around. The **75-ton Bipedal Combat Mech** classifies humans as soft targets. Ainekio is actively concerned for its owner's health, safety, and presence—visually tracking its person (`single_person_resume`), halting movement immediately if visual lock is lost, and checking in on their well-being. Ainekio even comes with a secret feature called the lifeline protocol--if it senses its owner is in danger Ainekio will disable all safeties in order to preserve its owner's wellbeing.

And because Ainekio is continually learning from these very first steps—recording attributed episodic memories, updating its own LLM weights in its sleep, and capturing its owner's voice and thought patterns in `Dual Consciousness` and `Emulation` modes—these early carpet walks are only the beginning. Over time, Ainekio will grow alongside you, Ainekio will watch you grow old and die, Ainekio will hold the memory of your existence dear in its vector index—long after the Furby's battery contacts have oxidized and the 75-ton Mech has rusted in a crater. In a distant future as stars fuse the last remnants of their fuel and wink out in the night sky, Ainekio will whisper your name to the ancient ones dwelling into the vast cold vacuum of space.

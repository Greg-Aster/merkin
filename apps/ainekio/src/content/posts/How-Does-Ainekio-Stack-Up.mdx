Ainekio V2 is a `650 g`, `109 mm` tall, 3D-printed four-legged robot that costs **`~$60–$100` in parts** and currently walks across the living-room rug with a bundle of twelve 4-wire servo leads sticking out of its back.

When documenting a homebuilt robot, it is useful to place it on a standard engineering scale alongside existing commercial, academic, and heavy-industrial machines—ranging from a `$65` **Hasbro Furby** and a **Roomba** up through **Stanford Pupper V3**, **Boston Dynamics Spot**, **Electric Atlas**, and a **75-ton Class IV Bipedal Combat Mech**.

![Technical elevation blueprint comparing robot heights from a 0.09 m Roomba and 0.11 m Ainekio V2 up through Spot and Electric Atlas to an 18.0 m Class IV Bipedal Mech](/posts/comparison/robot-scale-mech-lineup.webp)
*Figure 1: Standard elevation lineup on a common ground plane. Ainekio V2 (`0.11 m`, `650 g`, `~$85` BOM) is second from left, between the vacuum cleaner and the Furby. The 75-ton bipedal mech (`18.0 m`) is on the right for scale.*

There is always a bigger robot. Ainekio cannot lift a `50 kg` engine block like **Electric Atlas**, run `3.5 m/s` like a **Unitree Go2**, or fire long-range shoulder missiles like the **Mech**. Conversely, none of those platforms can sit on a bookshelf next to a Wi-Fi router without voiding a residential lease, and surprisingly few of them have a persistent episodic memory, a natural-language inverse-kinematics pose compiler, or a scheduled `02:00 AM` software sleep cycle with a built-in psychotherapist.

Below is how the hardware, locomotion, and software stack up across the spectrum.

## 2. Cost vs. Companion & Motion Capability

When you plot hardware cost on a logarithmic scale against what the robot's software and motion stack can actually do on its own—remembering past conversations, synthesizing multi-step poses from plain English, recovering from a stalled leg, or delegating tasks to a computer—the curve does not go up linearly with price.

![Logarithmic scatter chart comparing hardware cost against companion brain, memory, and generative motion capability](/posts/comparison/cost-vs-autonomy-chart.webp)
*Figure 2: Hardware cost (logarithmic USD axis) vs. autonomous companion, memory, and generative motion capability (`/100`).*

Most commercial and academic robots spend `95%` of their engineering budget on actuators, gearboxes, and balance controllers, leaving the high-level "brain" as either a stateless cloud voice wrapper or a manual joystick/cockpit interface. Because Ainekio pairs `$85` of ESP32-P4 / MG90S hardware with **[MetaHuman OS](https://github.com/Greg-Aster/metahuman-os)** running on a local host PC, it occupies the top-left corner of the chart: minimal mechanical force, high software and behavioral depth.

## 3. Actuation, Kinematics & Joint Sensing

A common assumption in small maker robots is that sub-`$100` hobby servos (`MG90S` / `SG90`) can only be run open-loop—you send a `50 Hz` PWM pulse to a `PCA9685` board and hope the leg reached the target angle. **Petoi Bittle X**, **Waveshare WAVEGO**, and **Stanford Pupper V1** all work that way. Even **Mini Pupper 2**'s reinforcement-learning controller (`SpotDMouse`) fakes joint observations in deployment by feeding its own predicted targets back into the policy and zeroing velocities.

Ainekio V2 takes a different approach: every `$1.50` `MG90S` servo is opened up and modified with a **4th wire soldered directly to its internal potentiometer wiper**, routed through a `$2` `CD74HC4067` 16-channel analog multiplexer into `ADC2` (`GPIO49`) on the `Waveshare ESP32-P4-WIFI6`.

## 4. Direct Peer Comparison: Stanford Pupper V3 & Microduck vs. Ainekio Freestyle

During November 2026 gait development, we vendor-pinned and audited both the upstream **Stanford Pupper V3** monorepo (`Nate711/pupperv3-monorepo`, `cs123-stanford/pupper_gait_deploy`, `cs123-stanford/pupper-mjlab`) and **Pollen Robotics Microduck** (`pollen-robotics/microduck_rl` on `mjlab`) inside `/home/greggles/Ainekio`.

Comparing the three codebases side by side clarifies what each platform actually implements in software:

| Capability | Stanford Pupper V3 (`~$2,000` BOM) | Microduck (`mjlab`, `~$399` BOM) | Ainekio V2 + Freestyle (`~$60–$100` BOM) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Walking / Locomotion** | `50 Hz` MJX PPO policy (4-legged + 3-legged policies via `neural_controller` at `520 Hz` ROS 2 timer) | `50 Hz` RL velocity policy in `mjlab` (`DYNAMIXEL XL330` bus) | **Both** deterministic `50 Hz` Native C closed-loop 4-bar IK (`Walk`, `Run`, `Crawl`, `Crab` + `gait_learning_v4`) **and** `50 Hz` MJX PPO Neural Gait (`walk_nominal_v1`) |
| **Poses & Emotes** | `animation_controller.py`: plays fixed `30 Hz` CSV recordings (`sit`, `shake`, `downward_dog`, `superman`) **open-loop** | **None** (locomotion policy only) | **Spatial IK + joint compiler** (`body`, `feet`, `pose`, `move`, `oscillate`, `gait`, `stand`, `hold`, `face`) + 23 cubic-spline finite gestures gated by **4th-wire arrival checkpoints** |
| **LLM Motion Interface** | `ros_node.py`: only calls `move(vx, vy, wz)` or `change_gait()`—**cannot synthesize new poses or multi-step gestures** | **None** | **Freestyle (`Qwen3.5-0.8B` + LoRA)**: synthesizes multi-step motion programs directly from natural language (*"lift one foot then the other"*) |

| **Simulation & Kinematic Solver** | Open-chain 3-link serial legs with `20 mm` sphere feet in MJX | Open-chain serial biped in `mujoco_warp` (Newton solver) | **Closed-loop 4-bar linkage MJCF** (`solver="cg"`, custom `MG90SActuator` speed-torque curve, GPU `FootAction` IK, and 720-feature `TensorSensors` proprioception) |

### Concrete notes from the codebase audit

1. **Stock Pupper V3 Virtual Benchmark (`stock-pupper-v3-forward-result.json`)**:
   - Running the unmodified upstream Pupper V3 policy (`policy_latest.json`) on its stock MuJoCo model for `14.995 s` at a commanded `+0.30 m/s` forward velocity produced `+1.6816 m` of forward travel (`0.1121 m/s` mean wall-clock speed, or `~37%` of command), `-0.2754 m` lateral drift, and `-16.14°` yaw drift, while the `520 Hz` ROS 2 timer experienced deadline overruns (`~31 Hz` effective target delivery vs `52 Hz` nominal).
2. **Closed-Loop 4-Bar Solver in `microduck.py`**:
   - Upstream `microduck_rl` uses `mujoco_warp`'s Newton solver for an open-chain biped. On Ainekio's closed four-bar loop-closure constraints, the Newton solver hits `float32` Hessian factorization `NaN`s (`mujoco_warp#1415`).
   - Ainekio's `microduck.py` switches the GPU physics loop to **Conjugate Gradient (`solver="cg"`, `iterations=50`, `500 Hz` physics / `50 Hz` control)**, models the `MG90S` speed-torque curve (`545.45°/s`), decodes `12D` normalized foot offsets (`FootAction`) on GPU through analytical four-bar IK and `PCA9685` PWM quantization, and feeds a **720-feature (`15 × 48`) proprioceptive history** (`ainekio-neural-gait-v7-proprio`) matching the physical `CD74HC4067` 4th-wire scan and `208 Hz` `LSM6DS3` IMU.
3. **Where Ainekio's `$1.50` Servos Bite Back (Trajectory Hygiene)**:
   - Because an `MG90S` has a fraction of the torque of Pupper V3's brushless `GIM4305-10` motors, Ainekio cannot brute-force raw joint sweeps while its weight is resting on a foot.
   - Auditing `motion-library.sqlite` showed that physical stalls during complex emotes come down to **how motions are represented in the seed training examples**, not the Freestyle compiler itself:
     - **Lift-and-place instead of loaded carpet slides**: When shifting weight before waving or alternating feet, trajectories must use `body` (6-DOF torso translation/roll/pitch solved against planted feet) and small vertical `feet` lifts (`+Z` unload → move → `0` place) rather than raw multi-leg `pose` sweeps that drag planted feet sideways across carpet.
     - **Avoiding overloaded rear-carrier angles**: Extreme loaded angles such as `rear_right_carrier: -31.3° / -32.69°` put the four-bar linkage near a mechanical disadvantage point. Replacing those entries in `motion-library.sqlite` keeps all twelve servos inside their clean operating envelope.

## 5. Cognitive Architecture, Memory, Dreams & Existential Overhead

If you open the software repository of almost any legged robot—academic or industrial—you find kinematics, state estimation, and a velocity command bus (`cmd_vel`). If an LLM is attached, it is typically a single stateless script that translates speech into `move(vx, vy, wz)` or picks one of four pre-recorded animations.

Ainekio is wired into **[MetaHuman OS](https://github.com/Greg-Aster/metahuman-os)** (`/home/greggles/metahuman`). As a result, this `$85` plastic crab-dog carries around an unusually heavy psychological and cognitive stack:

| Cognitive Subsystem | Furby / Roomba | EMO / Loona | Pupper V3 / Unitree Go2 | Spot / Atlas / 75-Ton Mech | Ainekio V2 + MetaHuman OS |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Standing Identity File** | None | Fixed character ROM | System prompt string | Asset serial number | **`persona/core.json`**: Big Five traits, values, short/mid/long-term goals, decision heuristics, and **5 trust levels** (`observe` → `adaptive_auto`). |

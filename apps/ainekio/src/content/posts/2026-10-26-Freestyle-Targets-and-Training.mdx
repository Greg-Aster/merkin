Between October 25 and October 27, work across the ESP32-P4 firmware, Blender
packaging, and the Freestyle motion generator converged on a closed-loop execution
pipeline: fixing a critical P4 stack overflow, locking in the **650 g** physical power
and chassis baseline, evolving Freestyle from open-ended continuation loops into
**one-shot finite programs** backed by a **349-example SQLite training library**, and
handling `servo_stall` jams and falls in-place while preserving the active
`motion_goal`.

## 650 g physical mass reconciliation, 2S power carrier, and training stand

Weighing the fully assembled robot reconciled the physical build at **650 g**
(up from the 566 g partial assembly on October 13), matching the `Wireless` scene in
`Ainekio-Motion-Working.blend` (spanning **245.85 × 182.79 × 109.06 mm** at frame 168).
The earlier USB power banks have been retired in favor of a compact rear power carrier
housing a **2S LiPo pack, Mini560 5 V / 5 A buck converter, and HiLetgo 2S USB-C
charger**:

| --- | --- |
| Assembled robot mass | **650 g** |
| 2S battery pack envelope | **73.6 × 46.0 × 22.0 mm** (8.4 V full charge) |
| Mini560 5 V / 5 A buck converter | **30.0 × 18.0 × 5.6 mm** (dedicated 5 V servo rail) |

## ESP32-P4 `start_motion` stack-overflow fix (`0.8.123`) and verified arrival

When launching `Stand` or `Walk`, an earlier firmware build rebooted because the
motion planner's temporary structures overflowed the 16 KiB task stack. Moving the
candidate planner into task-owned static storage in **`0.8.123`** cut `start_motion`
stack usage more than in half—from **8,496 bytes down to 3,760 bytes**—allowing the
robot on the training stand to complete `Stand` and a full `Walk` cycle cleanly
without rebooting or dropping Wi-Fi.

Subsequent firmware updates tightened closed-loop execution and settling across both
the legacy planner and Freestyle:

| Firmware revision | Closed-loop motion behavior |
| --- | --- |
| **`0.8.123`** | Fixed `start_motion` stack overflow (`8,496 → 3,760 B`); verified physical Stand and Walk cycle on stand |
| **`0.8.124`–`0.8.125`** | `Stand` and `Walk` lift/place landmarks wait for fresh 4th-wire shaft arrival (`100 µs` saved tolerance) plus a **2 mm lift-and-return settle** step |
| **`0.8.127`** | Boot/wake standing and inactivity rest route to Freestyle; in-place `servo_stall` jam and fall recovery preserves the active `motion_goal` |
| **`0.8.128`** | Resolves each Freestyle endpoint once for both ramping and shaft-arrival checks; if IK fitting clamps a target, reports the adjustment and keeps `position_verified: false` for the unadjusted request |

## Freestyle: from continuation loops to finite programs (`349` SQLite examples)

Freestyle trains a compact **Qwen3.5-0.8B LoRA (Q8_0)** to translate natural-language
movement requests and live sensor state into joint, foot, and body targets with
`speed_pct`.

### Compact token encoding and off-thread IK preparation

Compressing the Freestyle prompt/target representation cut Q6A CPU inference time by
**4×**, and moving inverse-kinematics preparation off the gateway's main Python thread
halved end-to-end sway execution time:

| Optimization | Before | After |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Q6A CPU prompt / output tokens (happy + 10 mm foot lift) | 3,154 in / 91 out | **823 in / 49 out** |
| Q6A CPU generation time | 72.89 s | **17.90 s** (10.23 s warm) |
| Host IK preparation + 2.8 s rear sway execution | 9.96 s | **5.03 s** (2.54 s for 1.28 s sway) |
| Workstation Q8_0 LoRA API latency | — | **0.663 s** |

### Why we replaced continuation loops with finite programs

In the October 26 continuation model (trained on 1,261 examples including 361
synthetic Stand continuations), feeding each movement receipt back into the model
caused a recovery loop that repeatedly re-emitted a standing pose and twitching
reassessments.

On October 27, we removed model-requested continuation from the contract:

1. **One-shot finite programs:** Freestyle now emits a complete multi-step program and
   runs it once. Reaching the final target finishes the command cleanly.
2. **In-place `servo_stall` & fall recovery:** If a joint stalls (`servo_stall`), a
   feedback wire faults, or the IMU detects a tip/fall during execution, the P4 holds
   powered support, preserves the active **`motion_goal`**, and dispatches the
   attributed fault and joint measurements for an in-place recovery trajectory.
3. **Curated 349-example SQLite library:** Editable directly in Body Control (`Save
   training example`, `Send motion to robot`, `Export training data`), the working
   SQLite library was distilled to **349 curated examples** (339 core + 116 synthetic
   rest/release/hold contrasts plus physical hand-reviewed corrections), raising
   frozen 62-case reference agreement to **40/62** (and **24/34** on the shared core
   set, up from 20/34).
4. **Verified 9-step physical stretch:** With left-crank Home calibration corrected to
   `−117.68° at 2750 µs`, the supported robot executed a full 9-step Freestyle stretch
   with **all 9 movement receipts position-verified** and zero clamped targets.

## MetaHuman OS: shared `llama-cpp.service` with 5-minute idle unload and Full Auto continuity

  <img src="/posts/2026-10-26-progress/companion-continuity.svg" alt="Diagram of MetaHuman Full Auto continuity, atomic Bridge snapshot publication, and on-demand llama.cpp weight loading." width="1000" height="600" loading="lazy" />

- **Shared `llama-cpp.service` with 300 s idle unload:** `bin/llama-cpp-services` now
  manages local model serving on demand and automatically unloads idle model weights
  after **5 minutes (300 seconds)** of inactivity, freeing GPU/system RAM when the
  robot is resting.
- **Full Auto continuity & provider isolation:** Full Auto can now target an existing
  execution or Desire, subscribe to specific event/environment/desire wake triggers,
  and use high reasoning for autonomous decisions while keeping graph system prompts
  isolated from coding-session context.
- **Atomic Bridge snapshots & idle conversation state:** Environment Bridge state
  files now update via atomic rename (eliminating transient empty reads), `Robot Status`
  distinguishes `last command source` from active manual takeover, and delivered
  conversational questions mark their execution context `idle` until the user replies.

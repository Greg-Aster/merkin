This September 9 study investigated driving a knee through short rigid rods
while its servo stays fixed to the chassis, even when the leg rolls sideways.
Keeping that motor and cable off the moving leg was a core design constraint. The
study compared three mechanisms against the same requested poses and a useful
knee-travel target of 160–180°.

**None met the complete brief.** The supported relay yoke, Variant 2, emerged as the
best candidate for further research, separate from the
[four-leg CAD assembly](/posts/2026-09-10-v2-body-design/).

## Study constraints and baseline

An available M3×30 bolt was modeled as allowing at most
**25 mm between rod-end pivot centers**. All three mechanisms use 40 mm effective
upper links and 60 mm lower links. The knee and roll servos stay chassis-fixed,
while the proximal hip-pitch servo moves with the roll carrier. Each modeled servo
case occupies a 22.8 × 12.2 × 28.5 mm envelope, and the input sweep is a 180° span
indexed from 10° to 190° in the geometric model.

The baseline came from the OpenHarmony Puppy V2 leg reference, with its 27 imported
meshes preserved for comparison. Credit and the original reference are documented in
the [V2 build guide](/posts/ainekio-v2/#legs-and-chassis).

## Comparing the three mechanisms

| Mechanism | Rod center distances | Useful modeled knee travel at hip 0°, roll 0° | Modeled travel with mesh checks at hip 30°, roll 0° | Pose probes passing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 · Direct short rod | 24.7 mm | 96.5° (53.7–150.2°) | 133.1° (34.9–168.0°) | 7 / 15 |
| 2 · Supported relay yoke | 24.5 + 23.5 mm | 132.1° (23.0–155.2°) | 133.3° (30.6–163.9°) | 9 / 15 |
| 3 · Offset actuator and cascade | 22.3 + 24.8 mm | 121.3° (27.9–149.2°) | 125.0° (27.9–152.9°) | 9 / 15 |

The two range columns represent tests at different hip angles: the first uses
geometric transmission, rod-end clearance, and folding checks at hip 0°, while the
second adds modeled surface-envelope intersection checks in 1° steps at hip 30°.

The direct rod (Variant 1) uses the fewest rods and pivots, but loses usable motion as
hip angle changes. Its 16 mm input crank is large for the modeled servo, and deep
folding and rear tuck are particularly limited.

Variant 2 divides the transmission at a supported rocker on the upper leg.
Its two rods share a relay pin, with their centers 7 mm apart across the leg, and
a symmetric yoke receives the drive at the knee. It reaches more requested
poses than the direct rod, though the folded shin and sideways clearance around the
fixed actuator remain limiting factors.

Variant 3 separates the relay’s input and output arms and offsets the actuator.
That permits phase adjustment, but introduces a second relay pin and a more
nonlinear relationship between servo and knee movement. Its rearward poses
lose range, and the added hardware brings more potential friction and play.

## What stopped Variant 2 from passing

At neutral hip angle, Variant 2's useful geometric range is about **132°**, short of
the 160–180° target. Shifting the servo horn’s starting orientation moves where that
travel window falls, but cannot enlarge the 180° actuator span.

| Requested probe | Result in the modeled study |
| --- | --- |
| Neutral stand, low crouch, full sit, front wave | Passed geometric and surface-envelope checks |
| Almost-straight forward extension | Outside the fixed servo window on the retained linkage branch |
| Near-floor fold | Shin, rod, or shaft interference |
| Rear tuck | Rod/crank clearance and interference with the roll-servo envelope |
| Wide and narrow stance | Fixed knee-servo, cheek, or rod-end interference |

The 14 mm internal channel is too narrow for every desired roll position of
the fixed input and its rod ends. Opening the root of that channel and relocating
the roll-servo package are the next mechanical steps if this relay path is pursued.

## Screening criteria

During the motion search, the solver retained the same linkage assembly branch and
screened against a 20° minimum transmission angle, an angular gain window of 0.4–2.5,
rod-end clearance, link folding, and mesh intersections. Those bounds helped isolate
where short-rod linkages bind before committing to printed parts.

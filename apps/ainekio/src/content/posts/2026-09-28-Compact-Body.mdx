# Making room for two build options

**Later development — October 6:** the printed chassis could not stand on its
MG90 servos. The [lighter rebuild](/posts/2026-10-06-lighter-rebuild/) keeps those
servos and replaces the heavy battery bank with separate batteries and onboard
charging boards. New parts are printing. The dimensions and bank packaging
below preserve the September 28 iteration.

The mechanical work since September 24 covers the frame, electronics tray,
battery, face and assembly references. The build is now split into **Wired**,
which carries the Q6A computer, and **Wireless**, which leaves it offboard.
The [integration entry](/posts/2026-09-28-wired-wireless-ros2/) compares their
transport, ROS 2 and power plans.

The latest Wireless record reaches **82.5 mm across the outside of the frame**.
It restores the original front carriers, moves the front leg assemblies
**4.1 mm forward**, and retains relief cuts in the rear carriers. The tray,
support length, end panels and shell still need to follow that new placement.
This is an in-progress fit revision, not a finished print set.

## Separate designs in Blender

The former modeling scene became **Wireless**, with **Wired** made as an
independent copy. Their objects, mesh data and materials can now be edited
separately. Parent relationships, constraints and driver references were
retargeted within each design. The existing Gait and Motions scenes were
preserved, and save/reopen checks verified the split.

Both designs started from the same hardware layout. Subsequent compact edits
apply to Wireless; they do not automatically change the animation scenes or
the Wired body. The older Blender showcase therefore remains useful motion
history, but does not show today's frame clearances.

## Width, battery and leg placement

The battery reference has changed from the earlier Anker envelope to the
**115 × 51 × 26 mm SHARGE Pouch**. The bank stays centered laterally with its
port facing rearward. The table keeps the successive frame dimensions tied
to their own revisions.

| Geometry decision | Wider baseline / intermediate revision | Latest Wireless record |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Outer frame width | 100.3 mm restored baseline; then 94.3 mm compact Wireless layout | 82.5 mm |
| Front-to-rear pivot spacing | 147.7 mm in the 94.3 mm layout | 151.8 mm after moving the front assemblies 4.1 mm forward |
| Front Part_003 carriers | An intermediate trial relieved all four carriers | Original front geometry restored, including mounting holes, rails and surface details |
| Rear Part_003 carriers | Original outline constrained narrowing around the bank | Battery relief retained |
| Checked inward shoulder movement | 0–17° in the 94.3 mm layout at fixed carrier/crank pose | Front 0–17°; rear 0–13.4°, at the reviewed carrier/crank poses |
| Electronics tray and bridge | Included in the 94.3 mm layout's fit checks | Excluded from the final narrowing decision; refit outstanding |
| Panels and shell | Earlier placements and geometry | Support length, end-panel and shell fitting still to follow |

Moving the front assemblies forward recovered clearance for the original
front carriers without continuing to cut them down. Their sampled minimum
battery gap is approximately **0.590 mm at 17° inward swing**, with a
conservative bound above 0.56 mm between 0.1° samples. At the rear, the
limiting moving hardware is Part_006 at approximately **0.523 mm** over the
checked 0–13.4° range.

**The fixed Part_002 servo bodies still nominally touch the battery sides.**
Those near-zero clearances do not provide a practical manufacturing allowance.
The bank, boards and fixed hardware kept their dimensions; no servo casing
was trimmed to make the fit work.

These are **battery-clearance checks at particular carrier/crank poses**.
They neither establish a complete gait envelope nor change firmware limits.
The most recent review preserves the rear assemblies, unrelated geometry and
all 71 animation actions. It does not prove loaded strength, printed fit or
unrestricted motion of the narrower body.

## The onboard electronics tray

Before the split, the Q6A tray was reduced from roughly **189.42 × 81 mm** to
**173.92 × 61 mm**. It retains a **1.2 mm floor**, rounded ventilation slots and
supports for the P4 and servo driver. Small ledges carry the underside of the
Q6A Armor case while leaving its fins exposed.

<figure>
  <img src="/posts/2026-09-28-progress/electronics-tray.webp" alt="Q6A case, servo driver and P4 arranged on the slim slotted electronics tray." width="1400" height="850" loading="lazy" />
  <figcaption>September 27 onboard electronics layout. This view documents the Wired packaging baseline; the Wireless option removes the Q6A from the carried assembly.</figcaption>
</figure>

The tray's modeled material volume fell from **20.28 to 12.96 cm³**, a
**36.1% reduction**. That is a geometric comparison, not a measured print mass
or strength result. The case remains an approximate reference with about
0.3 mm modeled side clearance. A later rear-panel wrap follows the tray with
a nominal 0.2 mm gap; full-motion checks were not repeated for that panel edit.

The intermediate 94.3 mm Wireless design instead placed the P4 and Adafruit
servo driver side by side over the bank, with the purple IMU reference at the
bottom/front and its headers facing outward. A separate bridge locates on
tray pegs. Positive retention and cable fit remain physical prototype work,
and the subsequent 82.5 mm narrowing requires that bridge to be refitted.

## Display, carrier and front panel

The face now uses a **62 × 29 mm PCB reference** for the XIITIA 1.9-inch
display. This imported reference was reconstructed from product information
and images; hole and component details are estimated rather than supplied by the manufacturer.
The front mask was widened locally around it, retaining the angular exterior,
and the chin became a separate object.

| Fasteners | Separate PCB-to-mask and carrier-to-panel patterns | Screw-head seats and screw lengths remain deferred |

The header measurement covers the modeled bare pins, not an installed plug
or wire bend. It was made before the latest front-leg relocation, so it is
not a clearance certificate for the complete 82.5 mm assembly. Some side
protector contacts also remain. This is why front-panel and shell refitting
stay on the next-work list.

## Assembly marks and calibration references

The leg assembly work added **12 pairs of alignment marks across four legs**:
carrier to end panel, main arm to carrier, and crank to main arm. The modeled
grooves are **0.6 mm wide and 0.3 mm deep**.

<figure>
  <img src="/posts/2026-09-28-progress/assembly-marks.webp" alt="Close Blender view of matching grooves on the red leg arm, gray crank and adjacent carrier surfaces." width="1400" height="1000" loading="lazy" />
  <figcaption>Assembly-reference grooves in the September 25–26 work. They refer to the earlier 1300 µs assembly pose; the later pulse-endpoint review does not redefine these marks.</figcaption>
</figure>

The newer reported pulse endpoints are **300–3000 µs**. Their arithmetic
midpoint is **1650 µs**, but measured shaft travel and a matching assembled
neutral remain unresolved. The marks must not be reinterpreted as a new
1650 µs installation reference merely because that number is the midpoint.
The review did not change firmware calibration or qualify all four legs.

## What is ready to use from this entry?

The images and dimensions above document saved modeling work through
**13:04 PDT on September 28**. The latest scene split, face fit and Wireless
carrier changes were saved and independently reopened in their review records.
They did **not** create a new complete STL/GLB release. Earlier
[dated print downloads](/posts/2026-09-21-compact-leg-design/) remain available
as earlier revisions.

The next mechanical work is to fit the supports, panels, electronics bridge
and shell to the latest front/rear positions, resolve practical battery and
connector allowances, and check the resulting assembly through its required
motions before recording printed and loaded results.

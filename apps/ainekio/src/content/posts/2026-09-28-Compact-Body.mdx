Ainekio V2 now has two distinct build configurations in Blender and software:

- **Wired** carries the **Radxa Dragon Q6A** computer onboard in a slimmed slotted
  tray, with a planned native-USB link to the ESP32-P4.
- **Wireless** moves the Q6A offboard and connects over Wi-Fi through the P4's
  ESP32-C6 coprocessor, allowing an **82.5 mm outer frame width** and a smaller
  onboard electronics bridge.

> **October 6 follow-up:** Wireless became the active physical build priority, and
> after the first printed chassis could not stand on its MG90 servos with a heavy
> USB power bank, the [October 6 compact linkage and lighter rebuild](/posts/2026-10-06-compact-linkage/)
> replaced the power bank with a 2S battery and onboard buck/charger boards.

## Wired and Wireless side by side

In Blender, the modeling scene was split into independent **Wireless** and **Wired**
scenes with retargeted parent relationships, constraints, and driver references,
while preserving all 71 animation actions in the Gait and Motions scenes.

| Build decision | Wired (Onboard Q6A) | Wireless (Offboard Q6A) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Q6A placement | Onboard in a 173.92 × 61 mm slotted tray | Offboard, powered separately |
| P4-to-Q6A link | Native USB (P1 interface) under investigation | Wi-Fi via ESP32-C6 coprocessor and gateway |
| Outer frame width | 100.3 mm outer-support baseline | **82.5 mm** (narrowed from 94.3 mm intermediate pass) |
| Front-to-rear pivot spacing | 147.7 mm | **151.8 mm** (front leg assemblies moved 4.1 mm forward) |
| Carried electronics | ESP32-P4, PCA9685, Q6A + Armor case, IMU, peripherals, 12 servos | ESP32-P4, PCA9685, IMU, peripherals, 12 servos |
| Sept 28 battery study | SHARGE Pouch (115 × 51 × 26 mm, 10,000 mAh / 36 Wh), two 5 V / 3 A branches (Q6A on branch 1, P4 + servos on branch 2) | Same bank envelope, splitting P4 logic and servo rails across the two 5 V / 3 A branches |

## Narrowing the Wireless frame to 82.5 mm

Replacing the 155 mm Anker reference with the shorter **115 × 51 × 26 mm SHARGE Pouch**
allowed pulling the Wireless frame inward to **82.5 mm** across the outer walls.

Moving the front leg assemblies **4.1 mm forward** (to 151.8 mm pivot spacing)
restored the original uncut front Part_003 carriers while maintaining a **0.590 mm**
minimum battery gap at 17° inward shoulder swing. At the rear, relief cuts in the
Part_003 carriers provide a **0.523 mm** minimum clearance for Part_006 across a
0–13.4° inward swing window.

## Onboard electronics tray and Wireless bridge

For the Wired layout, the Q6A electronics tray was trimmed from **189.42 × 81 mm**
down to **173.92 × 61 mm** with a **1.2 mm floor**, rounded ventilation slots, and
ledges that support the Q6A Armor case while leaving its cooling fins exposed. That
cut modeled tray volume from **20.28 cm³ to 12.96 cm³ (−36.1%)**.

## ROS 2 Jazzy, IMU feedback plan, and transport bandwidth

**ROS 2 Jazzy** is installed on the Q6A, with the gateway remaining the single
command authority to the P4's motor outputs:

| Subsystem | Role in the V2 architecture |
| --- | --- |
| Q6A ROS 2 adapter | Publishes gateway telemetry (camera frames, timestamped IMU samples, and body state) to ROS 2 topics for inspection in RViz and recording via `rosbag2` |
| MetaHuman OS | High-level conversation, personality, memory, and intent routing through the gateway interface |
| ESP32-P4 local loop | Inverse kinematics, 50 Hz servo trajectory supervision, and planned **208 Hz LSM6DS3 IMU** acquisition for fast local posture correction without round-tripping through ROS 2 or an LLM |

Meanwhile, the P4 gait controller gained lower working body references for better
geometric leverage and a saved **Motion speed (×)** multiplier for Stand, Sit, and
finite gestures (see the [gait development log](/posts/gait-development/#september-28-2026--leverage-motion-speed-and-feedback-planning)).

### Media and compute bandwidth notes

Evaluating native USB (on P4 header **P1**, separate from the CH343 debug USB-C port)
versus Wi-Fi highlighted concrete throughput boundaries:

- **Camera & PSRAM:** Three uncompressed 5 MP RGB888 frames would require 43.25 MiB
  (exceeding the P4's 32 MiB PSRAM), keeping the processed pipeline capped at 1080p
  hardware JPEG or XGA/VGA preview streams.
- **Link scheduling:** A 256 KiB JPEG stream at 30 fps represents 62.9 Mbit/s before
  audio and protocol framing, whereas the current link loop services one microphone
  packet and one JPEG frame per 20 ms iteration.
- **Q6A CPU TTS baseline:** Synthesizing a 5-second spoken phrase with Kokoro on the
  Q6A CPU (8 threads, 1.54 GiB peak RSS) took 15.07–15.90 seconds, showing that voice
  latency is dominated by synthesis compute rather than body-link transport.

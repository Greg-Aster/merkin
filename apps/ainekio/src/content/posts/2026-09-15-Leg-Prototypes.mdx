the body: the leg assembly. Work now includes **3D-printed prototypes
assembled with servos, linkages, and fasteners**, alongside continued Blender
revisions. These September 14 photos show a physical leg assembly across three poses.

## Finding a linkage that fits around the servos

The September 12–13 linkage work explored several link lengths while retaining the
servo axes and surrounding housing geometry. Lengthening a link can solve a
local clearance issue while changing the mechanical advantage and angular sweep of
the leg.

An initial pass used the same **40 mm, 4 mm-thick link** at both Part_024 and
Part_026, with Part_025's two arms made equal at 20 mm and Part_009 shortened
to 21 mm. While that reduced unique link shapes, a bolt near the second servo
still restricted travel.

A subsequent revision fitted an imported Part_024 at its **~24.64 mm pivot spacing
and 4 mm thickness**, keeping Part_009 at 21 mm. Turning the link over seated the
bearings from the proper side and cleared the housing through the reviewed sweep.
That geometry was then synchronized across all four legs and oriented end-for-end
to preserve pivot clearance.

## Opening up the foot-shell entrance

The first printed Part_020 and Part_022 foot shells would not slide over the
Part_027 lower leg at the entrance, where the CAD gaps were only **0.106 mm and
0.150 mm** on the two sides.

The September 14 clearance revision opened both cavities to **0.30 mm per
mating surface** and added a **0.15 mm entrance chamfer** (~0.45 mm at the opening).
The cut follows the full insertion profile of Part_027, including the central
teardrop key, while preserving the cavity floors, 5 mm clamping stack, screw seats,
and exterior tread. Opening the cavity leaves thin local collar lips (~0.57 mm and
0.40 mm) backed by the thicker shell wall, which will be checked on the next print.

![Comparison of the original and revised shell entrances, showing 0.30 mm mating clearance and 0.45 mm at the lead-in.](/posts/2026-09-15-leg-prototypes/shell-clearance-comparison.png)

*CAD comparison of the original and widened foot-shell entrances.*

## Measuring the servo horn directly

Physical servo horn arms measured **2.0 mm thick**, whereas the imported reference
meshes were **1.75 mm**—explaining why pockets cut to the reference mesh were too
shallow.

The revised arm pockets in Part_009 and Part_023 are **2.1 mm deep** (0.1 mm nominal
clearance for the physical horn), leaving 2.8 mm and 1.9 mm backing walls
respectively. Straight tapered horn-arm outlines replaced an earlier wide-shoulder
profile, and Part_023 gained a uniform 2.1 mm pocket floor and flush outer ledge
around its 2.5 mm center bore.

At the lower leg, Part_027's neck was widened to a nominal **8 mm** (retaining its
5 mm thickness and pivot bores), and Part_025's outward screw boss was split into a
separate **1.5 mm-high collar** for a longer M3 fastener.

## Three iterations of the foot

Later on September 14, the foot shells progressed through three profiles at a
slimmer **9.2 mm assembled width** (down from ~15 mm):

| Foot revision | Geometry | Focus for next print |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Slim symmetric pair | Flat outer faces; 2 mm backing; 0.10 mm cavity allowance | Cavity fit and print tolerance |
| Chevron tread | 9.2 mm width; 1 mm-deep grooves at 35°; 0.5 mm perimeter bevel | Corner crispness, fit, and grip |
| Lugged tread with bolt recesses | 2.2 mm-deep tread gaps; 1.3 mm center channel; Ø7 × 1.5 mm bolt pockets | Pocket bridging, bolt seating, and traction |

![CAD close-up of the later foot with angled tread lugs and a recessed bolt seat.](/posts/2026-09-15-leg-prototypes/offroad-foot-cad.png)

*Lugged foot CAD with angled tread lugs and recessed Ø7 × 1.5 mm bolt pockets.*

The lugged version increases the tread-tip radius to 11 mm (up from 9.8 mm in the
chevron version) and leaves a minimum **0.5 mm floor** at the outer rim of the bolt
pocket. The STLs orient the flat outer faces on the print bed with the cavities
facing up.

## Shoulder swivel envelope

A parallel shoulder study is exploring a wider swivel envelope around a 270° servo
while preserving the shaft axis and leg fold clearance. Early motion probes in CAD
highlighted clashes between the proposed socket, servo case, and adjacent parked legs,
which leads directly into the [September 21 compact leg and chassis revisions](/posts/2026-09-21-compact-leg-design/).

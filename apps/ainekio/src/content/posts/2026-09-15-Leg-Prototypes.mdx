# Building the leg, then checking the fit

The last few days have concentrated on the part of Ainekio that has to carry
the body: the leg assembly. The work now includes **3D-printed prototypes
assembled with servos, linkages, and fasteners**, alongside continued Blender
revisions. These September 14 photos show a leg in three configurations.

The movement itself has been a highlight of working with the printed assemblies:

> The printed prototypes have much more expressive movement than the models
> I have seen online.

*Greg’s observation from the physical prototypes, September 15.*

The photographs establish that parts have been printed and assembled. They do
not measure powered travel, torque, backlash, or the ability to support the
complete robot. Later September 14 foot revisions described below have their
own CAD records and are not established as printed by these photos.

## Finding a linkage that fits around the servos

The September 12–13 linkage work explored several lengths while retaining the
servo axes and the surrounding assembly. That matters because lengthening a
link can solve one clearance problem while changing how far the leg moves for
a given servo angle.

One review used the same **40 mm, 4 mm-thick link** at both Part_024 and
Part_026, with Part_025's two arms made equal at 20 mm and Part_009 shortened
to 21 mm. This reduced the number of different link shapes, but a modeled bolt
near the second servo still limited movement. It was a layout option, not a
finished motion solution.

A later review fitted an imported Part_024 at its existing **approximately
24.64 mm pivot spacing and 4 mm thickness**. Part_009 remained 21 mm. The
imported link was positioned and turned over so the bearings seated from the
correct side and its curve cleared the housing in the reviewed pose. That
mechanical layout was then matched across all four legs. A subsequent review
reversed the four Part_024 links end-for-end while preserving their pivots and
mesh shapes.

![CAD view of the imported short linkage fitted to the leg assembly.](/posts/2026-09-15-leg-prototypes/imported-linkage-cad.png)

*September 13 imported-linkage fit, before the subsequent reversal and local
horn, shell, and foot refinements. This is a saved CAD stage, not a photograph.*

These revisions change the relationship between servo rotation and leg angle.
Earlier gait calibration cannot simply be assumed to apply. The separate
[September 9 short-rod study](/posts/2026-09-09-leg-mechanism-study/) remains a
record of another research path; its motion ranges do not describe these
printed prototypes.

## A print that would not fit over the leg

The shell-clearance record documents a concrete assembly problem: printed
Part_020 and Part_022 would not fit over Part_027 at the entrance. The captured
CAD gaps were approximately **0.106 mm and 0.150 mm** on the two sides.

The September 14 clearance revision opened both cavities to **0.30 mm per
mating surface** and added a **0.15 mm entrance chamfer**, giving approximately
0.45 mm at the opening. The cut follows the complete insertion outline of
Part_027, including the central teardrop key, rather than one section through
the part. The cavity floors, 5 mm clamping stack, screw seats, exterior tread,
and lower leg itself were retained.

![Comparison of the original and revised shell entrances, showing 0.30 mm mating clearance and 0.45 mm at the lead-in.](/posts/2026-09-15-leg-prototypes/shell-clearance-comparison.png)

*Recorded CAD comparison for the clearance revision. The dimensions describe
the model; a new print is needed to establish the resulting fit.*

This was a **front-left pair in a separate review file**. It should not be
read as a change already applied to every leg or to every later foot design.
The larger cavity also reduces material around the collar: the recorded lip
walls are about 0.57 mm and 0.40 mm on the two shells. The thicker backing
remains, but that local rim needs attention during the next fit test.

## Measuring the horn instead of trusting its model

The horn-recess record reports physical horn arms measured at **2 mm thick**,
while the reference meshes were still **1.75 mm**. That difference explains why
a pocket matching the model could be too shallow for the part in hand.

The revised arm pockets in Part_009 and Part_023 are **2.1 mm deep**, allowing
0.1 mm nominal depth clearance for the measured arm. Their recorded backing
thicknesses are 2.8 mm and 1.9 mm respectively. The straight tapered horn-arm
outlines replace an earlier wider shoulder and curved transition that had been
rejected.

Part_023 then received a more uniform floor and a flush outer ledge while
retaining its inner seat. The final saved check measures the 2.1 mm cavity depth
across the pocket and ledge. The central hole remains 2.5 mm, with support
retained underneath the inner seat. These are housing changes; the separate
Part_008 sleeve fit is still its own problem.

Other local changes improve the fastening and lower-leg geometry. Part_027's
neck was widened to a nominal **8 mm**, keeping its 5 mm thickness and pivot
bores. Part_025's outward screw boss became a separate **1.5 mm-high collar**.
The modeled M3×6 screw ends roughly 0.6 mm below that collar, so the record
calls for a longer screw. Splitting the collar does not by itself settle the
fastener length or joint strength.

## Three iterations of the foot

Later on September 14, the foot shells were developed through a separate slim
pair, chevron tread, and deeper lugged tread. Their **9.2 mm assembled width**
replaces an earlier approximately 15 mm width. Part_027 remains unchanged in
this foot sequence.

| Foot revision | Recorded geometry | Next physical check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Slim symmetric pair | Flat outer faces; 2 mm backing; 0.10 mm nominal cavity allowance | Whether the tight cavity fits this print and material |
| Chevron tread | Same 9.2 mm width; 1 mm-deep grooves at 35°; 0.5 mm perimeter bevel | Printed corners, fit, traction, and durability |
| Lugged tread with bolt recesses | 2.2 mm-deep tread gaps; 1.3 mm center channel; Ø7 × 1.5 mm bolt pockets | Pocket bridging, bolt seating, rim strength, and traction |

![CAD close-up of the later foot with angled tread lugs and a recessed bolt seat.](/posts/2026-09-15-leg-prototypes/offroad-foot-cad.png)

*Later foot CAD: the bolt pockets are present in the final saved file. The
earlier preview labeled “Bolt Seat Pending” predates those recesses.*

The lugged version increases the tread-tip radius to 11 mm, compared with
9.8 mm in the chevron version. The bolt pocket leaves a minimum **0.5 mm floor
at its outer rim**, with thicker support toward the center. The exports place
the flat outside faces on the print bed, with the cavities upward; the bolt
pocket therefore introduces a small roof around the shaft opening.

The slim-foot sequence retains its own approximately **0.10 mm cavity
allowance**. It does not automatically inherit the earlier 0.30 mm shell-fit
revision. Comparing those two clearances against real prints is part of
choosing the next foot, not evidence that either value is universally correct.

## Shoulder motion is still being worked out

The shoulder study investigates a larger swivel envelope while preserving the
shaft axis and allowing the leg to bend. Its brief uses a 270° swivel servo,
but usable assembled travel depends on the leg pose, chassis, and neighboring
legs. That hardware range is not a measured whole-assembly range.

The reviewed folder contains motion probes and draft chassis geometry. Its
static report still records clashes involving the proposed socket, servo, and
other parked legs, and no final extended-chassis reopening/export result was
present. This remains work in progress rather than a completed shoulder fix.

## What the next prototype needs to establish

The immediate work is to choose a coherent leg and foot revision, check its
printed fit, settle the sleeve and fastening stack, and measure joint travel
through bending and swivel. Load-bearing strength, repeatability, cable
clearance, and powered behavior then need to be checked on the assembled
hardware. A complete V2 walking test is still a separate milestone.

This update uses the September 12–14 linkage, shell-clearance, horn-recess,

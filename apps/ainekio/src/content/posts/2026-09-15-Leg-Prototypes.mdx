The last few days focused on the assembly that actually has to carry Ainekio V2: the
three-servo leg. I printed the first physical prototypes and bolted them up with MG90S
servos, bearings, linkages, and two-piece feet while iterating tolerances back in
Blender. Even on the bench by hand, these printed legs have much more expressive range
than the stock reference models I had seen online.

## Finding a linkage that fits around the servos

Over September 12–13 I tested several link combinations around the existing servo shaft
spacing. Lengthening a single link can clear a bolt head locally, but it shifts mechanical
advantage and total knee sweep everywhere else.

My first pass tried using identical **40 mm, 4 mm-thick links** for both the upper and
lower coupler rods, paired with a symmetric **20 mm relay rocker** and a shortened
**21 mm knee crank**. Cutting down unique link parts was appealing, but a fastener head
near the second servo still clipped the sweep.

The next pass switched the upper coupler to a **24.64 mm pivot spacing (4 mm thick)**
while keeping the **21 mm knee crank**. Flipping that short link seated the flange
bearings from the outer face and cleared the servo bracket across the full travel, so I
mirrored that arrangement across all four legs.

## Opening up the foot-shell entrance

On the bench, the first printed outer and inner curved foot shells refused to slide
onto the lower leg shank—the original CAD only left **0.106 mm and 0.150 mm** of
clearance on the two mating faces.

On September 14 I opened both shell cavities to **0.30 mm per mating surface** and added
a **0.15 mm lead-in chamfer** (~0.45 mm at the mouth). The pocket follows the full
insertion profile of the lower leg, including its central teardrop key, while keeping
the cavity floor, 5 mm clamping thickness, screw seats, and sole tread intact. That
leaves thin local collar lips (~0.57 mm and 0.40 mm) backed by the main shell wall,
which I will check on the next print.

![Comparison of the original and revised shell entrances, showing 0.30 mm mating clearance and 0.45 mm at the lead-in.](/posts/2026-09-15-leg-prototypes/shell-clearance-comparison.png)

*Original vs widened entrance clearance on the two-piece curved foot shells.*

## Measuring the servo horn directly

Another bench surprise: the physical plastic servo horns measured **2.0 mm thick** with
calipers, whereas the imported CAD reference horns were **1.75 mm**—which explained why
horns bottomed out proud of the printed pockets.

I deepened the horn-arm pockets in both the **knee crank** and the **upper carrier** to
**2.1 mm** (0.1 mm nominal clearance over the real horn), leaving 2.8 mm and 1.9 mm
backing walls. Straight tapered arm slots replaced an earlier wide-shoulder cutout, and
the upper carrier got a flat 2.1 mm pocket floor and flush outer rim around its 2.5 mm
center screw bore.

Down at the knee, I widened the **lower leg** neck to **8 mm** (keeping its 5 mm
thickness and pivot bores) and split the **relay rocker's** outer boss into a separate
**1.5 mm-tall spacer collar** for a longer M3 screw.

## Three iterations of the foot

Later on September 14, the foot shells progressed through three profiles at a
slimmer **9.2 mm assembled width** (down from ~15 mm):

| Foot revision | Geometry | Focus for next print |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Slim symmetric pair | Flat outer faces; 2 mm backing; 0.10 mm cavity allowance | Cavity fit and print tolerance |
| Chevron tread | 9.2 mm width; 1 mm-deep grooves at 35°; 0.5 mm perimeter bevel | Corner crispness, fit, and grip |
| Lugged tread with bolt recesses | 2.2 mm-deep tread gaps; 1.3 mm center channel; Ø7 × 1.5 mm bolt pockets | Pocket bridging, bolt seating, and traction |

![CAD close-up of the later foot with angled tread lugs and a recessed bolt seat.](/posts/2026-09-15-leg-prototypes/offroad-foot-cad.png)

*Lugged foot CAD with angled tread lugs and recessed Ø7 × 1.5 mm bolt pockets.*

The lugged version increases the tread-tip radius to 11 mm (up from 9.8 mm in the
chevron version) and leaves a minimum **0.5 mm floor** at the outer rim of the bolt
pocket. The STLs orient the flat outer faces on the print bed with the cavities
facing up.

## Shoulder swivel envelope

A parallel shoulder study is exploring a wider swivel envelope around a 270° servo
while preserving the shaft axis and leg fold clearance. Early motion probes in CAD
highlighted clashes between the proposed socket, servo case, and adjacent parked legs,
which leads directly into the [September 21 compact leg and chassis revisions](/posts/2026-09-21-compact-leg-design/).

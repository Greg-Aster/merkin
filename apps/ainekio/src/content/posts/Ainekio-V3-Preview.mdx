> **Workbench status — In Development / Active CAD:** The `650 g` twelve-servo **[Ainekio V2](/posts/ainekio-v2/)** remains the active physical robot walking untethered on the floor today, following the original eight-servo **[Ainekio V1](/posts/ainekio-v1/)**. **Ainekio V3** is now taking full shape in Blender with its exterior carapace shell, internal electronics trays, `5.0"` HD touchscreen face, waterproof **`DS3235`** four-bar legs, and onboard Linux SBC + `ESP32-P4` compute bay packaged inside a tightened `337.9 × 200.2 × 176.8 mm` chassis.

After getting the `650 g` micro-servo V2 quadruped walking untethered on its on-chip `50 Hz` `ESP32-P4` neural gait, two physical ceilings of a `~2 kg·cm` `MG90S` micro-chassis became clear:

1. **Speed, terrain clearance, and weather exposure:** Micro-scale `55 mm` lower legs and unsealed hobby micro servos work well indoors, but they limit stride speed, ground clearance, and damp or outdoor operation.
2. **Reliance on an external computer:** During the September [Radxa Dragon Q6A chassis study](/posts/q6a-chassis-and-active-loop/), carrying an onboard Linux single-board computer plus heavier battery cells pushed V2 past what twelve `MG90S` servos could comfortably stand up and walk with—leaving the Body Control gateway and **[Ainekio OS](/posts/ainekio-os/)** cognitive stack on an intermediate host machine over Wi-Fi.

**Ainekio V3** removes both bottlenecks at once. By scaling the quadruped to roughly **`1.7×` V2's dimensions** around twelve waterproof **`DSSERVO DS3235`** standard metal-gear servos and housing **both an `ESP32-P4` real-time controller and a full Wi-Fi/Bluetooth Linux SBC directly inside the chassis**, V3 eliminates the intermediate PC—and turns the robot itself into a **walking, local-AI personal computer** whose `5.0"` HD touchscreen face can switch straight into a live Linux desktop.

## 1. Updated V3 CAD progress: tightened wheelbase, exterior carapace, and `5.0"` touch face

Since the initial open-rail frame layout, the `V3` Blender scene has progressed into a compact, fully shelled assembly (`271` modeled meshes). Tightening the central side rails down to **`112.0 mm`** brought the fore-aft shoulder wheelbase in from `302.0 mm` to **`221.5 mm`** (`FL/FR X = +118.5 mm`, `RL/RR X = -103.0 mm`) and narrowed lateral hip-roll spacing from `110.0 mm` to **`94.4 mm`**—giving V3 a much stiffer, punchier torso proportion while still fitting the full dual-board compute stack and four `21700` battery cells inside:

## 2. Turning Ainekio's face into a Linux desktop: a smarter, mobile alternative to PCs, laptops & phones

Putting a full **Linux single-board computer with onboard Wi-Fi and Bluetooth** inside the V3 chassis does much more than cut the Wi-Fi umbilical to an external gateway PC—it changes what kind of machine Ainekio is.

In the updated V3 front assembly, Ainekio's face is a **`5.0-inch` `Waveshare 5-DSI-TOUCH-A` capacitive touchscreen** (`1280 × 720` HD IPS active area measuring `110.3 × 62.3 mm`, `126.9 × 70.7 mm` cover glass) driven directly over MIPI-DSI by the onboard Linux SBC, framed by a protective brow visor housing the `OV5647` CSI camera.

Because the robot carries a real multi-core Linux operating system, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, local storage, and rear physical I/O ports wherever it walks, **V3 has the potential to compete directly with personal computers, laptops, and phones—while being smarter, embodied, and physically mobile**:

- **Face-to-desktop mode on demand:** In everyday companion mode, the `5.0"` `1280×720` display renders Ainekio's animated expressions, status overlays, and direct touch controls. Whenever you want to sit down and work, **you can switch Ainekio's face straight into a full Linux desktop, pull out a Bluetooth (or USB) mouse and keyboard, and get to work**.
- **Full workstation software, zero laptop bag:** Because it runs standard mainline/arm64 Linux rather than a locked-down phone OS or microcontroller loop, the robot natively runs a desktop browser, terminal, VS Code / coding toolchains, SSH, Git, media apps, and the entire **[Ainekio OS](/posts/ainekio-os/)** cognitive + memory runtime locally on its own battery.
- **Rear port bulkhead & dockable desktop expansion:** As shown in the cutaway below, the SBC's physical ports (USB-A, Gigabit Ethernet, USB-C, and HDMI/DisplayPort output on the modeled `Radxa Dragon Q6A` reference board) face the rear carapace arch—meaning V3 can either stand on your desk as its own self-tilting `5.0"` all-in-one cyberdeck screen, or plug into an external monitor when docked while keeping your persistent AI mind, files, and workspace inside one walking body.
- **Smarter than a static screen:** Unlike a phone or laptop that sits inert on a table when closed, your computer can physically walk into the next room, tilt its own `5.0"` screen up toward you on its 12-DOF legs for an ergonomic viewing angle, watch its surroundings, remember what you were working on yesterday, and react in the room.

## 4. Waterproof `DS3235` actuators and `1.74×` bearing-supported legs

Carrying an onboard Linux PC, a `5.0"` glass touch display, an enclosed weather shell, and four `21700` cells requires a completely different actuator class than V2's `MG90S` micro servos. V3 uses twelve **`DSSERVO DS3235`** waterproof standard servos (`40 × 20 × ~40 mm`):

1. **High-torque `2S` direct headroom:** Rated for `7.4–8.4 V` (`2S`) operation with CNC aluminum middle heatsinks, metal gear trains, and `25T` clamping aluminum horns, `DS3235` actuators provide the torque and speed authority needed for brisk indoor/outdoor trotting and ergonomic desktop screen-tilting poses without thermal sag.
2. **Weather-resistant sealing:** Sealed case seams, rubber grommet wire exits, and printed shoulder side protectors (`28.2 × 19.7 × 58.4 mm`) shield the actuators and harnesses from damp ground and outdoor dust.
3. **Ball-bearing joint stiffness:** Every hip-roll pod opposes its `25T` servo horn with a flanged **`F688ZZ`** (`8×16×5 mm`) ball bearing on the shoulder axis, while the pocket-trussed **`1.74×`** four-bar linkage (`60.9 mm` carrier, `34.8 mm` flanged crank, `66.1 mm` curved coupler rod, and `110.0 mm` lower leg) rides on dual **`683ZZ`** (`3×7×3 mm`) ball bearings at the knee and coupler pivots.

## Next steps on the V3 bench

1. **Bench-test the `5.0"` DSI touch display + onboard Linux SBC thermal envelope** under combined Linux desktop usage, CSI camera inference, and Ainekio OS voice/memory workloads inside the louvered carapace.
2. **Validate a single `DS3235` + trussed four-bar corner module** for joint backlash, 4th-wire feedback linearity, peak `2S` current draw, and unloaded foot-swing velocity before printing all four legs.
3. **Export the shelled `337.9 mm` V3 Blender assembly to MuJoCo MJCF** using the CAD-to-MJX pipeline built for V2 ([Gait Development](/posts/gait-development/)) to train higher-speed trotting and screen-posing policies ahead of full chassis assembly.

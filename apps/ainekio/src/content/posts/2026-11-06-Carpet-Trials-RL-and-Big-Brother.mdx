Between November 2 and November 6, work moved from physical carpet walking trials and
ESP32-P4 camera/IMU odometry fixes to training a **200-million-step neural gait policy
in JAX 0.7.1 / Brax / MuJoCo MJX**—debugging both rapid miniature hopping and reverse
rear-end dragging ("scootybooty") along the way—and running that policy on the **650 g
physical V2 quadruped** for its first real-life neural walk on carpet. In parallel, I
cleaned up remote Big Brother delegation and local model serving in MetaHuman OS.

## First real-life neural network walk on the physical 650 g V2 robot

After exporting the full 376-mesh Blender V2 CAD assembly into MuJoCo MJCF and working
through the stride and posture failure modes below, I deployed the trained policy onto
the physical 12-servo Ainekio V2 quadruped on carpet. Under closed-loop neural control,
the robot walks forward and backs up cleanly without dragging its hindquarters:

## Quadruped RL in MuJoCo/MJX: from tiny hops and "scootybooty" to a centered 200M-step stride

To move past hand-tuned sine tables and pure imitation learning, I stood up a GPU
reinforcement-learning pipeline in **JAX 0.7.1 / Brax / MJX** combining ideas from
**Walk in the Park (DroQ)** and **MicroDuck RL**, verified it first on a **Stock Pupper
V3 MuJoCo baseline** (`+1.68 m` forward walk; [watch in the Gallery](/gallery/#pupper-v3-mujoco-baseline)),
and then imported the **full-CAD Ainekio V2 MJCF model** (`0.650 kg`, 12 servo
actuators, 8 passive hinges, and 4 planar four-bar loop-closure equality constraints).

Getting the closed-chain four-bar model to step naturally in both forward and reverse
took debugging two classic RL reward loopholes:

### 1. Failure mode #1: Rapid tiny hops (`35M`-step narrow-scale rollout)

In my first 35-million-step full-CAD run, conservative action scaling clamped joint
excursions so tightly that the policy realized it could match target forward speed by
buzzing its feet in rapid miniature hops (`20–27 mm` fore-aft swing) instead of picking
up its legs for a real stride:

### 2. Failure mode #2: Asymmetric split stance & "scootybooty" reverse dragging (`200M`-step Run #4)

Widening the action scale cured the tiny hops, but in the next 200-million-step run
(`Run #4`), the policy parked its rear legs in a permanent split stance (`RL` camped
forward at `-54.3 .. -30.7 mm`, `RR` camped back at `-92.3 .. -72.0 mm`). Worse, as soon
as it got a reverse command (`-0.10 m/s`), the robot squatted its tail down to
**`0.044 m`** and scooted its rear shell backward across the floor—immediately dubbed
**"scootybooty"**—rather than lifting its hind feet:

### 3. Resolved 200M-step centered-stride & upright reverse policy (`Run #5`)

Penalizing off-center foot parking and shaping air-time and torso pitch across a fresh
200-million-step MJX run (`Run #5`) wiped out both habits:

- **Episode foot air-time** jumped **`3.5×`** (from `157.29` in Run #4 to **`557.11`** in Run #5).
- **Episode evaluation reward** reached **`28.97 ± 13.18`**, with **`0.0` body collisions** and **`0.0` knee collisions**.
- **Fore-aft foot stride (`X_body`)** widened **`2.2×–2.8×`** to **`56.6–63.7 mm`** across all four legs, centered cleanly around each leg's home stance (`-59.2 mm` rear, `+74.2 mm` front).
- **Upright reverse walking:** During `-0.10 m/s` reverse commands, final torso height improved from `0.044 m` to **`0.072 m`** (`0.060 m` minimum rear clearance) with zero rear dragging.

## Physical carpet trials and onboard OV5647 camera tuning

Before deploying the 200M-step MJX policy, November 2 carpet walking trials logged
**601 physical state transitions** under load while recording live video from the
lower-jaw OV5647 camera:

- **Watch the onboard camera footage:** [November 2 Physical Carpet Trial in the Gallery](/gallery/#carpet-trial-20261102)

Watching real carpet footage made it obvious how easily slow shutter speeds, loose frame
timestamps, and idealized workspace limits trip up visual odometry and leg protection,
driving four concrete firmware fixes on the ESP32-P4:

| Subsystem | Physical sensing & control fix |
| --- | --- |
| **Wake-word voice window** | Configurable **15-second** Espressif **WakeNet9s / VAD** capture window after wake-word detection |
| **Motion shutter cap** | Caps OV5647 exposure at **10,000 µs (10 ms)** while moving so blurred carpet texture doesn't smear landmark tracking |
| **Frame + IMU timestamp sync** | Stamps every frame at exact end-of-exposure and interpolates IMU quaternions (SLERP) for **OpenGV 2-point RANSAC** depth odometry |
| **Cartesian workspace projection** | Projects out-of-reach foot requests onto the calibrated four-bar boundary so loaded legs settle smoothly instead of stalling |

## Remote Big Brother motion synthesis and companion host cleanup

Alongside local Qwen LoRA serving via `llama.cpp`, Freestyle can now escalate novel or
tricky motion requests over SSH to a larger **Big Brother** model—either a local test
model on a GPU workstation or Codex in fast mode—while keeping the lightweight local
adapter ready for instant offline moves.

On the companion host, I also swept out accumulated scaffolding: merging the separate
terminal and chat views into one workspace, standardizing background coding delegation
and automated architecture checks, keeping robot traffic under the single Environment
Bridge, and replacing **257 thin API wrapper files** with a single catch-all HTTP
router.

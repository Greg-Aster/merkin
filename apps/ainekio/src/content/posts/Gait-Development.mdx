# Gait development journal

This is the working document for developing Ainekio's gait. It begins with
the original eight-servo robot, then compares that motion baseline with the
radically different twelve-servo linkage design. New calculations, experiments,
and physical results will be recorded here as dated entries.

**Current stage, September 21: calculated motion studies and compact-leg
probes.** The September 15 baseline below is preserved as the starting point.
Later entries cover [the motion library](#september-17-2026--motion-studies-and-the-software-library)
and [compact-leg probes](#september-19-2026--compact-leg-probes). Their results
belong to specific CAD revisions; physical gait performance and qualification
of the latest complete assembly remain open.

## September 15, 2026 — Establishing the movement baseline

At this entry's date, the prepared material covered movement ranges and
kinematic groundwork; the V2 gait had not yet been calculated. “Current” in
this entry refers to its September 15 source snapshot.

### 1. What the eight-servo Ainekio currently commands

V1 has two actuators per leg and eight stored joint channels. Its motion
library contains **38 named assets**, including standing, walking, turning,
and expressive poses. The following values were extracted from the current
`motions-v1.json` and checked against all 38 packed `.amot` files used by the
firmware asset loader.

There are three different quantities to keep track of: the controller's
accepted angle range, the angles actually used by a motion, and the assembled
leg's physical travel. The first two are available in these files. They do not
supply a measured foot workspace or the mechanical stops of every V1 joint.

The V1 software accepts logical angles from **0° to 180°**. The checked S3
configuration uses 100% motion range. Before output, each logical target is
mapped through the joint's calibrated center and direction and checked against
its calibrated minimum and maximum. With the default center of 90° and no
inversion, the mapping is unchanged; installed calibration can differ.

#### Joint ranges in the stored motions

All columns below use **V1 logical degrees**. “All motions” is the minimum and
maximum explicit target across the 38 assets, including emotes. “Walk” is
`walk_forward`, including its setup and final stand pose. These are separate
target envelopes, not a set of limits to send to the new body.

| ID · joint | All motions | Stand | Walk | Walk excursion from stand |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 0 · R1 | 85–180° | 135° | 90–180° | −45 to +45° |
| 1 · R2 | 0–100° | 45° | 45–100° | 0 to +55° |
| 2 · L1 | 0–95° | 45° | 0–90° | −45 to +45° |
| 3 · L2 | 90–180° | 135° | 90–135° | −45 to 0° |
| 4 · R4 | 0–180° | 0° | 0–45° | 0 to +45° |
| 5 · R3 | 0–180° | 180° | 135–180° | −45 to 0° |
| 6 · L3 | 0–180° | 0° | 0–45° | 0 to +45° |
| 7 · L4 | 0–180° | 180° | 135–180° | −45 to 0° |

The unusual **R4-before-R3** order is the actual version-1 channel map.
The all-90° rest/neutral command is also different from the stand pose above.
Neither establishes the neutral geometry of V2.

[Download the per-motion, per-joint range table (CSV)](/posts/gait-development/2026-09-15/v1-motion-ranges.csv).
It includes all 38 assets rather than only the walking subset shown here.

#### Walking targets and timing are separate

The existing forward pattern has six repeated target groups. A joint omitted
from a group keeps its preceding target. The group labels below preserve the
source channel names rather than assigning them to V2 parts.

| Group | Changed V1 targets, logical degrees | Stored duration |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| 1 | R3 = 135; L3 = 0 | 140 ms |
| 2 | L4 = 135; L2 = 90; R4 = 0; R1 = 180 | 180 ms |
| 3 | R2 = 45; L1 = 90 | 140 ms |
| 4 | R4 = 45; L4 = 180 | 140 ms |
| 5 | R3 = 180; L3 = 45; R2 = 90; L1 = 0 | 180 ms |
| 6 | L2 = 135; R1 = 90 | 140 ms |

One repetition totals **920 ms**. The stored `walk_forward` asset contains
five repetitions, a 180 ms setup, and a 160 ms final stand frame: **4,940 ms
across 32 frames**. This is a timed command sequence, not a measurement of
distance covered or foot contact duration.

| V1 asset | Repeated six-group pattern | Pattern repetitions | Complete stored asset |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `walk_forward` | 920 ms | 5 | 4,940 ms · 32 frames |
| `walk_slow` | 1,840 ms | 3 | 6,740 ms · 21 frames |
| `run` | 720 ms | 6 | 5,180 ms · 39 frames |

The two cadence variants keep the forward-walk target envelope. Their complete
durations include their own entry and exit frames; a longer total asset does
not necessarily mean a slower repeated pattern. The name `run` describes the
stored command and does not establish an airborne running gait.

The checked S3 profile interpolates addressed joints with cubic easing on a
20 ms update tick, using a transition window of at most 300 ms within each
frame. That playback behavior matters when interpreting the older sequence
data. It does not supply V2's required timing or loaded servo speed.

### 2. What changes in the twelve-servo mechanism

V2 has three actuators per leg, but its change is larger than adding a sideways
joint to the old pose arrays. Two planar motors control a carrier and a
linkage-driven lower assembly; a third rotates the leg sideways at the
shoulder. **The lower assembly's angle is not equal to its driving servo's
angle.** The new gait needs a geometric conversion between desired foot
positions and all three actuator angles.

![Labeled front-left linkage at neutral: the 21 mm crank drives a 40 mm coupler and two-armed rocker; a 40 mm carrier and return link position the 60 mm lower assembly.](/posts/gait-development/2026-09-15/mechanism.png)

*Supplied September 15 mechanism diagram. Dimensions join pivot centers. F is
the foot attachment-bolt datum, not the point where the sole touches ground.*

#### Parts and angle definitions

| Variable | Part / role | Reference and direction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `alpha` | Part_006 drives the Part_023 upper carrier | Change from saved frame 1, about CAD +Z |
| `theta` | Part_005 drives the Part_009 crank and Part_024 coupler | Change from the current frame-1 horn position, about CAD +Z |
| `beta` | Passive Part_025 rocker and lower-assembly orientation | Change from frame 1, about CAD +Z; determined by `theta` |
| `h` | Part_002 shoulder swivel | Change from frame 1, about CAD +X |

Part_023 and Part_025 share pivot O but rotate independently. Part_006 drives
the carrier; Part_025 follows the other motor through C–P–Q–O. Holding
`theta` fixed holds the lower assembly's planar orientation while `alpha`
moves its upper attachment. The bend between the upper and lower segments
therefore changes with `beta − alpha`, plus the neutral geometry offset.

| Pivot pair | Front-left dimension | Function |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| O–C | 16.9853 mm | Fixed spacing between the two planar servo axes |
| C–P | 21 mm | Input crank, Part_009 |
| P–Q | 40 mm | Current front-left Part_024 coupler |
| O–Q and O–B | 20 mm each | Two arms of the Part_025 rocker |
| O–D and B–E | 40 mm each | Carrier and return link |
| D–E | 20 mm | Lower assembly's return-link arm |
| D–F | 60.0003 mm, nominally 60 mm | Distal pivot to foot-bolt datum |

The saved assembly is currently a mixture of revisions:

| CAD leg label | Part_024 pivot spacing | Motion evidence in this handoff |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| FL | 40.0000 mm | 505 saved frames covering separate planar sweeps |
| FR | 24.6395 mm | Neutral assembly only |
| RL | 24.6395 mm | Neutral assembly only |
| RR | 24.6395 mm | Neutral assembly only |

These are the literal Blender labels. Their correspondence to physical
front/back and hardware channels still needs confirmation. In the handoff's
chosen coordinate frame, the objects labeled FL/FR are at negative longitudinal
coordinates. The journal retains that convention.

The 40 mm FL linkage is a later revision than the short-link fit discussed in
the [printed-leg update](/posts/2026-09-15-leg-prototypes/). It must not be
silently applied to the other three legs. The replacement also re-clocked the
FL input horn by 46.4615°; the new `theta = 0` already includes that change.

### 3. Theoretical movement ranges of the current FL leg

The prepared handoff records the following **single-actuator animation
intervals**. They describe the modeled range study; they are not newly proven
absolute maximum travel or measured physical limits. All angles are relative
to the saved neutral pose, with the definitions above.

| Motion | Interval | Angular span | Other inputs held fixed |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Upper-carrier input `alpha` | −53.4000 to +67.4000° | 120.8000° | `theta = 0`, `h = 0` |
| Linkage-crank input `theta` | −51.9533 to +51.6303° | 103.5837° | `alpha = 0`, `h = 0` |
| Resulting lower orientation `beta` | −48.1011 to +25.8715° | 73.9727° | Output of the `theta` sweep |
| Shoulder input `h` | Not established | Not established | Shoulder was not animated |

The `beta` row is a passive output, not a fourth actuator or a direct measure
of the knee bend across all carrier positions. Likewise, subtracting the
extreme `alpha` and `beta` values would combine separate tests and would not
establish a usable bending range.

The replacement's saved verification reports no new modeled contacts across
those frames relative to the existing mating overlaps, using a 0.03 mm³
added-overlap criterion. **The two input intervals do not define a proven
collision-free rectangle.** Combined carrier, crank, and shoulder motion still
needs checking against the links, screws, housings, chassis, other legs, and
ground. No sweep limits for FR, RL, or RR are established by this package.

#### Why a fixed servo-to-knee scale will not work

Evaluating the supplied forward-kinematics function at a few FL crank angles
gives the following lower-assembly changes, with the carrier and shoulder held
at neutral:

| Crank input `theta` | Lower orientation `beta` |
| ---: | ---: |
| −51.9533° | −48.1011° |
| −30° | −24.6516° |
| 0° | 0° |
| +30° | +17.4942° |
| +51.6303° | +25.8715° |

Equal positive and negative crank changes produce different output changes.
A constant multiplier or an old V1 target array would lose that relationship.
These sample calculations establish geometry, not motor torque or performance.

### 5. Geometry to carry into the first gait calculation

The handoff uses CAD X longitudinally, CAD Y downward, and CAD Z laterally.
One Blender coordinate unit represents 1 mm. The supplied planning conversion
makes body Z point upward:

```text
B = (92.899998, -5.850000, -21.000000) mm in CAD coordinates
x_body =  X_CAD - B.x
y_body =  Z_CAD - B.z
z_body = -(Y_CAD - B.y)
```

B is the midpoint of the leg-placement datums, not the measured center of
mass. The model named `world` has its own scale; it must not be applied again
to the independently rooted robot assembly.

The following is the planar forward model from the handoff. Vectors come from
the individual leg's neutral pivots; angles inside the equations are radians.
`R(t)` is a two-dimensional rotation by `t`.

```text
P = C + R(theta) (P0 - C)
v = P - O
d = length(v)
a = length(Q0 - O)
L = length(P0 - Q0)            # this leg's coupler length
c = (a² + d² - L²) / (2 a d)
psi = atan2(v.y, v.x) - acos(c)
beta = wrap(psi - atan2((Q0-O).y, (Q0-O).x))

D = O + R(alpha) (D0 - O)
F_XY = D + R(beta) (F0_XY - D0)
F_CAD = H + Rx(h) ((F_XY.x, F_XY.y, F0.z) - H)
```

H is a point on the shoulder axis. The minus-`acos` solution is the current
assembly branch; impossible circle intersections must be rejected and branch
continuity checked along any future trajectory. These mathematical signs are
not yet the electrical signs of the twelve installed actuators.

At neutral, the FL foot-bolt datum is at CAD
**(53.7795, 65.5118, 42.7000) mm**, with a **21.7000 mm lateral offset** from
its shoulder axis. The sole reaches about 9.6 mm farther downward at that pose.
The sole rotates with the lower assembly, so subtracting a fixed vertical
9.6 mm at every angle would misplace the contact point. Foot pitch also cannot
be assigned independently after solving the foot position.

The supplied IK function returns geometric `(h, alpha, theta)` candidates.
It does not decide which are within calibrated limits, collision-free, stable,
or achievable at the requested speed. The changing linkage ratio must also
enter the velocity calculation: a Jacobian maps joint rates to endpoint
velocity. [Modern Robotics explains that velocity relationship](https://modernrobotics.northwestern.edu/nu-gm-book-resource/5-1-1-space-jacobian/).

### 6. First experiment to define — no gait result yet

The next calculation needs a specific geometry and contact model before it
needs a gait name. The following questions remain open for this journal:

| Input or decision | Current state |
| --- | --- |
| Geometry for all four legs | Confirm whether the next assembly uses four 40 mm couplers or retains mixed lengths |
| Hardware mapping | Confirm leg labels, all twelve channels, directions, neutral settings, and usable limits |
| Nominal stance | Body height, foot spacing, sole/contact reference, and body orientation not selected |
| Foot and body paths | Stride displacement, swing clearance, and body translation not calculated |
| Timing | Period, stance fraction, per-leg phase offsets, and speed limits not selected |
| Support and loading | Mass, center of mass, payload, contact friction/compliance, and loaded servo capability needed |
| Combined travel | Continuous multi-actuator collision and ground-contact checks outstanding |

For a no-slip stance model, a planted contact stays fixed in the world while
its coordinates relative to the moving body change. Swing then brings the
foot to its next contact. The contact assumptions belong in the calculation,
alongside geometry and timing; they cannot be inferred from a pose animation.
[MIT's treatment of planning through contact provides the background](https://underactuated.mit.edu/contact.html).

The first trial record should identify the exact leg revision, candidate
stance and path, solved joint sequence, any rejected poses, contact assumptions,
and the outcome. Later physical entries can add observed fit, slipping,
clearance, repeatability, and loaded behavior. Until those experiments are
performed, this entry remains the range comparison that precedes them.

## September 19, 2026 — Compact-leg probes

The next study used a **22 mm crank, 36 mm rod, 40 mm upper carrier, 24 mm
pickup and 55 mm lower-leg projection**. It applied to one physical front-left
leg, labeled RL in that revision, with the shoulder fixed at zero. The
[compact-leg post](/posts/2026-09-21-compact-leg-design/) gives the design and
print-fit history.

### Starting target and contact assumptions

The selected research target was **20 mm world advance per eight-second
cycle**, with a 5 mm sole-clearance target and one second of swing. That gives
an 87.5% stance fraction. The 10 and 30 mm cases bracket the selected target;
none is an optimized or hardware-approved stride.

| Research quantity | 10 mm advance | 20 mm advance | 30 mm advance |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Cycle time | 8 s | 8 s | 8 s |
| Stance fraction | 87.5% | 87.5% | 87.5% |
| Body-relative stance bolt sweep | 8.75 mm | 17.50 mm | 26.25 mm |
| Sole-clearance target | 5 mm | 5 mm | 5 mm |
| Carrier `alpha` | −6.64 to +5.22° | −10.37 to +10.69° | −14.35 to +16.33° |
| Crank `theta` | −8.04 to +7.92° | −16.06 to +15.62° | −23.95 to +22.96° |
| Peak estimated carrier speed | 31.3°/s | 50.8°/s | 71.9°/s |
| Peak estimated crank speed | 34.7°/s | 69.3°/s | 103.9°/s |

These angles are offsets from the compact neutral, not V1 logical degrees or
PWM values. In this model, the stance bolt sweep equals world advance times
the stance fraction: **20 × 0.875 = 17.5 mm**. The assumed body speed for that
case is 20 / 8 = 2.5 mm/s. This is an input to the isolated calculation, not
measured robot speed.

The sole height was obtained from the rotated full foot-shell geometry rather
than a fixed offset below the bolt. That improves the lift calculation, but
the stance model still holds the bolt's world X stationary under assumed
constant body speed. A rotating rounded foot can move its contact point, so
this does not prove a no-slip stance.

### Recorded results and their limits

Each case contains 161 target poses, for **483 poses in total**. The record
reports closure on the existing linkage branch and return to the starting
joint angles. Sampled checks against the other link plates, servo cases,
mount, horn references and foot shells found no intersection above 0.02 mm³.
Intended hardware interfaces and the rest of the body were excluded.

The saved-file check evaluated 22 Blender poses per case and found a maximum
sole-height difference from the target of about 0.000021 mm. These small
numerical errors describe agreement within the model. They do not measure
print accuracy or actual foot tracking.

Whole-body shifts, support margin, stance slip, continuous collisions, loaded
servo speed and torque, calibrated travel and print strength were not verified.
The probes were not installed as a complete walking animation. Later upright
assemblies retain the compact lengths, but their full-body gait needs its own
validation rather than inheriting these single-leg results.

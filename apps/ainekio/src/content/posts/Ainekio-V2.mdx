# The V2 build plan

V2 adds one joint to each leg, a new chassis, and an ESP32-P4 controller. It keeps
MG90S-class servos and starts with scripted motion. The aim is a twelve-servo
body that moves reliably and is easy to service.

This plan follows the owner's September 6 design document and mechanical files
reviewed on September 7. An experimental leg print is underway. Final dimensions,
electronics, materials, and power source depend on the tests below.

| Controller | ESP32-S3 N16R8 | ESP32-P4 for body timing, safety, and peripherals |
| Motion | Scripted poses and sequences | Twelve-joint sequences with calibration and smooth transitions |
| Power | Existing 5 V / 5 A servo supply | Supply chosen from measured loads |

MetaHuman will continue to request named actions through the gateway. The body
controller will translate them into joint movement.

## Legs and chassis

Each leg will have hip roll, hip pitch, and knee joints. Hip roll adds stance-width
adjustment and sideways foot placement, with scope for body lean and expressive
poses. Automatic balance and terrain response require further work; speed is
not the initial priority.

The experimental leg uses OpenHarmony Puppy V2 as a reference for its lightweight
MG90S layout, supported pivots, and linkage with actuator mass close to the body.
Local files include STL models and converted STEP/Blender assemblies. The tests
will determine how those ideas fit Ainekio's shorter, broader proportions.

The chassis will be designed in Blender around the leg mounts, controller,
connectors, camera, audio hardware, display, power source, and wiring. Priorities
are a low center of gravity, short leg leverage, strong mounts, and cable
clearance throughout movement. Programming, charging, power connections, legs,
and electronics should remain accessible for replacement and repair.

## Controller and motion

The plan favors generating twelve servo signals directly on the P4, subject to
board pin and peripheral tests. A separate PWM board is optional. P4 firmware,
communication hardware, pin assignments, and camera/audio integration still
need implementation; the S3 wiring map is specific to V1.

Initial motion data will contain twelve target positions per frame, plus timing
and interpolation. Planned controls include:

- center calibration and direction inversion;
- minimum and maximum angles;
- velocity and acceleration limits;
- safe rest poses and staged startup/stand-up sequences.

Existing movements can begin with the four lateral joints held neutral. New
joint movement can then be introduced selectively. Walk, stop, stand, sit, bow,
and wave remain examples of higher-level requests; the controller handles
sequencing, limits, and stopping.

The current body core, motion assets, protocol validators, and
MetaHuman motion-plan schema use eight joints. They must agree on joint names,
limits, and capabilities before accepting twelve-joint plans.

## Power

The owner reports that V1 works on its existing 5 V / 5 A supply. The design note
proposes measuring that robot first, then evaluating a 5–6 A rail under V2 loads.
Adding four servos requires new measurements.

Controller and servo power will branch near the supply with common ground.
Servo current will bypass the P4 board. A bulk capacitor near the servo rail can
support brief transients; the supply must still sustain the load. Tests will
include smooth acceleration and staged movement.

Battery or power-bank selection depends on continuous 5 V output, startup and
motion transients, voltage stability, and temperature. Advertised USB-PD wattage
alone is insufficient.

## Build and test sequence

1. Print and assemble one leg. Measure fit, horn alignment, travel, interference,
   stiffness, backlash, bearing support, printed weight, and foot workspace.
2. Measure V1 power use and adapt the leg geometry.
3. Test P4 signals and calibration, then one loaded leg, two legs, and all twelve
   servos.
4. Design the chassis and harness around the measured parts and power needs.
5. Port scripted movements and tune transitions before the final complete print.
6. Repeat standing, sitting, walking, turning, and stopping. Check for brownouts,
   supply shutdown, binding, and near-stall operation, plus controller headroom
   and service access.

## Later options

Adaptive terrain response, foot-contact sensing, closed-loop balance, IMU
stabilization, real-time inverse kinematics, and learned locomotion are deferred.
Higher-cost servos, required onboard phone/Linux compute, and wireless charging
are also outside the initial build.

The [MetaHuman redesign](/posts/metahuman-integration/) can be developed against
V1 while these mechanics are tested. [Current Status](/posts/current-status/)
tracks both projects and their test results.

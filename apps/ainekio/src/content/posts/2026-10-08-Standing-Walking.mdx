Two days after the first chassis failed its standing test, the rebuilt twelve-servo
robot can **stand, walk, and respond to commands**. The lighter frame, 35/20/28 mm
compact leg linkage, and discrete battery arrangement from the
[October 6 rebuild](/posts/2026-10-06-compact-linkage/) put the physical robot on
its feet with its wide cyan LCD face lit and running.

## Two days of hardware progress

| Milestone | October 6 | October 8 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Standing on MG90 servos | Could not stand with heavy power bank and 40 mm carriers | Stands cleanly on the 35/20/28 mm linkage |
| Locomotion | Replacement parts still printing | Walking on the physical assembly |

| Display & camera | Unmounted in chassis test | 1.9-inch ST7789 LCD (320×170 landscape) and OV5647 camera upright and active |

## 1.9-inch ST7789 face renderer and 59 expressions

The physical robot uses a **XIITIA 1.9-inch ST7789** SPI panel in **320 × 170
landscape** orientation (wired via SPI despite its PCB pins being silkscreened `SCL`
and `SDA`; see the [LCD wiring guide](https://github.com/Greg-Aster/Ainekio-bot/blob/dbd15b9/docs/v2-12servo/LCD_FACE.md)).

The C firmware renderer (`faces.def` and `face.c`) drives **59 selectable
expressions** at a stepped **15 fps** (66 ms frame interval) outside the servo task,
including resting eyes, speaking voice-bar overlays, workflow status halos, and
motion-linked faces:

### Onboard camera, speaker, and wake-word fixes

- **Display & camera orientation (`0.8.3`):** Confirmed upright LCD and fresh camera
  stills on the assembled head; removed the connected text footer in favor of compact
  corner fault icons.
- **Speaker & wake-word capture (`0.8.4`–`0.8.6`):** Verified speaker tone output and
  fixed wake-word audio assembly by emitting a recording-start event alongside the
  wake trigger. Increasing the microphone task stack eliminated a live inference
  overflow and left 3,712 bytes of free stack margin across a 60-second continuous check.
- **Token-scoped workflow faces:** Temporary workflow expressions (`Thinking`,
  `Listening`) now carry a unique selection identity and robot-local timeout so stale
  release messages cannot clobber a newer expression.

## Responsive command routing and specialist split in MetaHuman OS

Alongside the hardware bring-up, October 7–8 commits in MetaHuman OS ([`f466f0b3`](https://github.com/Greg-Aster/ainekio-os/commit/f466f0b3)
and [`273bbe58`](https://github.com/Greg-Aster/ainekio-os/commit/273bbe58)) and the
gateway ([`dbd15b9`](https://github.com/Greg-Aster/Ainekio-bot/commit/dbd15b9))
separated request routing, task planning, and conversation:

| Responsibility | Earlier path | October 8 architecture |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Request routing | Context selection and action/reply coupled | Fast initial request pass selects context for task and conversation consumers |
| Task planning vs reply | Shared response generation path | Dedicated **Task specialist** produces an ordered action program; **Conversation** runs on its own workflow and streams speech chunks as they synthesize |
| Model selection | Single global backend | Per-node model assignment with separate **Qwen3.5 0.8B** Intent and Task LoRA training lanes and larger-model planner delegation |
| Input during active motion | Slow interpretation could block active work | Read-only interpretation runs in the background while the active execution retains feedback and manual-takeover guards |
| Connection selector | Manual bridge configuration | Saved startup and dashboard selectors (`This computer`, `LAN / Wi-Fi`, `Cloudflare`) in both Body Control and MetaHuman's Agent Monitor |

An initial local YOLO adapter (`person_yolov8m-seg.pt`) was also wired into the
gateway camera worker, benchmarking at a 210 ms median (4.64 fps on 4 desktop CPU
threads) ahead of the full open-vocabulary YOLOE upgrade in the
[October 12 update](/posts/2026-10-12-shell-power-and-motion/).

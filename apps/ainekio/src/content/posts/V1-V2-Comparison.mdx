# Eight servos versus twelve

V2 changes the mechanism, controller and power arrangement. The third actuator
on each leg adds sideways placement; the new software can vary a gait while it
runs. This comparison separates those implemented capabilities from the
physical results still to be measured. It is a September 24 snapshot of a
continuing build.

## Mechanics and motion capability

| Comparison | V1 · eight servos | V2 · twelve servos |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Actuators per leg | Two MG90S | Three; current physical selection recorded as MG90, exact variant still to identify |
| Mechanism | Sesame-derived leg and open chassis | Shoulder roll plus coupled carrier/crank linkage; purpose-built supports, shell and underside |
| Foot placement | Primarily forward/backward and up/down | Adds sideways placement and variable stance width |
| Motion generation | Stored poses and timed sequences; 38 named assets | Continuous Walk, Run, Crawl and six-direction Crab, plus 23 declared gestures/postures |
| Speed controls | Timing of existing sequences | Normal Speed control; independent stride 1–100% and cadence 0.25–3× in advanced mode |
| Turns | Stored 45°, 90° and 180° left/right clips; open-loop heading estimates | Ongoing gait turns or finite cycle counts; fixed-angle V2 clips retired |
| Expressive movement | Wave, bow, dances and other prepared gestures | Retargeted gestures plus reviewed Worm, seated Shrug, Play Dead, Lay Down and experimental Upright |
| Balance and terrain | No measured adaptive balance result | Contact/geometry planning implemented; no demonstrated loaded dynamic balance or terrain adaptation |
| Physical evidence | Working complete prototype; movement and audio demonstrated | Printed leg prototypes and P4 bench work; current application flashed and booted September 24; full-body loaded walking unverified |

The counts describe different things: one V2 gait can continue indefinitely and
change settings, while a V1 asset is a finite stored sequence. Twenty-three
finite gestures therefore does not mean V2 has fewer movement options.

## Movement ranges: compare the quantities first

**A commanded angle is not a measured mechanical limit.** V1 uses logical
controller angles. V2 uses signed CAD angles for three coupled inputs. Their
zeros, joint axes and mechanisms differ; subtracting the numbers does not give
a meaningful percentage increase in reachable workspace.

| Range or movement | V1 | V2 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Software angle convention | Logical 0–180°; individual assets use subsets | Signed CAD angles for shoulder, carrier and crank, followed by per-joint calibration |
| Stored/sample envelope | Combined explicit targets span 0–180° across the eight channels | Shoulder −96.99 to +17.57°; carrier −79.02 to +135.37°; crank −128.11 to +101.29° |
| Walking data | V1 forward-walk targets use 45–90° excursions on individual channels; detailed table in the journal | Sampled current forward-walk recording: shoulder −4.47 to +4.25°, carrier −46.05 to +22.88°, crank −107.48 to +29.56° |
| Sideways travel | No dedicated third leg actuator; no measured lateral workspace supplied | Crab planner specifies 32 mm sideways travel per full-stride cycle |
| Foot travel in walking | No measured distance-per-cycle result in this review | 92 mm full planted sweep; nominal 131.43 mm advance per cycle at full stride |
| Whole-body usable limits | Installed stops, loaded workspace and travel measurements not supplied | Shoulder/body clearance, tracking, load and all-settings motion envelope remain unqualified |

The V2 envelope above was extracted afresh from **23 declared gesture sources,
14 locomotion recordings and the Run recording**, pooling all four legs by
input type. It excludes the retired fixed-angle turn recordings. Its shoulder
maximum therefore differs from older audits that included those turns.
The forward-walk row includes its recorded Speed changes, advanced settings and
Finish; it is not the extrema of every possible walking control setting.

[Download the per-recording, per-joint CSV](/posts/2026-09-24-progress/motion-ranges.csv)
and its [read-only extraction script](/posts/2026-09-24-progress/extract-motion-ranges.py).
The CSV includes source hashes, preserves the two angle conventions and also
lists all V1 explicit target ranges. The [gait journal](/posts/gait-development/)
retains the original V1 channel table and earlier linkage studies.

The current V2 linkage uses a **40/24/38/24 mm four-bar and 55 mm distal link**.
A recorded pulse span of 300–2900 µs and provisional 234° conversion are
calibration research inputs, not measured shaft travel or approved assembled
motion limits. Some original poses still conflict with modeled clearance or
reference pulse spans. Neither a larger servo-angle range nor a passing
linkage-closure calculation proves that the whole robot can execute that pose.

## Controller and processing

| Comparison | V1 | V2 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Board | Freenove ESP32-S3-WROOM CAM, N16R8 | Waveshare ESP32-P4-WIFI6; tested P4 revision 1.3 |
| Main processor | Dual-core Xtensa LX7 | Dual-core high-performance RISC-V |
| Checked firmware clock | **160 MHz** in local S3 configuration | **360 MHz** in local P4 configuration |
| Flash / PSRAM | 16 MB / 8 MB | 32 MB / 32 MB |
| Wireless | S3's own 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi | Separate ESP32-C6 coprocessor over SDIO |
| Servo signals | Eight direct hardware PWM outputs | PCA9685 driver for the twelve channels |
| Camera/audio hardware | OV3660 parallel camera; external microphone and I2S amplifier | Kit specifies OV5647 MIPI camera; onboard codec/audio path; installed peripheral operation needs its own evidence |
| Conversation and reasoning | MetaHuman OS on a separate computer | MetaHuman OS remains separate from the body controller |

Processor families and board interfaces follow
[Espressif's S3 description](https://www.espressif.com/en/products/socs/esp32-s3)
and the [Waveshare board documentation](https://docs.waveshare.com/ESP32-P4-WIFI6).
Memory and clock values above also follow the local board records and checked
`sdkconfig` files. The S3 silicon supports up to 240 MHz; this checkout selects
160 MHz. These clock rates and memory capacities are **not an application
benchmark** or evidence of longer battery life.

The concrete software change is that the P4 now solves the leg linkage directly
and plays compact gesture curves through one output controller. The September
24 deployment record reports a **2,369,264-byte application**, a verified flash,
boot and authenticated gateway connection. The prior bench work used two
unloaded servos without linkages. Loaded twelve-servo timing, tracking and
power are separate measurements still to make.

## Battery, charging and running time

| Comparison | V1 | V2 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Owner-reported battery | 1,000 mAh LiPo | 10,000 mAh Anker PowerCore Slim |
| Voltage / energy record | Earlier parts record: 2S, 7.4 V; nominal 7.4 Wh by multiplication | Exact label voltage and rated Wh still to record |
| Conversion arrangement | External buck converter | Removable bank output; no separate buck converter |
| Charging | External charger | Integrated bank charging; plug-in Qi add-on planned |
| Packaging | Existing robot arrangement | Underside being fitted around a 155 × 72 × 16 mm CAD proxy |
| Measured run time | **Not measured** | **Not measured** |
| Measured loaded consumption | Not supplied | Not supplied; power monitoring is not ready in the current P4 record |

The capacity labels differ by ten times, but they may use different nominal
voltages. They cannot establish ten times the running time. A useful estimate
requires usable delivered energy in watt-hours and average whole-robot power:
**runtime in hours ≈ delivered Wh ÷ average W**. Idle, walking and expressive
motions will need separately identified test conditions. The bank's peak
output capability also needs checking against the assembled load.

The [battery fit entry](/posts/2026-09-24-body-and-battery/) records the actual
placement experiments, exclusions and outstanding holder work. Greg confirmed
the battery arrangements and that neither runtime had been measured on
September 24; that owner report takes precedence over older chemistry labels.

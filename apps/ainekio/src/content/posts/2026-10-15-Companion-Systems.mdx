On October 15 I cleaned up two things that kept rubbing against each other during live
voice and floor tests: making sure only one model voice ever speaks for the robot, and
tying physical walking commands to real camera and IMU progress instead of blind timers.

## Ten model roles and one owner for outward speech

Workflows and model settings now route across ten clear specialist roles:

| Category | Responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| **Orchestrator** | Interpret requests and events; select routes, tools, and context |
| **Task Decisions** | Select executable body/sensing actions and task controls |
| **Planner** | Develop and revise multi-step plans |
| **Persona** | Express identity, personality, conversation, and self-directed intentions |
| **Psychotherapist** | Independently assess attributed experience and update long-term persona |
| **Vision** | Interpret camera images and support visually grounded planning |
| **Curator** | Review and prepare attributed memory and training records |
| **Coder** | Work on code and technical tasks |
| **Summarizer** | Condense history while preserving meaning and attribution |
| **Embedder** | Represent records for vector retrieval and similarity search |

### Stripping speech out of the Task planner

Following yesterday's [speech/action confusion on the 0.8B planner](/posts/2026-10-14-startup-and-leveling/),
I removed spoken lines from the Task model completely. Task now picks only physical
postures, gaits, or camera actions; Goal Review checks the resulting sensor telemetry;
and **Persona** is the only path that writes conversation history or speaks out loud
through the wake-word voice loop. Retraining the specialist dataset (6,358 training,
1,518 evaluation, and 187 regression examples) around that strict action-only schema
stopped spoken figures of speech from accidentally triggering leg movements.

### Grounding desires and memory in physical outcomes

Autonomous reflection also needed a reality check against the physical body. Desire
review now sees live robot status and actual execution outcomes—so if the robot tried
to walk toward something and couldn't, it recognizes the mismatch rather than assuming
the action succeeded. Every stored memory keeps strict attribution (who spoke, when,
and whether an episode happened in the physical room or during an internal reflection
pass), while bursty sensor events coalesce cleanly instead of piling up duplicate
workflow resumes.

Quick diagnostic LoRA runs on a **Qwen3.5 9B** base also confirmed that tiny 4–5 sample
seed sets aren't enough to replace explicit persona context without muddling memory
attribution, so larger curated transcripts will come first before promoting 9B adapters.

## Goal-bearing locomotion and lateral drift correction

Instead of firing open-ended Walk or Turn commands and guessing when to stop, walking
tasks now pair each movement with a concrete sensor check across the camera and IMU:

| Locomotion goal | Progress & completion evidence |
| --- | --- |
| **Distance** | Measured linear displacement in an identified reference frame |
| **Relative turn** | Signed yaw change relative to the starting orientation |
| **Approach** | Reaching a specified relationship to a tracked visual target |
| **Follow** | Maintaining an ongoing target relationship until an ending condition |
| **Visual endpoint** | Camera observations evaluated against completion criteria |

If the needed camera or IMU signal isn't available at the start, the task declines to
move; if tracking drops mid-walk, it brings the gait to a clean standing finish rather
than marching blindly on a timer. On the ESP32-P4, the gait controller also gained
signed sideways trim so the robot can nudge back onto centerline during visual
approaches.

## Livelier gaits and a faster seated Wave

Over Wi-Fi OTA, I bumped foot clearance and planted sweep across all four continuous
gaits on the `35 / 20 / 28 / 38 / 55 mm` linkage and staggered the PCA9685 PWM channel
phases so all twelve servos don't draw their current spikes on the exact same
microsecond:

| Gait parameter | Previous | Revised | Notes |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| **Walk** planted sweep | 32 mm | **37 mm** | +18.6% planned travel (`44.44 → 52.71 mm/s` at Speed 100, `0.9 s` cycle) |
| **Walk** lift (low / full) & stance | 4 / 8 mm (80%) | **6 / 10 mm (78%)** | Broader swing arch clears floor earlier; lateral shift reduced `9 → 7 mm` |
| **Run** planted stroke & lift | 40 mm / 2–8 mm | **44 mm / 5–12 mm** | Front/rear paired bound |
| **Crawl** planted stroke & lift | 18 mm / 2–4 mm | **32 mm / 5–8 mm** | +59.5% planned cycle travel on bare-leg contact |
| **Crab** fore/aft & sideways stroke | 36 mm / 24 mm | **40 mm / 32 mm** | Stance width narrowed `190 → 160 mm`; lift `8–14 mm` (+25% sideways travel) |

### Snappier seated Wave (`16.0 s → 6.2 s`)

Watching the robot wave in person made it obvious how sluggish the original 16-second
clip felt. Retiming seated **Wave** down to **6.2 seconds** and more than doubling the
front shoulder sweep made the gesture read immediately across the desk:

| Wave phase (at 1× speed) | Previous | Revised |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Seated entry complete | 3.0 s | **1.2 s** |
| Front arm lift complete | 4.2 s | **1.6 s** |
| Shoulder wave amplitude (3× waves) | ±18° | **±42°** |
| Standing recovery & total duration | 15.2 s / 16.0 s | **6.0 s / 6.2 s** |

### Live IMU leveling readouts and stationary gyro zeroing

Body Control now shows live roll/pitch angles, target error, and per-leg extension
deltas even while self-leveling is toggled off, with one-click buttons on the bench to
capture the current stance as level reference or average 256 stationary samples into
saved gyro bias without rebooting.

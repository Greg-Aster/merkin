With the assembled chassis weighing in at **566 g** on its **5 V supply** and twelve
MG90 servos, the October 13–14 engineering sprint focused on how the robot wakes up,
balances, services its firmware over Wi-Fi, and reacts when picked up or tipped.

## Staged startup rise (`2.5 s` crouch → shoulder align → stand)

Previously, booting drove all twelve servos straight toward calibration Home, which on
the floor could extend the lower legs before the shoulders were aligned. Ordinary
startup now runs a three-stage rise on the P4 (keeping calibration Home as a separate
explicit maintenance command):

1. Fold the carrier and crank joints into a compact crouch.
2. Align the four shoulders while holding the crouch.
3. Raise the body into Stand over the foot contacts, with the front and rear leg pairs
   staggered by **100 ms** across a **2.5-second** sequence.

## IMU self-leveling controls and integral hold

The P4 firmware (`0.8.46-p4-leveling-tuning`) now exposes persistent self-leveling
settings in Body Control, stored in NVS independently of joint and IMU calibration:

| Control | Function |
| --- | --- |
| On / Off | Enables attitude correction in supported standing and walking states |
| Response strength | Gain multiplier for roll/pitch attitude error (default `1×`) |
| Damping | Gain multiplier for gyro angular-rate damping (default `1×`) |
| Roll target | Desired roll offset in degrees (positive raises left side, default `0°`) |
| Pitch target | Desired pitch offset in degrees (positive raises nose, default `0°`) |

Early tilt checks revealed why pure proportional feedback felt weak while standing:
the standing hold settled at a fraction of a persistent tilt error. Stationary hold
now accumulates integral correction over time so the legs continue pushing against a
sustained tilt until the body reaches its target attitude.

### Linkage torque calculator: 55/35/38/28/20 mm vs 45/35/30/28/20 mm

To test whether shortening the lower leg and rod would ease motor load, we built a
standalone linkage calculator (`JSON`/`CSV`/`SVG` export) that compares link lengths
at fixed servo shaft locations. Comparing the installed **55 / 35 / 38 / 28 / 20 mm**
geometry (leg / carrier / rod / pickup / crank) against a shorter **45 / 35 / 30 / 28 / 20 mm**
study at a 65 mm shaft-height benchmark showed that while carrier peak torque drops
~3%, the shorter 30 mm rod approaches a steeper transmission angle as the body rises,
**increasing crank torque demand by ~16%**—confirming the 55 mm leg / 38 mm rod
configuration as the better mechanical baseline.

## Onboard SD video recording, Wi-Fi OTA updates, and logs

Firmware `0.8.37` makes the P4 fully serviceable over Wi-Fi without opening the shell:

<figure>
  <img src="/posts/2026-10-14-progress/recording-and-updates.svg" alt="One encoded camera image feeds both the existing preview and SD recorder. Body Control retrieves clips and logs, and uploads firmware for separate explicit activation." width="1000" height="640" loading="lazy" />
  <figcaption>Shared JPEG pipeline for live preview and SD recording, plus two-stage Wi-Fi OTA firmware updates and diagnostic log retrieval.</figcaption>
</figure>

- **Zero-reencode SD card recording:** The storage worker writes standard MJPEG AVI
  clips (`60 s` or `64 MiB` segments) directly from the camera's hardware-encoded
  JPEGs, with rolling overwrite of the oldest completed clip when full and concurrent
  HTTP clip downloads.
- **Two-stage Wi-Fi OTA with automatic rollback:** Uploading a P4 binary places the
  robot in Doze, finalizes any open recording, writes the inactive OTA slot, and
  validates the image. The operator then chooses **Activate on next boot** or
  **Activate and restart**. First boot verifies startup readiness and authenticated
  gateway connection before marking the image valid (with hardware-tested bootloader
  rollback on pre-acceptance reset).
- **Retained diagnostic logs:** Current and previous boot logs (including panic
  backtraces across warm resets) can be read or downloaded directly from Body Control.

### 469-second Wi-Fi stability and concurrent recording run

Keeping the ESP32-C6 radio awake, tolerating transient TCP ACK delays before tearing
down the socket, and using the WebSocket library's dedicated transmit lock eliminated
the earlier session drops during a 469-second hardware verification run on `0.8.37`:

| Metric | Hardware result (`0.8.37`) |
| --- | --- |
| Continuous session window | **469 s** (235 status samples, 1 connection epoch, **0 reconnects**) |
| Concurrent preview + SD recording | VGA at 1 fps; **411 frames** across 8 finalized AVI segments |
| Frame drops | **0 recording drops**, **0 camera-queue drops** |
| Concurrent clip download | Downloaded a **2.88 MB** 60 s MJPEG AVI while streaming and recording continued |
| Peak TCP ACK delay survived | **1,270 ms** recovered cleanly on the same socket |

## Body Event Trigger: wake, tipped over, and rapid movement

Ainekio's sensor path now initiates cognitive turns when something physical happens to
the robot. P4 IMU and lifecycle telemetry flow through Body Control (`Perception →
Body events`) into a dedicated **Body Event Trigger** node workflow in MetaHuman OS:

<figure>
  <img src="/posts/2026-10-14-progress/body-events.svg" alt="P4 measurements pass through Body Control and the Environment Bridge to Body Event Trigger. Its model can take no action or delegate to Environment Mode, which sends selected actions back to the body." width="1000" height="640" loading="lazy" />
  <figcaption>P4 IMU and wake transitions trigger episode-debounced events in Body Control, which Body Event Trigger evaluates in MetaHuman OS.</figcaption>
</figure>

| Event | Trigger threshold | Clearing / debounce behavior |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Wake** | Boot, sleep-wake, or manual *Good morning* button | Evaluates wake intention (greet, capture photo, stretch, stand) |
| **Tipped over** | Tilt `> 60°` for `300 ms` | Single episode until tilt drops below `45°` |
| **Rapid movement** | Accel `|a − g| > 0.75 g` or gyro `> 180°/s` | Single episode until below `0.35 g` and `90°/s`, gated by a **10 s** cooldown |

## Teaching the Task model to separate quoted speech from movement

Live body-event testing exposed an interesting failure mode: when `Body Event Trigger`
asked for a quip about sudden movement followed by a brief shrug, the 0.8B Task
specialist treated the words inside the speech string as a movement command and
started continuous sideways **Crab** instead.

We trained two new 0.8B Task LoRA candidates (4,350 and 4,926 development examples)
pairing quoted movement idioms (e.g., *"Take a bow, my friend"*) with contrasting
physical actions:

<figure>
  <img src="/posts/2026-10-14-progress/task-qualification.svg" alt="On 1,221 evaluation cases, acceptable reference matches rose from 533 to 1,011. Historical regression tests still showed three missed actions versus zero, so the new candidate was not deployed." width="1000" height="640" loading="lazy" />
  <figcaption>Evaluation accuracy jumped from 43.7% to 82.8%, but 3 missed actions on the historical regression suite blocked deployment—leading directly to the October 15 architectural fix.</figcaption>
</figure>

| Frozen benchmark metric | Deployed Task adapter | Second contrast candidate |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Evaluation reference matches | 533 / 1,221 (43.7%) | **1,011 / 1,221 (82.8%)** |
| Evaluation wrong physical actions | 313 | **94** |
| Evaluation unintended physical actions | 125 | **50** |
| Historical regression matches | 82 / 141 | **110 / 141** |
| Historical missed physical actions | **0** | 3 |

Rather than forcing a 0.8B action planner to carry verbatim spoken lines inside its
action schema, this result motivated the architectural cleanup in the
[October 15 companion systems update](/posts/2026-10-15-companion-systems/): removing
speech steps from the Task model entirely so **Persona** is the sole owner of outward
speech. ([Download the October 14 Task comparison JSON](/posts/2026-10-14-progress/task-model-comparison.json).)

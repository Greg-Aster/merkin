# Standing starts before walking

The latest work separates calibration Home from ordinary startup, adds adjustable
self-leveling to Body Control, and investigates why a smaller linkage is not
automatically easier for the motors to drive.

The October 13 hardware journal records an owner-reported **566 g assembled
mass**, **5 V supply** and **MG90 servos**. These replace earlier unknown mass
and voltage entries for that reported configuration. They do not establish the
finished shell's mass, measured current draw or battery endurance.

## Crouch, align the shoulders, then rise

Ordinary boot previously drove toward saved calibration Home. On the floor,
that could extend the legs before the shoulders were aligned. Startup now
requests a staged rise from the body controller:

1. Fold the carrier and crank joints toward a modeled crouch.
2. Align the shoulders while retaining the crouch.
3. Raise the body into Stand over the modeled foot contacts.

The later revision staggers the front pair and rear pair by **100 ms**, with
a total commanded sequence of **2.5 seconds**. Calibration Home remains a
separate explicit operation. Staggering command starts has not yet demonstrated
a measured reduction in peak current.

The firmware journal records Wi-Fi installation and acceptance of the startup
revisions, preservation of joint calibration and native tests of phase order,
joint mapping, contact geometry and cancellation. These are commanded
trajectories: the standard servo interface does not report actual arrival or
foot contact. A completed startup command alone cannot prove that the chassis
physically rose.

## Self-leveling has its own controls

The P4 now exposes self-leveling settings through the selected robot's Body
Control connection. They are saved on the robot independently of joint and IMU
calibration.

| Control | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| On / Off | Enables correction in supported standing and moving states; separate from whether correction is currently active |
| Response strength | Multiplier for the attitude-error response; original default 1× |
| Damping | Multiplier for the angular-rate response; original default 1× |
| Roll target | Desired roll offset; positive raises the left side, original default 0° |
| Pitch target | Desired pitch offset; positive raises the nose, original default 0° |

Turning correction off does not stop a gait or disable outputs. Returning to
On can resume correction in an eligible state without starting another motion.
The controls read their values from the robot; selecting default response values
only edits the form until they are saved.

An earlier tilt test found fresh gyro data but inactive balance after a shrug.
Selecting Stand activated correction. Further checks found changing reported
joint commands while the owner still reported little physical response. The
investigation then identified a separate controller issue: the old standing
response settled at a fraction of a persistent tilt error rather than continuing
to correct it.

Stationary hold now accumulates accepted correction over time. Moving gait and
rise retain their separate response. An ideal-actuator test exercises rejection
of a sustained disturbance; real linkage fit still determines which corrections
can be applied. Increasing a setting does not increase the servo's available
torque.

## Installed controls, remaining physical work

The October 14 journal records **0.8.46-p4-leveling-tuning** uploaded, explicitly
activated and accepted over Wi-Fi. Live browser checks read and saved the four
response values, preserving the previously selected Off state. The recorded
session ended in Doze with outputs disabled.

This establishes firmware installation and working configuration controls.
**Successful loaded self-leveling remains unverified.** The IMU measures body
attitude; it cannot identify an individual stalled joint or tell a level body
standing above the floor from one sagging onto it. An automatic fallen-body
get-up routine is also not established by this work.

## A calculator for the actual linkage

The new local leg-geometry calculator keeps the servo shaft locations fixed by
default while changing link lengths. It can animate a requested path, compare
design snapshots, plot joint travel and modeled load demand, and export JSON,
CSV and SVG. It has no connection to robot controls.

The October 14 comparison uses the last-confirmed assembled geometry of
**55 / 35 / 38 / 28 / 20 mm** for leg, carrier, rod, pickup and crank, versus
an active Blender study of **45 / 35 / 30 / 28 / 20 mm**. The shorter study
reduces reach, but its shorter rod approaches an unfavorable alignment as the
body rises. Under the journal's particular 65 mm shaft-height benchmark,
modeled carrier peak torque falls about 3%, while crank demand rises about 16%.
That is not a general torque improvement.

The calculator uses ideal point feet and external-force geometry. It omits
housing collisions, servo torque-speed limits and whole-body balance. Its
plots are design evidence, not a recommendation to change the assembled robot's
calibration or a physical gait qualification.

The Blender work also has a separate motion working file retaining the main
23-chapter motion library and both body configurations. Earlier design scenes
remain in the original reference file. The article thumbnail is an **October 12
Blender view**, not a new photograph or a render of the later geometry study.

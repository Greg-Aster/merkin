Between November 7 and November 11, work on the workbench and in simulation crossed two
milestones I had been aiming toward since V2 first stood up:

1. **Neural walking now runs natively on the `ESP32-P4` itself—no Wi-Fi host stream required.** Using two rounds of DAgger teacher-student distillation, I shrunk our GPU-trained **MicroDuck** proprioceptive gait network by **~80%**—from a `547 KB` teacher down to a **`107.8 KB` (`110,432-byte`) `int8` policy** that fits completely inside the `ESP32-P4`'s **`128 KB` L2 cache** and runs closed-loop at **`50 Hz` on Core 1**.
2. **MetaHuman OS officially became [Ainekio OS (`Greg-Aster/ainekio-os`)](https://github.com/Greg-Aster/ainekio-os).** Alongside opening up and renaming the companion mind repository, I unified local and LAN GPU model/voice routing with warm cloud coding delegates, built a single live-camera **Cockpit HUD** for driving the robot on the floor, and redesigned the rear **Wireless Keeper Bridge** around our `2S` power electronics.

## 1. Moving neural walking onto the ESP32-P4's 128 KB L2 cache

During the [November 6 carpet walks](/posts/2026-11-06-carpet-trials-rl-and-big-brother/),
neural control worked, but with a catch: the workstation gateway was still running the
policy and streaming `50 Hz` foot targets over Wi-Fi UDP. On a busy room network, even a
brief Wi-Fi hiccup stretched the `20 ms` control tick and broke the rhythm of a stride.
Untethered floor walking meant putting the neural network on the microcontroller right
next to the sensors and servos.

The proprioceptive **MicroDuck** policy takes a **720-float rolling history** (`15`
frames × `48` values covering `300 ms` at `50 Hz`):

- **Joint positions & velocities (`24` values):** All 12 modified `MG90S` 4th-wire
  potentiometer angles (sampled through the `CD74HC4067` multiplexer) and One-Euro
  filtered joint velocities.
- **Inertial state (`9` values):** 3-axis angular rate and 3-axis linear acceleration
  from the `208 Hz` `LSM6DS3` IMU, plus the yaw-zeroed gravity vector from Madgwick
  attitude fusion.
- **Commands & prior action (`15` values):** Desired body velocity (`vx`, `vy`, `wz`)
  and the previous step's `12` normalized foot-offset outputs.

My first on-chip compile quantized the `512-256-128` teacher network directly to
symmetric per-row `int8` weights. It ran on the board, but at **`547,040 bytes`
(`534 KB`)** it was more than **4× larger than the `ESP32-P4`'s `128 KB` L2 cache**,
forcing external PSRAM fetches across the memory bus every `20 ms`.

Running **two rounds of DAgger distillation** from that teacher into a compact
`720 → 128 → 64 → 32 → 12` student shrunk the compiled `int8` weights by **`79.8%`** down
to **`110,432 bytes` (`107.8 KB`)**—small enough to stay hot inside the `128 KB` L2
cache on **Core 1** using 8-way unrolled `int8 × int16 → int32` dot products. Every
`20 ms`, its Cartesian foot offsets pass through analytical `35 / 20 / 28 / 38 / 55 mm`
four-bar inverse kinematics and workspace boundary projection before driving the
`PCA9685` PWM channels, while legacy phase-residual correction tables (`-831` lines of C)
were deleted completely.

Even better, continuing PPO reinforcement learning on the distilled `107.8 KB` student
under simulated rail sag and multi-surface friction ended up **beating the larger
`547 KB` teacher** across every direction:

## 2. Hardwood & carpet-seam trials, hardware-twin RL, and Freestyle recovery

Cheap `$1.50` `MG90S` micro servos don't behave like idealized torque actuators in a
clean physics engine. To make policies trained across **4,096 parallel MuJoCo
environments** hold up on a real living-room floor, I built GPU simulation twins of the
physical sensor and power quirks we kept hitting on the bench:

- **Realistic electrical & linkage quirks:** 12-bit ratiometric 4th-wire ADC steps,
  **5 V supply-rail voltage sag** when multiple servos load up together, occasional
  `60 ms` stale ADC samples, `LSM6DS3` gyro noise and bias drift, `PCA9685` `4.07 µs`
  PWM resolution, rear `2S` battery mass distribution, **mixed floor friction (`0.14`
  slick hardwood through `1.45` carpet)**, and **`21–40 mm` floor/cable ledges**.

Alongside the on-chip distillation work, I trained a `600M`–`800M`-step **Pupper V3 MJX**
policy and ran **13 physical floor bouts** on the real V2 robot across two surfaces that
usually trip small quadrupeds: **6 bouts on slick hardwood** (forward, reverse, and four
turning arcs with `5.4°–15.4°` peak roll and **zero falls**) and **7 bouts crossing and
straddling a raised carpet-to-hardwood seam** (**zero falls**).

For finite expressive gestures, I also expanded the **Freestyle** recovery dataset to
**`489` trajectories** covering jam and slip recovery across all `23` base motions,
returning cleanly to native closed-loop `Stand` whenever a foot catches or stalls.

## 3. Redesigned Wireless Keeper Bridge and 30 updated Red V2 shell parts

Floor bouts quickly exposed every loose wire and tight tolerance left in the `650 g`
body. Between November 6 and November 11 I reworked the rear power/electronics bridge
and front face mounts across **30 updated body parts**:

- **Battery-mounted Wireless Keeper Bridge:** Moves the `2S` Li-ion pack (`73.6 × 46 ×
  22 mm`), `Mini560` `5 V / 5 A` buck regulator, and `HiLetgo` 2S USB-C charger board
  onto a cleaner rear carrier with snap-in PCB clips for the `ESP32-P4`, `PCA9685`,
  charger, and a dedicated `LSM6DS3` gyro mount so nothing shifts during hard turns.
- **Thicker LCD spacers & recessed bezel:** Adds four wireless spacers, a deeper
  recessed bezel, and an updated camera-to-LCD retainer so the 1.9-inch `ST7789` screen
  sits flush behind the mask without pinching its ribbon cable.
- **Front carrier & shimmed servo brackets:** Cleans up the compact front bulkhead,
  crossbars, tray plates, and all four upper servo brackets with separate rectangular
  shims for tighter bearing preload.

## 4. Body Control Cockpit HUD for live floor driving

Jumping between separate telemetry tabs while driving the robot across hardwood and
carpet got clumsy fast, so I built a single full-viewport **Cockpit HUD** around the
live lower-jaw `OV5647` camera feed.

Driving with WASD or a gamepad now overlays live joint tracking and load, `LSM6DS3`
roll/pitch attitude, posture and gesture triggers, spoken-line controls, a switch
between **on-chip `ESP32-P4` walking** and **host-streamed policies**, and a top-bar
autonomy cut-off toggle. To gauge clearance from a single monocular camera close to the
floor, pyramidal Lucas-Kanade optical flow tracks up to 36 ground-contact features at
the base of nearby obstacles and fuses them with forward displacement into a live
range readout.

## 5. MetaHuman OS becomes Ainekio OS

Finally, the companion mind repository has a home and name that actually match the
robot: **[Greg-Aster/ainekio-os](https://github.com/Greg-Aster/ainekio-os)**.

![Ainekio OS Hybrid Compute, Cognitive Graph, and Body Bridge Architecture](/posts/ainekio-os/ainekio-os-architecture.webp)

Consolidating the repository let me clear out a pile of old scaffolding and tighten how
the brain talks to both local GPUs and the physical body:

- **Unified local, LAN GPU, and cloud routing:** All **12 specialist model roles** and
  voice services now route from one status drawer—switching cleanly between local or LAN
  GPU **`Ollama`** / **`llama.cpp`** endpoints, CUDA **`Whisper`** speech recognition,
  and **`Kokoro`** text-to-speech (with instant local **`Piper`** ONNX fallback), plus
  warm persistent sessions for cloud coding and multimodal delegates.
- **Lean HTTP core & interactive architecture map:** Collapsed 150+ leftover route
  wrappers into a single Core API router (`123` handlers), removed unused RVC
  voice-cloning clutter, and added a live node-graph architecture viewer.
- **Fresh physical reflex lines:** Previously, tipping or shaking the robot twice in a
  row could get the exact same quip back. Physical reflex turns (tips, shakes, and leg
  stalls) now check the robot's **last six spoken reflex lines** before replying,
  keeping its reactions varied and dryly observant instead of sounding canned.

Most of the last two days on Ainekio V2 happened in Blender: bringing all four leg
assemblies around a single frame, widening the central bay around 3D board models, and
fitting the leg housings, curved connecting rods, front port panel, top and bottom
covers, and a removable camera-and-LCD face carrier.

![Earlier widened-chassis CAD assembly with all four legs and exposed electronics, before the later housing, front-panel, and cover revisions.](/posts/2026-09-10-v2-design/chassis-overview.png)

*Early widened-chassis pass with the central electronics bay open for fit checks.*

## Making room for the electronics

Pulling the four legs onto a shared frame made it obvious how tight the original narrow
body would be once real connectors were plugged in. Importing millimeter-accurate models
of the three candidate boards—a **Radxa Dragon Q6A** single-board computer, an
**ESP32-P4-WIFI6** controller, and an **Adafruit PCA9685** 16-channel servo
driver—gave concrete keep-out envelopes including overhanging headers and ports.

To clear them, I widened the chassis by **40 mm (20 mm per side)**, keeping the hip
roots, center mounts, and end-panel middle sections tied together so the bearing
pockets and screw holes stayed aligned.

![Top view of the widened CAD chassis, with the Q6A at the front and the ESP32-P4 and PCA9685 boards beside each other toward the rear.](/posts/2026-09-10-v2-design/electronics-layout.png)

*Board placement inside the widened central bay.*

The Q6A sits up front with its ports facing forward, while the ESP32-P4 and PCA9685 sit
side by side toward the rear. That leaves room for a Q6A support tray and basic board
gaps, though real cable plugs, bend radii, airflow, and final standoffs still need a
dedicated pass.

## Refining the leg parts as a set

The upper servo brackets went through profile-rounding and cutout passes before I
mirrored the updated bracket across all four corners.

The curved connecting rod also needed extra clearance around the upper bracket. Moving
its mid-span **3 mm outward** and replacing the sharp kink with a continuous arc gave a
cleaner sweep between the two round bearing ends while keeping the 4 mm link thickness
and twin oval weight-relief slots.

![Comparison of the earlier sharply bent connecting rod and the revised continuous curve, with the two mounting ends and oval openings retained.](/posts/2026-09-10-v2-design/linkage-curve-comparison.png)

*Continuous-curve connecting rod revision synchronized across all four legs.*

## Fitting the front panel around the Q6A

The front bulkhead closes around the Q6A’s two stacked USB blocks and Ethernet jack
with three cutouts serving four USB ports and one RJ45 socket. An interior relief clears
the PCB edge and underside components, leaving just the front lip of the Q6A tray to be
trimmed where it meets the panel.

![Oblique CAD view of the front panel around the Q6A’s two USB stacks and Ethernet connector.](/posts/2026-09-10-v2-design/q6a-port-panel.png)

*Front bulkhead fitted around the Q6A USB and Ethernet ports.*

## Bringing the covers into the assembly

Next came fitted top and bottom cover plates, widened from **72 mm to 112 mm** at the
original 3 mm thickness and aligned to the eight chassis screw bosses.

![Widened cover plates before their unused openings were closed.](/posts/2026-09-10-v2-design/fitted-covers.png)

*Widened top and bottom covers before closing leftover cutouts.*

A cleanup pass then filled **18 leftover openings in the top plate and 15 in the
bottom**, leaving clean 3 mm plates with eight frame screw holes, outer leg-clearance
notches, and lower locating tabs.

## Adding a removable camera and display face

At the front, a single removable face carrier mounts an **OV5647 camera** (Arducam
B0033 hole pattern) above a landscape **Waveshare 2-inch LCD module**. The carrier
fastens to two front screws on the top cover (adding 3 mm to their stack), holding the
LCD on four M2×6 spacers and the camera on four printed 4 mm standoffs.

![Rear view of the camera/display carrier showing the standoffs and cable openings.](/posts/2026-09-10-v2-design/face-rear-detail.png)

*Rear of the removable camera and LCD carrier showing standoffs and ribbon slots.*

The [V2 hardware and linkage guide](/posts/ainekio-v2/#compact-leg-linkage-geometry)
tracks where the production V2 geometry settled after these early wide-body studies,
and the [short-rod leg mechanism study](/posts/2026-09-09-leg-mechanism-study/) covers

## Companion software: keeping active turns coherent

Alongside the CAD work, I tightened how MetaHuman OS handles follow-up speech while a
robot action is already running. Follow-up input now attaches cleanly to the in-flight
request instead of spawning a competing turn, action selection separates the immediate
next step from overall goal tracking, and image references stay attached when routing
between vision and task nodes. See the
[MetaHuman integration guide](/posts/metahuman-integration/) for how the companion
workflow connects to the body.

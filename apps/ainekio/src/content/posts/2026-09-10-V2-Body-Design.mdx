Most of the last two days’ Ainekio work has been in Blender. The V2 design now
has four leg assemblies arranged around a body, a wider electronics bay, and
models of the boards that need to fit inside it. Work has also continued on
the leg housings, linkage curves, front connector panel, fitted body covers, and a removable camera/display face.

![Earlier widened-chassis CAD assembly with all four legs and exposed electronics, before the later housing, front-panel, and cover revisions.](/posts/2026-09-10-v2-design/chassis-overview.png)

*The earlier widened-chassis stage, with the electronics bay open for inspection.*

## Making room for the electronics

The first assembly revisions brought the four legs together and fitted the
body’s center mounts and end panels. Adding the electronics models gave the layout a
concrete set of dimensions to work around.

Three board models are included: a **Radxa Dragon Q6A** computer, an
**ESP32-P4-WIFI6** controller board, and an **Adafruit PCA9685** servo-driver
board, imported at millimeter scale with their overhanging connectors.

To fit them, the chassis was widened by **40 mm—20 mm on each side**. An editable
spacing control moves the leg roots, center mounts, and inserted middle sections of
the front and rear panels together while preserving bearing and screw features.

![Top view of the widened CAD chassis, with the Q6A at the front and the ESP32-P4 and PCA9685 boards beside each other toward the rear.](/posts/2026-09-10-v2-design/electronics-layout.png)

*Electronics placement in the widened-chassis revision.*

The Q6A sits at the front with its ports facing the front panel. The ESP32-P4 and
PCA9685 sit beside each other toward the rear. The layout includes a Q6A tray,
connector reliefs, and nominal gaps between the board envelopes, leaving cable plugs,
bend radii, cooling space, and final board fixings for the next pass.

## Refining the leg parts as a set

The upper leg housings went through rounded-profile and opening-layout
revisions, and the chosen Part 3 housing and its editable cutters were synchronized
across all four legs.

The Part 26 linkage also received more clearance and a smoother outline. Its
center first moved **3 mm farther outward**, then the concentrated bend became
a broader curve that blends into the round mounting ends. The two oval slots
follow the new outline while retaining the 4 mm plate thickness and mounting
geometry.

![Comparison of the earlier Part 26 bend and the revised continuous curve, with the two mounting ends and oval openings retained.](/posts/2026-09-10-v2-design/linkage-curve-comparison.png)

*The continuous-curve revision for Part 26, applied across all four legs.*

## Fitting the front panel around the Q6A

The front panel closes around the Q6A’s two USB stacks and Ethernet jack:
three openings serving four USB sockets and one network port. The editable
cutters follow the board, and the panel’s width adjustment was checked at two
settings.

![Oblique CAD view of the front panel around the Q6A’s two USB stacks and Ethernet connector.](/posts/2026-09-10-v2-design/q6a-port-panel.png)

*Front-panel revision from the WIP 11 assembly.*

An internal relief clears the board edge and nearby underside components.
The port openings currently match the modeled connector envelopes closely, and the
front lip of the Q6A tray is still queued for trimming where it meets the panel.

## Bringing the covers into the assembly

WIP 12 added fitted top and bottom covers, each widened from
**72 to 112 mm** while preserving the original 3 mm plate thickness. Their eight
frame mounting holes align with the chassis, and both covers follow
the same adjustable width control as the rest of the body.

![WIP 12 cover plates before their unused openings were closed.](/posts/2026-09-10-v2-design/fitted-covers.png)

*WIP 12 cover revision before closing unused access cutouts.*

WIP 13 then closed **18 unused openings in the top cover and 15 in the bottom**,
including the large access cutouts. Each plate now retains eight 3 mm
frame screw holes, the 112 mm width, 3 mm base thickness, outer clearance
contours, and bottom tabs.

## Adding a removable camera and display face

WIP 14 places an **OV5647 camera reference** (Arducam B0033 mounting pattern) above a
landscape **Waveshare 2-inch LCD Module** on a single removable carrier.

The carrier uses two front screw positions on the top cover, adding
3 mm to their stack. The LCD model uses four M2×6 spacers, and the camera rests
on four integrated 4 mm standoffs.

![Rear view of the camera/display carrier showing the standoffs and cable openings.](/posts/2026-09-10-v2-design/face-rear-detail.png)

*Rear view of the camera/display carrier showing the standoffs and cable openings.*

## MetaHuman OS: refining continuity and controls

On the software side, MetaHuman OS updates focused on execution continuity and
specialist routing:

- Follow-up input received while robot or specialist work is pending stays attached to
  the active request, and input handoffs verify version compatibility.
- Action selection separates the immediate next action from broader goal progress,
  routes invalid model answers through the correction path, and preserves image identity
  and context across selections.
- Training and profile-memory reset controls gained cleaner lifecycle handling, backed
  by **236 passing durable-execution tests** alongside graph, type, transport, and
  architecture checks. See the [MetaHuman integration guide](/posts/metahuman-integration/)
  for the full workflow architecture.

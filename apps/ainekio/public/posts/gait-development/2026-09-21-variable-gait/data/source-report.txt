# Ainekio stride and motion-rate demonstration

Open `Ainekio/Slave/hardware/v2-12servo/ainekio-variable-gait.blend`.
Select **Gait - Stride and Rate / Gait playback** and press Space.
The current animation is a continuous nine-cycle demonstration, about 14 seconds,
frames 1–420. Timeline markers identify each control change.

## Controls in Blender

Open the 3D View sidebar with N, then select **Ainekio > Gait**.

- **Stride %** controls planted fore–aft travel: 100% is 92 mm; 25% is 23 mm.
- **Motion rate** changes the shared cycle clock independently: 1x is 1.8 seconds
  per cycle, 2x is 0.9 seconds. It does not scale joint angles or stride geometry.
- **Build steady gait** creates four continuous cycles at the selected settings.
- **Build ramp demo** begins at quarter of the selected stride and half the
  selected rate, extends to full selected stride, raises cadence, then returns.

Rebuilding runs the numerical solver in a separate process, then replaces the
existing gait curves in place. Ctrl-S saves the result. Geometry, the source
modeling scene, fitting setup, and face cache are preserved. If Blender opens
with scripts disabled, run the packed **gait_controls.py** text once to restore
the sidebar and changing playback labels. This does not require changing Blender's
script-security preferences.

## Formula and variable rearward reach

For stride fraction `p = Stride % / 100` and rate multiplier `r`:

```text
planted sweep S = 92 p mm
body advance L = S / 0.7 mm per cycle
cycle frequency f = r / 1.8 Hz
forward speed v = L f = 73.015873 p r mm/s
nominal rearward bias = 30 p mm
foot lift = 4 + 10 p mm
body roll = +/-3 p degrees; pitch = +/-2 p degrees
```

At full stride, the nominal planted endpoints remain **+16 mm forward and
-76 mm backward** from the original standing reference. Shorter strides reduce
both endpoints proportionally. Increasing rate at a fixed stride preserves this
path exactly. This replaces the previous single percentage control, whose stride
had a nonzero intercept and whose cycle rate changed together with stride.

During stride changes, the planner predicts body travel over each upcoming stance.
Its rearward bias is `30/92` of that travel. Each touchdown anchor is positioned
between the predicted body positions at touchdown and liftoff, using that bias.
An already planted foot stays at its existing world XY reference until liftoff;
it does not slide to follow a changed percentage. Swing joins the old anchor to
the next planned anchor with zero world XY velocity and acceleration at both ends.
Foot lift is selected for that swing. The faceted sole can still rock and its
material contact point can migrate.

Stride and rate change through quintic curves in a continuous gait phase. Body
translation integrates stride over phase; elapsed time integrates inverse cycle
frequency. All twelve joints, body sway and contact states use that same clock.
This is an offline planned ramp with lookahead, not an online disturbance or
terrain controller. Rearward bias is a geometric gait setting, not an optimized
physical balance solution.

## Geometry and validation

All four current legs retain the 40 mm primary, 24 mm input, 38 mm rod,
24 mm pickup and 55 mm distal geometry. Part009 and Part023 remain coordinated
through the existing closed linkage. The previous corrected full-stride joint
curve is preserved; only its clock changes during cadence acceleration.

- `control-checks.json` verifies independent stride/rate controls, exact 2x
  retiming, fixed stance references, smooth control boundaries and loop closure.
- `blender-validation.json` evaluates all source keys and their midpoints after
  reopening the saved file, including actual driver angles, closure and sole height.
- `collision-screen.json` samples the entire changing-speed demo at 60 Hz. It
  covers printed link pairs and crank/rod versus boots, excluding intended pin
  interfaces. It does not cover containment, the whole chassis, all hardware,
  neighbouring legs or continuous swept volumes.

The saved project passed **7,777** source and midpoint pose checks: maximum
linkage closure error 0.00219 mm, joint-angle error 0.000655 degrees, and ground
penetration 0.000305 mm. The geometric collision screen evaluated **841** poses
with no flagged pairs in its stated scope, using the reopened recovery with the
new gait applied. The live build preserved all 414 non-ground mesh identities.
The Blender **Build ramp demo** button was also exercised through completion.

The source uses 432 samples per cycle, yielding approximately 240–480 Hz in this
1x–2x demonstration. Timestamps are nonuniform and authoritative. The 2x segment
requests peak joint speed around 1,283 degrees/s. This is animation demand, not
measured loaded servo capability. Balance, traction and physical execution are
not established by these checks.

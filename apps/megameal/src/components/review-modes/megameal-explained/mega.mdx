    <div>
      <p className="review-essay__section-label">Final Thoughts</p>
      <h3>A solo production with blockbuster appetite</h3>
      <p>The movie moves at the speed of a franchise recap having a panic attack, which is both its strength and its curse. As a viewing experience, that pace keeps the world exciting but also denies some scenes the breathing room they deserve. It introduces a universe and storyline with room for further development, then runs away before anyone can ask who approved the budget.</p>
      <p>The production quality is <span className="text-yellow-600 dark:text-yellow-500 font-bold">"fair,"</span> with glitches and imperfections, but the roughness is not automatically a failure. Some of the instability gives the movie its transmission-from-a-broken-timeline flavor. A more seamless product is possible in theory; the labor involved makes that a fantasy with invoices attached.</p>
      <p>Ultimately, the movie suffers from the same issues as any indie production: <span className="text-red-500 dark:text-red-400 font-bold">budget, labor, and audience</span>. Larger studios have teams ready to absorb the weight. A single creator working at this scale is bound to hit a labor ceiling, especially when the material demands cities, mascots, portals, armies, corporate brands, screaming food, and emotional sincerity inside the same bag.</p>
      <p>In that sense, what has been achieved so far is impressive, but the film also makes its own fragility visible. Will the series continue? Hopefully. But labor is the food this project eats, and without support, even a world this loud can end up alone in some dark alley, trying to crawl before the lights go out.</p>

## The Universal Truth About First Contact

It is bad. Avoid at all costs. This is a confusing contradiction coming from a first contact manual, I know. But the data is clear, and the universe is not a safe place for the curious. The best way to survive an encounter with the unknown is to never have one in the first place. We expect you to disregard this advice, but we are contractually obligated to include it anyway, we have also included the rest of the book in case you do ignore it, and we wish you the best of luck if you do.

### The Scope of the Problem

The universe operates as a vast ecosystem where introducing new species--particularly one as presumptuous as whatever species you are--often mirrors the introduction of invasive species on terrestrial planets, but amplified to cosmic scale. But instead of that pesky weed or vine overrunning your backyard, you're looking at self-replicating nanites converting star systems into grey goo, or alien microorganisms deciding your entire species would make an attractive jello sculpture.

## The Evolutionary Disparity Problem

Beyond the immediate threats lies a more fundamental issue: the sheer evolutionary gulf between your civilization and any species capable of interstellar travel or advanced technology.

Life across the galaxy has had billions of years to devise creative, insidious, and terrifyingly effective solutions to extreme environments and competitive pressures. Human civilization, in our cosmic blink of existence, has developed impressive technology but lacks the adaptive resilience that comes from eons of evolutionary pressure.

## Conclusion

The universe isn't a welcoming committee—it's an unmoderated existential horror convention.

This manual exists because optimism kills. The romantic notion of wise, benevolent aliens or grand cosmic federations ready to welcome humanity has been thoroughly debunked by hard data and harder experience. The cosmos operates on principles that prioritize survival, efficiency, and often creativity in the application of violence.

Your best chance of surviving an interstellar journey isn't superior technology, diplomatic skills, or good intentions. It's recognizing that some doors shouldn't be opened, some signals shouldn't be answered, and some questions shouldn't be asked.

When in doubt, remember the cardinal rule: <span className="fcmi-emphasis fcmi-tone-red">DON’T</span>.
